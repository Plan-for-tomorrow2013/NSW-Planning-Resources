## 4. DEVELOPMENT CONTROLS

### 4.1 General

1\) WSUD measures must only be commissioned once 80% of the development’s dwellings are completed, or upon receiving the Subdivision Certificate. This ensures that the WSUD measures are not commissioned until most of the development’s infrastructure is completed, and the landform is stabilised. Until the 80% threshold is reached, temporary WSUD measures must be implemented.

2\) All WSUD components, including subsoil drainage, must be located entirely within the property boundary to which the development application applies and must not contribute to any increased flooding risk.

3\) Adequate spacing must be provided for the installation, accessibility, and long-term maintenance of WSUD systems. WSUD systems must be purposefully integrated into the development layout to maximise land efficiency and deliver co-benefits, rather than being confined to residual or leftover spaces. Designs that limit the system performance, co-benefits, maintenance access, or safety will not be accepted by Council, and applicants will be required to revise the design accordingly. For detailed guidance on designing WSUD measures that comply with access and maintenance requirements, refer to Council’s WSUD Guidebook for Developers.

4\) Where WSUD measures will be handed over to Council responsibility after an establishment and maintenance period, proprietary cartridge, and filter WSUD measures will not be used, or accepted as a treatment option for pollutant removal. However, Council may accept some proprietary gross pollutant trap systems such as those listed below:

i) non-blocking high performance continuous deflection separation units, and

ii) non-blocking vortex separator systems.

Notes:

Council may consider supplementary proprietary devices where:

i) Independent peer-reviewed research demonstrates their effectiveness, and performance claims are supported by complete, unrestricted information that allows Council to assess and, if appropriate, accept those claims. For more information and guidance on Council’s requirements regarding proprietary WDUD systems, please refer to Council’s WSUD Guidebook for Developers. A requirement for a positive covenant may be imposed by a condition of consent, requiring the maintenance and / or repair of a new, or existing WSUD measure, and an associated easement for this purpose may also be required to be registered on title, in accordance with sections 88, 88B and 88BA of the Conveyancing Act 1919. The positive covenant will be binding on all

subsequent property owners and registered with the appropriate land authority. The covenant will ensure the ongoing maintenance of the WSUD system(s) in perpetuity, or as specified in the development conditions. Where required, the applicant must ensure that the covenant complies with the requirements set out in Council’s WSUD Guidebook for Developers, as well as any relevant local, state, or national laws governing land use, environmental protection, and water management. In cases where WSUD measures become redundant, due to changes in surrounding development, upstream improvements, or the installation of centralised WSUD systems, Council may decommission those systems as needed to maintain overall water quality.
