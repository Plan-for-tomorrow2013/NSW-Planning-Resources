### 4.5 Groundwater

1\) All Development Applications involving excavation for below-ground WSUD structures must assess the potential for groundwater interaction. Where an assessment of the site indicates possible interaction with groundwater or groundwater-dependent ecosystems, a Groundwater Management Plan must be included in the applicant’s WSUD Strategy. The Groundwater Management Plan must address and demonstrate:

i) Whether groundwater will be encountered during excavation or operation.

ii) No potential impacts on groundwater, or groundwater-dependent ecosystems.

iii) The measures taken to prevent adverse effects, including waterproof and construction methods (e.g. tanked structures).

iv) That the design of the groundwater management system will not alter groundwater behaviour in the surrounding area.

v) That the groundwater regime will be maintained as close as possible to pre-development conditions during both construction and operational phases.

2\) The extraction of groundwater is considered an ‘aquifer interference activity’ and requires assessment, approval and permits from Water NSW. Aquifer interference activities must be licensed to account for all water taken, have an approval, and must be designed and managed to ensure impacts are acceptable. Before starting excavation, one or more of the following approvals will be needed and be include in the applicant’s WSUD Strategy:

i) A water access licence.

ii) A water supply works approval.

iii) An aquifer interference activity approval.

3\) Any proposed groundwater management systems must be designed for a minimum lifespan of 100 years. For construction on hillsides involving permanent structures (other than piles or footings below the water table), the method of construction must be clearly outlined in a WSUD Strategy and approved by Council.

4\) All groundwater management activities, including monitoring, must align with state guidelines and be outlined in WSUD Strategy and approved by Council.

5\) Groundwater management systems must be designed so that they are safely and easily accessible and maintainable.

6\) All groundwater management activities, including monitoring, must comply with the approved groundwater assessment and management plan, as agreed upon with Council as part of an applicant’s WSUD Strategy.

7\) All construction techniques must be employed that minimise or eliminate the need for dewatering. If dewatering is proposed, consultation with the NSW Government is required before to submitting the development application.

8\) If below-ground structures are within three metres of each other, the design must accommodate natural groundwater flow through perimeter, or drainage systems to prevent flow restrictions. If construction methods, or the size of a below-ground structure impede natural flow paths, artificial drainage must be implemented.

Note:  Council prohibits the discharge of intercepted groundwater into waterways, Lake Illawarra, Council’s stormwater infrastructure, or the ocean if it could have an adverse environmental impact, including the discharge of highly saline groundwater. In such cases, the groundwater must be directed to the sewer through a trade waste agreement (subject to approval from Sydney

Water and Council).
