## 1. INTRODUCTION

Water is a precious and limited resource that must be managed carefully to meet the needs of current and future generations. This Chapter of the Wollongong Development Control Plan (DCP) outlines the requirements for the incorporation of Water Sensitive Urban Design (WSUD) for development within the Wollongong Local Government Area (LGA).

Wollongong’s unique geography, situated between the escarpment and the coast, creates varied climate and water conditions that influence stormwater behaviour. In the steep escarpment areas, rainfall generates rapid runoff and limited infiltration. While on the flatter coastal floodplains, water moves slowly and remains on the surface for longer periods. The region’s sandy and fine soils are highly erodible, presenting challenges for stormwater and erosion management and requiring specific design responses.

Development alters natural water flow patterns and introduces pollutants from impervious surfaces such as roads, driveways, and footpaths. With an expected population increase of nearly 70,000 by 2045, urban development will place growing pressure on local water systems. WSUD provides a strategic approach to manage the impacts of urbanisation. It integrates urban planning, stormwater management and environmental protection to:

Reduce peak stormwater flows. Improve water quality. Reuse rainwater. Support water-efficient landscaping. Protect natural waterways and habitats.

Wollongong’s waterways, and Lake Illawarra face increasing pressure from urban development. By incorporating WSUD during the design and development phases, significant environmental and community benefits can be achieved, including:

Improved water quality, helping to protect downstream waterways, the ocean, and Lake Illawarra. Protection of water resources, by managing stormwater sustainably and treating pollution at the source. Reduced erosion and sedimentation downstream of development. Minimising the environmental impact of new developments. Lower demand for drinking water, through harvesting and reuse. Cooling the region, by mitigating the urban heat island effect. Enhanced biodiversity, by supporting local wildlife and expanding natural habitats.

In 2024, the NSW Government assessed the impacts of development in the West Dapto Urban Release Area. The study found that continuing with a business-as-usual approach would harm Lake Illawarra. To address this finding, this Chapter incorporates pollution reduction targets based on the NSW Government’s Lake Illawarra Catchment – Applying a risk-based framework for improving water quality for an area identified as Zone A.
