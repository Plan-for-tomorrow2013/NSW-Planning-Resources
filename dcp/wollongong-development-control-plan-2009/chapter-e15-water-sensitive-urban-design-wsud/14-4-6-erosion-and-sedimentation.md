### 4.6 Erosion and sedimentation

The Erosion and Sedimentation Development Controls in this Chapter apply to all development or activity that will involve disturbance of the soil surface, or that includes the cut or placement of fill, or storage of materials over three tonnes. Developments must comply with the following requirements:

1\) During the construction of WSUD measures, applicants must comply with the actions and recommendations listed in the latest version of Managing Urban Stormwater: Soils and Construction (the Blue Book) prepared by Landcom.

2\) The WSUD measures implemented by the developer must be designed and executed to effectively control and direct stormwater runoff in a manner that prevents erosion of natural waterways. The selected measures must ensure that the flow of water is responsibly managed to avoid any adverse impacts on, surrounding water courses, including the risk of increased sedimentation, bank instability, or degradation of aquatic habitats.

3\) Stormwater runoff must not discharge directly into waterways or bushland. It must either be prevented or treated to reduce erosion, sedimentation, nutrient loads, and the spread of invasive seeds.

4\) Development must minimise site disturbance, including impact on vegetation and significant trees. For larger developments and subdivisions, site disturbances must be staged to limit erosion and reduce erosion risk. All overland stormwater flow must be diverted around any exposed sections of the site.

5\) Soil loss due to the development must be minimised through effective site management practices that reduce sedimentation impacts on downstream waterways and drainage systems and minimise windblown soil loss.

6\) Developers must adopt an environmentally sensitive and sustainable approach to prevent erosion and maintain the site’s natural hydrology. This includes minimising the amount of cut and fill proposed for the site by:

i) ensuring subdivisions and building designs follow the existing site contours,

ii) using foundation systems which minimise ground and tree root disturbances, and

iii) use grassed swales to direct stormwater towards vegetated areas at regular intervals (e.g. every three metres). This helps reduce flow volume and allows for shallower depressions in driveway profiles.
