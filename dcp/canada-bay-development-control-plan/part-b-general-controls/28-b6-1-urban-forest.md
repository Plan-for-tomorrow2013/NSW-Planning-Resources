#### B6.1  Urban Forest

The urban forest plays a critical role in creating healthy cities; they improve air quality, absorb carbon and rainfall, cool local environments, and support wildlife. The urban forest helps to create attractive urban places, providing seasonal variation and creating memorable landmarks.

A healthy and well-managed urban forest provides multiple environmental, social, and economic benefits.

#### Objectives

O1. To safeguard longevity and optimal health for all protected trees.

O2. To protect the urban forest by retaining existing trees and vegetation on public and private land. O3. To increase total canopy cover within the LGA and achieve canopy targets outlined in this section. O4. To enhance the urban forest to provide maximum benefits to the community and increase liveability. O5. Minimise conflicts between people, infrastructure and the urban forest.

O6. To grow and protect a resilient and diverse urban forest to ensure biodiversity. O7. To conserve and enhance the tree canopy and greenscape and to enhance visual amenity in the public domain.

O8. Maximise opportunities for positive interactions between people, infrastructure and the urban forest.

#### Controls

C1. The following minimum number of canopy trees are to be accommodated on site for all development and development types, whether new or involving alterations and additions as per Table B-P.

#### Table B-P Minimum canopy trees

| Lot/ dwelling | Minimum number of canopy trees | Tree canopy target (min % of site area) |
|---|---|---|
| Less than 400sqm | 2 | 20% |
| Greater than 400 but less than 600 | 3 | 25% |
| Greater than 600 but less than 800 | 4 | 30% |
| Every additional 200sqm | 1 additional tree | - |

#### Controls

C2. If replacement planting is required in accordance with this DCP, the minimum number of canopy trees required on site in accordance with the above control shall be increased to accommodate the replacement planting in accordance with this DCP.

All proposed developments are to be designed to enable planting to these additional requirements unless, regardless of the design, it can be demonstrated that there is insufficient available area for additional tree planting.

C3. Council has established canopy targets for the City of Canada Bay LGA based on the land zones provided below.  Conditions imposed on development sites will consider how the development will contribute to these targets. See Table B-Q for minimum canopy targets.

#### Table B-Q Minimum canopy target

| Land zone | Minimum canopy target |
|---|---|
| All zones | 25% |

#### Controls

C4. Where land in the immediate vicinity has access to views (as considered in Access to views section of this DCP), the proposed trees will consist of open form species to allow views to be gained through the canopy.  Note: Views are part of the public domain and not an exclusive right.

C5. Trees that are to be accommodated within the front setback are to be provided in accordance with Table B-R.

#### Table B-R Minimum trees in front setbacks

| Lot/ dwelling | Minimum number of canopy trees | Minimum height at maturity (m) | Minimum canopy spread at maturity (m) | Minimum landscaped area (sqm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Front setback <4.5m | 1 | 6-8 | 5 | 10sqm 4.5m wide |
| Front setback >=4.5m | 1 | 8-15 | 7 | 4.5 x 4.5 |

#### Controls

C6. Trees that are to be accommodated within the rear yard/private open space/ common open space are to be provided in accordance with Table B-S.

#### Table B-S Minimum trees in rear setbacks/ open spaces

| Lot/ dwelling | Minimum number of canopy trees | Minimum height at maturity (m) | Minimum canopy spread at maturity (m) | Minimum landscaped area (sqm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| All | 1 | 8-15 | 7 | 4.5 x 4.5 |

#### Table B-T Minimum Soil Volumes and Dimensions

| Plant type | Minimum Tree Dimensions | Soil volume | Soil depth | Soil area |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Large canopy trees | 12-18m high, up to 16m crown spread at maturity | 150m3 | 1,200mm | 10m x 10m or equivalent |
| Medium canopy trees | 8-12m high, up to 8m crown spread at maturity | 35m3 | 1,000mm | 6m x 6m or equivalent |
| Small canopy trees | 6-8m high, up to 4m crown spread at maturity | 9m3 | 800mm | 3.5m x 3.5m or equivalent |

Note 1: For trees planted on private property, it is the responsibility of the land owner to ensure that trees reach the mature dimensions (at maturity) as specified by Council in relevant approvals or conditions of consent. Trees which fail to survive, or that fail to reach specified mature dimensions must be replaced by the land owner.

For trees planted on public owned or managed land, as a condition of development, care must be provided by the proponent for a period of 12 months in accordance with Council’s Planting and Maintenance specification which will be provided as part of relevant approvals or conditions of consent. Trees which fail to survive must be replaced by the proponent.

Note 2: The above table containing minimum soil depths for planting on a structure has been calculated assuming fortnightly irrigation. Any sub-surface drainage requirements are in addition to the above minimum soil depths.
