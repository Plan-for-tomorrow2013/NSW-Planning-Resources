#### B6.7  Waterways and Foreshore Areas

Waterways and their foreshore areas play a critical function in ecological processes. Natural foreshore areas improve water quality and act as valuable corridors for native flora and fauna.

#### Objectives

O1. Protect, restore and maintain ecological processes, natural systems and biodiversity within foreshore areas.

O2. Minimise sedimentation and pollution affecting waterways and foreshore areas.

| Controls |  |
|---|---|
| C1. | Stormwater flow is to be designed to mimic natural run-off conditions and ensure a dispersed pattern of flow, avoiding newly created centralised or concentrated discharge points into waterways and/or foreshore areas. |
| C2. | Development is to maintain the biophysical, hydrological and ecological integrity of waterways and foreshore areas, ensuring sediment loads, salinity and chemical release to waterways are unchanged. |
| C3. | Where stormwater is proposed to be discharged to a wetland or waterway, pollution is to be reduced by installation of pollution and sediment control devices. Access to and cleaning of devices shall not compromise the wetland area’s function or natural attributes. The following standards are to be met: a) Pollutant levels shall be below those outlined in the ANZECC (2001) Guidelines for the Protection of Aquatic Ecosystems. b) Pollution control devices shall be located so that they are not within a wetland or watercourse itself. |
| C4. | In foreshore areas where sites contain locally native vegetation comprising species consistent with Coastal and/or Estuarine Swamp Oak Floodplain Forest, Coastal and/or Estuarine Saltmarsh, vegetation is to be retained. This includes fenced protection zones during development and the restriction of impacts to vegetation including stormwater and sediment run-off. |
