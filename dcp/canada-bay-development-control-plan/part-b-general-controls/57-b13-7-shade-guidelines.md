#### B13.7  Shade guidelines

Well-designed and correctly positioned shade provides protection from UV radiation where it is needed, at the right time of day and at the right time of year.

#### Objectives

O1. Ensure outdoor spaces are comfortable to use in all seasons.

O2. Protect users from direct and indirect sources of UV radiation.

O3. To provide well-designed shade.

#### All development types:

| C4. | Extend overhead barriers past use areas. Make sure there is at least one metre of overhang past the actual area of use. |
|---|---|
| C5. | Avoid highly reflective surfaces. Choose surfaces that reflect minimal UV radiation. |
| C6. | Consider the arrangement of existing structures. A large number of small shade structures can be grouped together to form a single larger canopy for greater protection. |

| Controls |  |
|---|---|
| C1. | Use a combination of built shade and natural shade to adequately shade an area. |
| C2. | Provide a shade structure/covered outdoor entertaining area that is an adequate size and proportionate to the size of the development and the number of potential occupants/users. Larger shade structures have more area that is not affected by indirect UV radiation reflecting in from the sides. |
| C3. | Provide barriers for side as well as overhead protection. Vertical screening with plants and trellises or opaque louvres can help to block indirect UV radiation, while still allowing breezes to flow through. |

#### Medium density developments:

C7. Provide a response that takes into consideration the Cancer Council NSW Guidelines to Shade.
