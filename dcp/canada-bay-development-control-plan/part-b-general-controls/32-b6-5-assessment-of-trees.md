#### B6.5  Assessment of trees

#### Objectives

O1. Providing a guide to the regulatory framework for the preservation of trees; O2. Helping in establishing a coordinated approach to the assessment and management of trees.

#### Controls

C1. The issue of a permit for the pruning or removal of tree(s) will be based on the following considerations:

a) The nominated tree works are consistent with the CCB LEP and DCP.

b) Arboricultural, ecological and/or occupational health and safety-based evaluation to determine the significance of the trees. Accordingly, any application for removal should demonstrate that the removal of the tree is appropriate based on an assessment of the significance/ health/longevity of the tree and risk to human life or property.

c) Where the tree presents an unacceptable level of risk (based on QTRA or TRAQ) that cannot be mitigated through pruning or other risk mitigation measures.

d) Any other reason at the discretion of Council’s staff, which can be justified either on technical or legal grounds according to particular circumstances.

e) Where trees to be retained will be impacted by development, the provisions of Australian Standard AS 4970 Protection of Trees on Development Sites must be applied.

f) All tree pruning work must be carried out by an Arborist of Australian Qualification Framework (AQF) level 3 and above, in accordance with Australian Standard AS 4373 Pruning of Amenity Trees.

g) Any tree approved to be removed from a site in accordance with a permit or development consent issued by Council, may have replacement conditioned in accordance with Part B6.6 of this DCP.

h) When it is suspected that trees are causing damage to structures or infrastructure, or that it is believed that trees must be removed to repair damage to structures or infrastructure, it must be demonstrated that no course of action that will allow the tree to be retained is viable. The burden of proof is on the applicant.

Evidence may be required to demonstrate that the tree to be removed meets the above criteria. This evidence is to be in the form of an arborists’ report (prepared by an AQF5 arborist in accordance with Council's 'Preparation of an Arborist Report'), and/or a structural engineer's report (prepared by a qualified structural engineer in accordance with Council's 'Preparation of a Structural Engineer's Report'). Refer to Tree fact sheets on Council's Forms and Factsheets webpage for more information.

Where tree works are required for development, the appropriate planning pathways must be pursued i.e.: DA, CDC, Exempt Development etc. Council will not consider tree works for development via a Tree Pruning and Removal Permit.

#### Controls

C2. Council will generally not issue a permit or development consent for the pruning or removal of tree(s) for the following reasons:

a) The dropping of leaves, flowers, fruit, sap, seeds or small elements of deadwood (or other natural processes);

b) Insect/animal nuisance (including termites and other wood boring insects);

c) Solar access to solar panel or data receivers (in the instance where an established tree was there first);

d) Increase general natural light or reduce shade created by a tree;

e) Enhance views;

f) Minor lifting of driveways, paths and paving or minor damage to outbuildings, garden structures, walls or landscape structures;

g) Damage to underground services (such as sewer lines, water services) where there are feasible alternatives to mitigate or solve problems and retain the tree;

h) The tree is large or overhanging neighbouring property or roof line;

i) Pruning to reduce height, except pruning to reduce the height of hedge/s. In some circumstances it may be necessary for you to supply, at your cost, an independent AQF5 arborist’s, structural, plumber’s and or pest report. A comprehensive report must meet the criteria as outlined in Council’s Guidelines for the Preparation of Reports available from Council. The Tree Management Officer will determine if such reports are necessary and such circumstances may include those where there is the possibility that the tree has been deliberately harmed or extra supporting information is needed.

#### Emergency Procedures Controls

C3. If a tree on your property is identified as posing an imminent risk to human life or property you should contact Council outlining why the tree is considered to be a risk and requires priority assessment as part of an application. Council may require a statement and/or photos (prepared by an AQF5 Arborist) to demonstrate that the tree requires immediate removal. Council will issue an expedited consent to allow removal of the tree if they are satisfied it poses and imminent risk under the provisions of Part 2.2 Clause 2.7 (3a) of the State Environmental Planning Policy (Biodiversity and Conservation) 2021. If Council is not satisfied that the tree is a risk to human life or property you will be advised to lodge the relevant application for processing in due course.
