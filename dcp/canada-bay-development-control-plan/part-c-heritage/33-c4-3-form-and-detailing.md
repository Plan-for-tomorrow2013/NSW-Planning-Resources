#### C4.3  Form and detailing

The form of a building is its overall shape and volume and the arrangement of its parts. The rooflines of buildings, and elements such as chimneys, parapet walls, verandahs etc can contribute greatly to the character of an area.

#### Objectives

O1. To ensure that the form and detailing of new development relates positively to the character of the conservation area.

O2. To ensure that buildings that contribute to the character of a conservation area retain their importance in the streetscape and/or townscape. O3. To encourage authentic restoration or reconstruction based on documentary (research) or physical evidence.

| Controls |  |
|---|---|
| C1. | Development must keep and repeat the single storey scale of similar original buildings that contribute to the conservation area, including the wall height. |
| C2. | New buildings should utilise architectural language compatible to that of the area and the immediate streetscape, including scale, roof form and slope, massing, proportions, fenestration patterns, materials, finishes, colours and other features. |
| C3. | First floor additions are not permitted if they require changes to the main roof of an existing house. |
| C4. | Rear additions are to be formed within existing side setbacks of the house. |
| C5. | The maximum wall plate height of an addition must not exceed the wall plate height of the existing house, as measured externally from the ground to under the eaves. |
| C6. | The scale of proposed alterations and additions must not overwhelm that of the main body of the existing building. The main body of the existing building must remain the visually dominant built form in the streetscape. In the case of a house, the main part of the house is generally set under the main roof. |
| C7. | Side walls must not exceed 7 metres in length. |

| Controls |  |
|---|---|
| C1. | Preferred forms of additions are linked pavilions or single storey skillion extensions. An alternative option, which will be considered on its merits, is to extend the existing roof over a single storey addition. In this case the new roof must match the form and slope of the existing roof and be set below the existing main ridge. |
| C2. | Important elements of the form of a contributory building, such as main roof forms, chimneys, parapet walls, verandahs etc, must be retained, must also retain their integrity (including structural integrity), and must not be obscured by alterations and additions. |
| C3. | The roof forms of new buildings must complement the original roof forms of nearby contributory buildings. |
| C4. | The roof forms of additions must complement that of the existing main roof of the house, if it is a contributory building. |
| C5. | Chimneys and roof features (eg ventilation gablets and louvres) must not |
| C6. | be removed from contributory buildings. Additions and alterations to existing buildings that contribute to the character of a conservation area should not detract from the original form of the existing |
| C7. | building as viewed from the public realm. Additions should utilise architectural language compatible to that of the original building. This includes scale, massing, roof form and slope, proportions, fenestration patterns, materials, finishes, colours and other externally visible features. |
| C8. | The treatment of the street façade of new development should relate to existing nearby buildings that contribute to the conservation area. This should include consideration of the massing and modifications of the building, proportions of verandahs, rhythm of openings, and height. |

| C9. | Keep existing roof forms on original |
|---|---|
| C10. | houses visible in their original form. Additional rooms above the existing main body of the house should be within the existing roof space as seen from the street, ventilated by flat in-plane windows facing the rear. Alterations of roof form, dormer windows, or mansard roofs are not supported. Open verandahs visible from the public domain are to be retained. |
| C11. | Inappropriate changes, such as the enclosure of front verandahs, are to be |

#### Controls

C15. Pavilion style additions:

• Links to a rear pavilion style addition must be set below the existing eaves and must have a minimum width of 1.5 metres (ie the distance from the rear wall of the main section of the existing house to the wall of the higher part of the proposed addition).

• The wall plate height may be 1 metre higher than the existing wall plate height such that the roof space can be used for a first floor.

• The external wall material and colour must be the same as the ground floor.

• The roof must have a simple pitched form with a single ridge and a roof slope to match that of the main roof of the house.

• The roof ridge of the pavilion may be 1 metre higher than the highest ridge of the main roof.

• Large dormers must be located on a rear roof plane. A single small dormer may be located on a side roof plane.

• Colours and materials must blend in with those of the retained house. In particular, wall and roof materials and colours must match those of the retained section of the existing house.

C16. New development and additions to buildings located on corner allotments must appropriately address the side street with regard to scale, form, materials and colours. It may be that the scale of an addition will need to be limited to a single storey.

C17. The roof space above the original house should not be integrated into an addition but should remain separate. Original roof forms should be reinstated.

C18. Additions must not be made to the front or sides of the main body of an original house.
