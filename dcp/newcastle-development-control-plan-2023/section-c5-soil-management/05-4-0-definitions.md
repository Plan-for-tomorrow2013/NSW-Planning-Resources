### 4.0 Definitions

A word or expression has the same meaning as it has in LEP 2012, unless otherwise defined. Other words and expressions include:

Clean water diversion - is a structure or control that intercepts clean runoff upstream of a project site or disturbed area and directs the clean runoff around rather than through the project site or disturbed area.

Flocculant - is a substance that causes colloidal particles to clump into larger units or ‘flocs’ that can either settle in a reasonable time or be filtered.

Pit baskets - are a device placed inside a stormwater pit used to collect sediments and/or gross pollutants, stopping them from being conveyed into downstream waterways.

Receiving waters - are waters into which water discharges flow from an activity.

Sediment basin – is a basin or tank in which stormwater containing settleable solids is retained to remove by gravity or ﬁltration a part of the suspended matter.

Urban heat island effect - is the tendency of cities to be much warmer than their rural counterparts. Urban surfaces such as roads and roofs absorb, hold, and re-radiate heat; raising the temperature in our urban areas. Human activities such as traffic, industry, and electricity usage also generate heat that adds to the urban heat island effect

Virgin excavated natural material (VENM) – is natural material, such as clay, gravel, sand, soil or rock fines that: o has been excavated or quarried from areas that are not contaminated with manufactured chemicals or process residues, as a result of industrial, commercial, mining or agricultural activities o does not contain any sulfidic ores or soils or any other waste o includes excavated natural material that meets such criteria for virgin excavated natural material as may be approved by the NSW Environment Protection Authority (EPA).
