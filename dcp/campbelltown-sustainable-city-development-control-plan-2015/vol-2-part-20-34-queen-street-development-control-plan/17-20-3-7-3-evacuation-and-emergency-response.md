#### 20.3.7.3 Evacuation and Emergency Response

#### Objectives

• To ensure safe evacuation and/or shelter in place of all occupants (residents, workers, and

visitors) during flood events.

• To support development with appropriate flood emergency planning consistent with NSW

SES guidance.

• To prioritise horizontal evacuation where feasible and provide shelter-in-place or vertical

evacuation alternatives where necessary.

• To facilitate emergency services access during flood conditions.

#### Controls

#### Low Hazard Horizontal Evacuation (5% AEP and below)

1\.	 Development must demonstrate provision for horizontal evacuation for all floods up to and

including Flood Planning Level 1 (5% AEP).

2\.	 The evacuation path must be lowstorage hazard, defined as H2 or less in accordance with Sec­

tion 7.2.7 of Australian Rainfall & Runoff – Book 6 (Ball et al., 2019).

#### Horizontal Evacuation for 1% AEP to PMF

3\.	 Horizontal evacuation is preferred for all flood events up to and including PMF, where the fol­

lowing can be demonstrated:

• Pedestrian access is available via a rising road to a designated area of refuge above the

PMF.

• Evacuation pathways do not pass through H2 hazard areas or areas of deepening water.

• The building provides at least one exit point above PMF, accessible to all occupants.

• All evacuation measures are inclusive and accessible, where practicable.

• Evacuation does not rely on lifts, elevators, or similar mechanical means.

• Adequate provision is made for emergency services access (NSW SES, Ambulance, Fire and

Rescue) during flood conditions.

#### Vertical Evacuation/Shelter in Place (PMF)

4\.	 Where horizontal evacuation is not feasible for flood events up to and including PMF, develop­

ment must provide for shelter-in-place or vertical evacuation, ensuring:

• Internal accessibility for all occupants.

• Safety from floodwaters at all times.

• Ongoing access to essential services, including power, water, and communications.

#### Flood Emergency Response Plan (FERP)

5\.	 Development that involves any of the following must be supported by a Flood Emergency

Response Plan (FERP):

• Construction of a new building.

• Significant alterations to an existing building.

• Intensification of land use or occupancy.

6\.	 The FERP must:

• Prohibit evacuation by private vehicle when external floodwaters are unsafe.

• Address combined flood and fire scenarios with appropriate response protocols.

• Include a building-specific flood emergency response plan, similar to a fire drill, and

demonstrate that it will be tested at least annually.

• Provide evidence of consultation with relevant state and local emergency agencies dur­

ing preparation.

• Be consistent with the NSW SES “Flood Safe” Guide and other applicable state guidance.
