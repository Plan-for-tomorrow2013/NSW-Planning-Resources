#### 20.3.7 Flooding

#### Objectives

•	 To ensure the safety of existing and future occupants and property of the subject site by en­

suring that flood risk associated with development is minimised and/or not increased beyond

the level that is acceptable by Council.

•	 To ensure the proponents of development and the community in general are fully aware of the

potential flood hazard and consequent risk associated with the use and development of land

within the site

•	 To reduce the impact of flooding on the site and its surrounding

•	 To design development, in full knowledge of the flooding risk, to alleviate flooding and risk.

•	 To minimise the risk to life by ensuring that the provision of appropriate flash flooding shelter

in place measures evacuation measures are available and where appropriate evacuation routes

are provided.

•	 To maximise the potential for buildings to be returned to use as quickly and efficiently as pos­

sible after being affected by flooding.

#### Controls

1\.	 Any future development application is to address the flooding requirements outlined in the

Flood Risk Management Manual 2023.

#### Note

#### Flood Hazard Categories and Terminology

Flood hazard categorisation is determined based on the depth and velocity of floodwaters and

is used to identify the level of risk posed to life, property, and infrastructure during major flood

events. Flood modelling for the site and any proposed development must classify flood hazard catego­

ries in accordance with recognised flood risk management practice. Hazard classification must

include, at a minimum, the following categories from lowest to most severe:

• Generally safe for people, vehicles, and buildings

• H2: Unsafe for small vehicles

• H3: Unsafe for vehicles, children, and the elderly

• H4: Unsafe for vehicles and people

• H5: Unsafe for vehicles and people; all building types are vulnerable to structural damage

• H6: Unsafe for vehicles and people; all building types are considered vulnerable to failure Flood hazard mapping must be included in the Flood Impact Risk Assessment (FIRA) to clearly

identify the extent and distribution of each hazard category across the site.

#### Flood Terminology

• Defined Flood Event (DFE) - The flood event selected as a general standard for the man­

agement of flooding to development Aims to reduce the frequency of flooding but does

not remove all flood risk, for example, in selecting a 1% AEP flood as a DFE you are accept­

ing that there is a 1 in 100 chance that a larger event will occur in any year.

• Annual Exceedance Probability (AEP) - The chance of a flood of a given or larger size oc­

curring in any one year, usually expressed as a percentage AEP is generally the preferred

terminology. ARI is the historical way of describing a flood event, for example, a 1% AEP

flood has a 1% or 1 in 100 chance of being reached or exceeded in any given year.

• Flood Planning Level (FPL) - The combination of the flood level from the DFE and free­

board selected for FRM purposes Different FPLs may apply to different types of develop­

ment. Determining the FPL for typical residential development should generally start with

a DFE of the 1% AEP flood plus an appropriate freeboard.

• Freeboard - Freeboard in the context of flooding refers to an extra height added to the

predicted flood level, acting as a buffer against uncertainties and unforeseen events dur­

ing flood events. This additional height, typically 0.5 meters, is incorporated into the Flood

Planning Level (FPL) to ensure structures and developments are protected from potential

flood damage. Freeboards for development controls and mitigation works will differ. In

addition, freeboards for development control may vary with the type of flooding and with

the type of development.

• Probable Maximum Flood (PMF) - The largest flood that could conceivably occur at a par­

ticular location, usually estimated from probable maximum precipitation (PMP), and cou­

pled with the worst flood-producing catchment conditions This is equivalent to the proba­

ble maximum precipitation flood in Australian Rainfall and Runoff (ARR).
