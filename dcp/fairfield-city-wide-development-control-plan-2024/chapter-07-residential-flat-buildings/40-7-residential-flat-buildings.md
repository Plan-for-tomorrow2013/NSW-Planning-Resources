## Chapter 7 – Residential Flat Buildings

ii. provide a deep soil zone of no less than 25% of the required open space area which adjoins deep soil zones of neighbouring properties where possible.

#### 7.8.1.2 Landscaping for Shop Top Housing

#### Objectives

a) To create attractive buildings, public spaces and walkways;

b) To improve visual quality and contribute to positive presentation to public domain;

c) To reduce impacts on climate change at the local level and improve the natural environmental features and local ecology of the centre;

d) To provide a buffer between existing residential dwellings;

#### Controls

a) Development shall incorporate landscaping in the form of planter boxes to soften the upper level of buildings.

b) Street trees are to be planted away from commercial awnings to minimise future maintenance issues.

c) Car parking areas, shall be landscaped in accordance with the controls in Chapter 12 – Car Parking, Vehicle and Access Management.

#### 7.8.2 Fencing and Walls

#### 7.8.2.1 Fences and Walls for Residential Flat Buildings

Fences and walls should be designed to define the boundaries between the development, provide privacy and security and contribute positively to the public domain. They also provide privacy by screening views and security by restricting access. Fences and walls can contribute to the image and identity of the place.

#### Objectives

a) To define the edges between public and private land.

b) To define the boundaries between areas within the development having different functions or owners.

c) To provide privacy and security.

d) To contribute positively to the public domain.

e) Ensure developments avoid blank canvas walls that attract graffiti.

f) Characterize a place within the built environment by marking the change in landscape.

#### Controls

a) Fence design - Fences adjoining streets are to reflect the materials of the buildings
