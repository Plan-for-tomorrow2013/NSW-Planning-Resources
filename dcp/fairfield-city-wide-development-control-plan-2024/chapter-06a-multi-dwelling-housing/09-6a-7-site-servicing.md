#### 6A.7  Site Servicing

Utilities and services include the day-to-day facets that every single dwelling uses. To ensure utilities and services are considered the following guidelines and controls are provided.

#### Objectives

a) To ensure that developments are serviced and provide for facilities.

b) To ensure front façades are free of utility services and structures.

c) To ensure dwellings are able to be identified by street number.

d) To have all services and servicing spaces must be properly integrated into the façade design, internalised and screened from the public view where possible

#### Controls 6A.7.1 Water and Sewerage

a) Water and sewerage connections must meet the requirements of Sydney Water.

b) Rainwater tanks, collecting rainwater from all site roof areas (including separate garages/sheds etc) and used for toilet flushing, laundry, and garden irrigation are encouraged.

#### 6A.7.2 Electricity

a) Internal/on-site power poles must be located at the intersection of the front and side boundaries. They must be black or grey in colour.

b) Electrical services must satisfy the requirements of the energy provider. Noting:

i. Pre DA consultation with Endeavour Energy is helpful in identifying any existing network assets impacted by the proposed development. Applicants are advised to submit load applications to Endeavour Energy as early as possible in the design phase.

ii. Upon receiving load applications, Endeavour Energy can work with applicants and their Accredited Services Provider L3 (network designer) with the preferred method of supply to their developments: for example, the installation of an indoor or pad mounted substation.

iii. Space required to be allocated for any proposed indoor and pad mounted substations can be incorporated within final architecture plans submitted to Council as part of the DA approvals process.

c) Meter boxes are to be placed in positions acceptable to the energy provider, but must not face the street.

d) Alternative energy systems which generates electricity on the land using an energy source other than the electricity supply grid, and includes a photovoltaic power system, must meet relevant requirements.

e) Substations, pump rooms, garbage storage areas and other service requirements should be located in basement car parks or out of view.

f) At grade electrical substations visible from the street frontage must be incorporated into the details of the landscape plan and be appropriately screened.

#### 6A.7.3 Telephone and Internet

Telephone lines installation must be in accordance with the requirements of the telecommunications provider.

#### 6A.7.4 Dwelling Street Number

a) Contact Council to request the allocation of house number if required.

b) Position and secure clearly visible street numbers near property entrances such as the front fence, wall or letterbox.

c) Street numbers should be positioned at a minimum height at 500mm above ground level (existing).

#### 6A.7.5 Postal Services

a) The size of a letterbox must meet the Australia Post approved size. Australia Post recommends a separate box or tube to accommodate newspaper and non-mail articles.

b) A letterbox must clearly mark the street number of the dwelling that it serves.

c) A letterbox must be located on the boundary of the property with the footpath or road or at the junction of the driveway with the road. The opening of the mailbox must face the footpath or road.

#### 6A.7.6 Waste and Recycling Service

a) Councils waste and recycling service to be provided to all residential dwellings in accordance with section 496 of the Local Government Act 1993.

b) Multi Dwelling Housing are defined in accordance with Fairfield Local Environmental Plan 2013.

c) Multi Dwelling Housing to provide communal Waste and Recycling storage and collection infrastructure integrated wholly within the built form.

d) Waste and Recycling storage and collection infrastructure configurations are outlined in Appendix H Model Waste Infrastructure, section 2.

e) Councils waste and recycling streams to be allocated in accordance with Councils waste generation rates:

i. Garbage = 120L per dwelling/week (collected weekly)

ii. Recycling = 80L per dwelling/week (collected fortnightly)

iii. Organics = 40L per dwelling/week (collected alternate fortnightly) *The bin allocation for each individual stream to be rounded up to the next whole number

f) Councils waste and recycling streams utilise 240L bin infrastructure with minimum dimensions of 600mm width by 740mm depth

g) Specifications of Councils standard 10,800mm heavy rigid waste collection vehicle are outlined in Appendix H Model Waste Infrastructure, sections 2.9 – 2.11. All infrastructure is to be designed to permit unobstructed access for Councils largest collection vehicle required to service Councils waste streams.

h) A detailed Waste Management Plan to be submitted to Council. The plan must:

i. Demonstrate Councils waste and recycling streams can be provided and are accessible to all residential dwellings

ii. Identify the waste streams and anticipated volumes expected to be generated throughout construction and in perpetuity when occupied by residents

iii. Demonstrate integrated waste collection and supporting infrastructure is provided to permit safe and efficient scheduled waste collections by Council

iv. Outline how the waste generated on-site will be managed within the development

v. Identify the path of travel from:

1\) Each residential dwelling to the communal Waste and Recycling storage room

2\) Waste and Recycling storage room to the presentation/collection area

vi. Outline how and where Councils waste streams will be stored, transported and collected for each component of the development

vii. Provide detailed architectural plans to demonstrate compliance with sections i-vi outline above

viii. Be prepared by a suitably qualified consultant experienced in the management of waste and recycling streams

#### 6A.7.6.1 Waste and Recycling Storage and Collection Infrastructure

a) The communal Waste and Recycling storage room to accommodate the following infrastructure and design principles:

i. Be of sufficient size to accommodate the developments residential bin allocation with minimum unobstructed internal dimensions using one of the following calculations:

1\) Single Sided = 2,200mm width by (Bin Allocation x 600mm) depth

2\) Double Sided = 3,200mm width by (Bin Allocation x 300mm) depth

ii. Minimum 1,200mm wide doorway opening

iii. Located within 20,000mm from the kerbside

iv. Integrated wholly within the developments-built form to support an enhanced urban design outcome

v. Enclosed, walled, and not permit through access to other on-site infrastructure

vi. Unobstructed service path to be a minimum 1,200mm wide, concrete, parallel to contours with a maximum 1:24 grade

vii. Rooms to be free of pylons, obstructions and other external services

viii. Permit safe, efficient, and unobstructed work practices for assigned users and waste collection staff

ix. Mechanical ventilation

x. Automated lighting

xi. Hot and cold-water taps

xii. Drainage connected to the sewer

xiii. Minimum unobstructed internal height clearance of 2,700mm

xiv. Water proofing throughout all intersections and extend 1,200mm high on walls

xv. Doors locked through Councils bi-lock system. Councils’ key specifications will be provided upon request

xvi. Provide infrastructure to minimise opportunities for vermin

xvii. Enhance Councils Crime Prevention through Environmental Design (CPTED) principles

xviii. Permit accessible and unobstructed resident access to Councils waste streams

b) To permit scheduled kerbside collections by Councils standard 10,800mm heavy rigid waste collection vehicle, an unobstructed presentation area to be provided and incorporate the following:

i. Large enough to accommodate the developments residential bin allocation with a 500mm clearance between each bin using the following calculation:

1\) Presentation area (mm) = Bin Allocation x 1,100

ii. Located wholly within the lot’s frontage

iii. Free from driveways, on-street parking, footpaths, streets trees and service utilities

iv. Presentation arrangement clearly displayed on architectural plans submitted to Council

v. Presentation areas that exceed 50% of the lots unobstructed frontage are to provide Waste and Recycling storage and collection infrastructure in in accordance with section 6A.7.6.2 of the Fairfield City Wide Development Control Plan 2024

c) To permit scheduled kerbside Bulky Waste Collections by Councils standard 10,800mm heavy rigid collection vehicle, an unobstructed presentation area to be provided. The area to provide minimum dimensions of 2,000mm width by 1,500mm depth clearly displayed on architectural plans submitted to Council

#### 6A.7.6.2 Site Restricted Developments

There may be lots where the developments residential bin allocation cannot be accommodated wholly within the lot’s frontage. A wheel out – wheel in service may be explored to service the development at Councils discretion. Should the site be deemed as viable by Council, the following Waste and Recycling Storage and Collection Infrastructure to be provided to support the provision of a safe and efficient waste collection service:

a) A communal Waste and Recycling storage room is to be provided in accordance with section 6A.7.6.1 of the Fairfield City Wide Development Control Plan 2024 and located within a maximum of 10,000mm from the kerbside.

b) A communal bulky waste collection room to accommodate the following infrastructure and design principles:

i. Minimum unobstructed internal area in accordance with the following calculation:

1\) Bulky waste area (m2) = total dwellings x 0.25m2

\*Minimum area of 10m2 to be provided to accommodate Councils waste stream

ii. Accessed via dual, 180-degree, outwards opening, self-closing sealed doors with a minimum opening of 1,800mm

iii. Minimum unobstructed internal width of 1,800mm

iv. In accordance with section 6A.7.6.1, subsections a) iii – xvii of the Fairfield City Wide Development Control Plan 2024

#### 6A.7.6.3 Integrated On-site Waste Collections

a) Where Councils standard 10,800mm heavy rigid waste collection vehicle is required to enter the site to perform scheduled collections as deemed viable by Council, a detailed swept path analysis, supported by scaled architectural plans to be submitted to Council to permit a detailed assessment. The swept path model to:

i. Demonstrate compliance with Australian Standards (AS) 2890.2 for a Heavy Rigid waste collection vehicle

ii. The preparation of the swept path models to be in accordance with the Austroads Design Vehicles and Turning Path Templates (latest edition)

iii. On-street parking to be provided on both sides of the road to demonstrate a business-as-usual model responsive to local traffic and parking availabilities

iv. Councils standard heavy vehicle waste collection vehicle to:

1\) Enter and exit the site ins a forward direction

2\) Can perform all required access and collection manoeuvres without impeding vehicle, pedestrian, cyclist, and user safety

3\) Have unobstructed minimum height clearances, maximum ramp grades, maximum rates of change, and unobstructed operational clearances throughout all required manoeuvres (ingress, egress, loading bay access) to permit safe and efficient scheduled collections

4\) A minimum 500mm clearance is to be provided from all obstructions throughout all required vehicle manoeuvres to perform scheduled collections

b) Specifications of Councils standard 10,800mm heavy rigid waste collection vehicle are outlined in Appendix H Model Waste Infrastructure, sections 2.9 – 2.11. All infrastructure is to be designed to permit unobstructed access for Councils largest collection vehicle required to service Councils waste streams.

#### 6A.7.7 TV Antennas

a) The antenna must be located at the rear of the site to reduce visibility from the street.

b) Master TV antennas are to be provided where there is more than one dwelling in any development.
