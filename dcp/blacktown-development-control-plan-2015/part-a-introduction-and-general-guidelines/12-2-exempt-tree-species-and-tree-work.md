Council understands that in some situation’s trees can be removed without seeking a

permit from us. These exempt situations and tree species are listed below.

#### A.2.2 Where exemptions do not apply

The exemptions listed above do not apply to:

a. any work on a tree that is or is located on a heritage listed property, a property listed within

a heritage conservation area, Aboriginal object or Aboriginal place of heritage significance

b. any tree that is listed on the Significant Tree and Vegetation Register

c. any land mapped as ‘Biodiversity’ on the Blacktown Local Environment Plan 2015 natural

Resources Map

d. threatened ecological communities or land that provides or has the potential to provide

habitat for native fauna or fauna classified as vulnerable or threatened under the

Biodiversity Conservation Act 2016 or the Environmental Protection and Biodiversity

Conservation Act 1999 (Commonwealth)

e. work that is contrary to a development consent that requires trees to be retained, or

f. a tree and vegetation on public land (eg. within the road reserve, parkland etc).

#### A.2.3 Where a Tree work permit is not required

A permit is not required (with the exception of criteria outlined above in ‘Where exemptions

do not apply’) to remove or prune a tree that is classified as follows.

a. ‘Dead’ - means a tree that is no longer alive and is not a habitat tree (means a tree that

contains hollows used by fauna). Photographic evidence clearly identifying the tree as being

dead is required.

b. ‘Dying tree’ - means a tree that is in such an advanced state of decline due to damage or

disease—where an insufficient amount of live tissue, green leaves or branches exist to

sustain life—that tree death is unavoidable. Evidence in the form of a written statement,

from an Arborist, with minimum qualification of Australian Qualification Framework (AQF)

Level 5 in arboriculture, is required.

c. ‘Dangerous Tree’ - means it is in imminent danger from falling and the action is necessary

to protect human life, buildings, or other property. Evidence, in the form of a written

statement, from an Arborist, with minimum qualification of Australian Qualification

Framework (AQF) Level 5 in arboriculture, is required.

d. Not meeting the definition of a prescribed tree.

e. Pruning of trees to maintain a 500mm clearance from house (private) service wires. This

may be to comply with a direction under Section 48 Electricity Supply Act 1995. The

exemption applies to tree branches only, not trunks.

f. Any removal of trees or abatement of branches required to comply with an order issued by

the Land and Environment Court (L&EC) under provisions in Trees (Disputes Between

Neighbours) Act 2006 unless otherwise directed by the Court.

g. Any removal of trees or abatement of branches required to comply with an order issued by

an authorised Officer of Blacktown City Council under provisions in the Local Government

Act 1993.

h. Trees listed as weeds through the Hawkesbury River County Council (HRCC) in line with

the Biosecurity Act 2015 and are not required for habitat purposes.

i. Clearing of vegetation under the 10/50 Vegetation Clearing Code of Practice (Landowners

must check the New South Wales Rural Fire Service website and open the ‘online tool’ to

find out if their property is within the clearing entitlement area). All clearing must be per this

Code of Practice and apply all relevant conditions to the property.

j. The removal or pruning is an action required, or allowed, to be carried out by, or under, the

Rural Fires Act 1997 without development consent.

k. The removal of torn or dead branches but does not include whole trees.

l. Pruning to maintain a hedge, at existing dimensions, or pruning of trees to create a hedge,

where the component trees do not qualify as prescribed trees.

m. Where tree removal, pruning, maintenance and replacement is undertaken by Council or its

duly authorised servants or agents, on land owned by, or under the care, control and

management of Council.

All work must be undertaken in accordance with the most current edition of Safe Work NSW

Code of Practice for the Amenity Tree Industry and the guidelines in, the current version of,

Australian Standard AS4373 Pruning of amenity trees.

#### A.2.4 Evidence accepted for exempt Tree work

Any of the following will be accepted as evidence and can be requested by us to ascertain

if the exempt works were warranted:

a. Photographs of the tree before removal or pruning occurs which clearly show:

• the species of the tree

• the location of the tree within the property and relative to other structures on the site, and

• a detailed arboricultural assessment report, prepared by an Australian Qualification

Framework (AQF) Level 5 or higher, Arborist, which clearly demonstrates that a tree is

dying and the likely cause of its death.

b. In the case of a “dangerous tree”, photographs of the tree which:

• clearly identify the species of tree

• clearly identify the location of the tree, and

• clearly demonstrate that it was an immediate danger to property or persons and that the

risk could not be alleviated in any alternative way other than by removal of the tree.
