## 10.  Development on flood prone land

### 10.1 General

The information contained within this section is based on the NSW Government's

Flood Prone Land Policy. The information provided below is used by Council as a guide only and compliance

with these development standards does not automatically ensure approval. Each

application will be considered on its individual merits.

### 10.2 Aims and objectives

Council's primary aim in relation to the development of flood prone land is to reduce

the impact of flooding and flood liability on individual owners and occupiers and to

reduce private and public losses resulting from flooding. The aims are as follows:

(a) increase public awareness of the hazard and extent of flooding on flood

prone land

(b) inform the community of Council’s controls and policy for the use and

development of flood prone land

(c) encourage development and construction which is compatible with the

identified flood risk to ensure the safety of the development itself

(d) adopt a merit-based approach to all Development Applications, which considers social,

economic and ecological factors, and

(e) deal equitably and consistently with applications for development on flood prone

land.

To achieve this, the objectives of this section are as follows:

(a) define flood risk precincts to categorise flooding by risk (Section 10.4.2)

(b) categorise land use by its vulnerability to flooding (Section 10.4.3.1)

(c) outline all flood related planning controls (Section 10.4.3.2)

(d) advise the flood related planning controls that apply to a development given its risk of

flooding and vulnerability to flooding (Section 10.4.3.3).

The above aim and objectives should be read in conjunction with the objectives of

Blacktown Local Environmental Plan 2015 and the relevant NSW Government Policies.

### 10.3 Definitions

Definitions or terms used within this Part are provided below. Annual exceedance probability (AEP) means the chance of a flood of a given or

larger size occurring in any one year, usually expressed as a percentage. For

example, a 1% AEP flood has a 1% or 1 in 100 chance of being reached or exceeded

in any given year. Annual Recurrence Interval (ARI) is the historical way of describing

a flood event. Australian Height Datum (AHD) means the official national vertical datum for

Australia which is a plane of level corresponding approximately to mean sea

level. Defined Flood Event (DFE) means the flood event selected as a general standard for

the management of flooding to development. The 1% AEP flood has been selected as

the DFE in Blacktown. Flood means a natural phenomenon where water inundates land that is usually dry,

generally due to weather systems that generate a high amount of rainfall. Flooding can

be due to water flowing within, out of, or towards a waterway. Types of flooding include

riverine, overland flow, groundwater and coastal flooding. Flood liable land is equivalent to Flood Prone Land Flood maps means maps held by Council which indicate predicted flood levels,

flood contours and other relevant information. Flood Planning Area (FPA) means the area of land below the FPL. Flood Planning Level (FPL) means the combination of the flood level from the DFE

and freeboard selected for flood risk management purposes. Flood prone land means land susceptible to flooding by the Probable Maximum Flood

event. Flood prone land is also known as the floodplain, flood liable land and flood

affected land. Freeboard means a factor of safety typically used in relation to the setting of

minimum floor levels or levee crest levels. Future flood risk means the risk future development and its users are exposed to as

a result of its location on the floodplain. This includes future changes to the catchment,

floodplain and climate to allow for an understanding of the future flood behaviour with

changed conditions. Flood Impact and Risk Assessment (FIRA) A study to assess flood behaviour,

constraints and risk, understand offsite flood impacts on property and the community

resulting from the development, and flood risk to the development and its users. Floodway means areas of the floodplain which generally convey a significant discharge

of water during floods. They often align with naturally defined channels. These areas are

those in which human life could be at risk from the passage of flood waters. Floodways

are areas that, even if only partially blocked, would cause a significant redistribution of

flood flow, or a significant increase in flood levels. Habitable floor includes any portion of a building designed, constructed, adapted or

used for human habitation whether forming part of a dwelling or any other building and

includes rumpus rooms and the like. Merit-based approach weighs social, economic, ecological and cultural impacts of

land-use options for different flood prone areas together with flood damage, hazard

and behaviour implications, and environmental protection and wellbeing of the state’s

rivers and floodplains. Overland flow flooding means inundation by local run-off on its way to a waterway.

The capacity of stormwater systems is exceeded and stormwater then flows above

ground following the low areas of the landscape. This is also known as local overland,

flash or pluvial flooding. Probable Maximum Flood (PMF) means the largest flood that could conceivably

occur at a particular location, usually estimated from probable maximum precipitation

(PMP), and where applicable, snow melt, coupled with the worst flood-producing

catchment conditions. Probable maximum precipitation (PMP) means the greatest depth of precipitation

for a given duration meteorologically possible over a given size storm area at a

particular location at a particular time of the year, with no allowance made for long-term

climatic trends (World Meteorological Organization 1986). Riverine flooding means creeks and rivers overflowing, breaking their banks and

flooding nearby low-lying areas and properties. These flows may be several metres

deep in the channels and relatively fast moving. This is also known as mainstream or

fluvial flooding. Survey Plan means a plan prepared by a Registered Surveyor. Unmapped overland flow means areas that are identified as being affected by

flooding which has not yet been investigated and/or listed by Council at that time. Unobstructed flow means the passage of floodwater is free from impediment or

obstruction, such as those caused by structures, temporary blockages, debris and

sediment.

### 10.4 Control of development on flood prone land

#### 10.4.1 Heads of consideration

In determining any application for development on flood prone land, Council will take

into consideration those matters listed under Section 4.15 of the Environmental

Planning and Assessment Act 1979 as appropriate. Council shall also take into

consideration the following:

(a) whether the proposed building materials are suitable.

(b) whether the buildings are to be sited in the optimum position to avoid flood waters

and allow efficient evacuation.

(c) whether proposed structures or the filling of land are likely to affect flood flows.

(d) whether consultation with other authorities is considered necessary.

(e) the NSW Government Flood Risk Management Manual (2023). In assessing Development Applications, Council can only take into account

information that is known to Council at the time of determining such applications.

#### 10.4.2 Flood Risk Precincts

The floodplains and overland flow pathways within Blacktown can be divided into precincts based on different levels of potential flood risk. Unmapped areas that have

not yet been investigated to confirm if they are impacted by riverine flooding or overland flow may still be subject to flood controls even if they are not identified in any on the precincts below. The relevant Flood Risk Precincts (FRP’s) for each of the floodplains are outlined below:

#### 10.4.2.1 Riverine High Flood Risk Precinct

Areas of the floodplain which convey a significant discharge of water during floods. These areas often align with naturally defined channels and are equivalent to the floodway during a 1% annual exceedance probability flood. The high flood risk precinct is where the highest flood damages and greatest potential risk to life would be anticipated from floods.

#### 10.4.2.2 Riverine Medium Flood Risk Precinct

Equivalent to the flood planning area. The freeboard used is 500mm This area does not include areas that have already been identified as being within the high flood risk precinct.

#### 10.4.2.3 Riverine Low Flood Risk Precinct

This includes all land that is flood affected by riverine flooding in some capacity, up to and including the Probable Maximum Flood, except for areas that have already been identified as being within the high or medium flood risk precincts.

#### 10.4.2.4 Overland Flow High Flood Risk Precinct

Areas of the floodplain which convey a significant discharge of water during floods. These areas often align with naturally defined channels and are equivalent to high hazard areas during a 1% annual exceedance probability flood. High Hazard is equivalent to the floodway where mapping is available or otherwise it is areas of H5-H6 hazard where floodway mapping is not available. The high flood risk precinct is where the highest flood damages and greatest potential risk to life would be anticipated from floods.

#### 10.4.2.5 Overland Flow Medium Flood Risk Precinct

Equivalent to the flood planning area. The freeboard used is 300mm. This area does not include areas that have already been identified as being within the high flood risk precinct.

#### 10.4.2.6 Overland Flow Low Flood Risk Precinct

This includes all land that is flood affected by overland flow flooding in some capacity, up to and including the Probable Maximum Flood, except for areas that have already been identified as being within the high or medium flood risk precincts.

### 10.5 Work as executed plan

When a work as executed plans is required by Council, it must be prepared by a

Registered Surveyor and contain the following information:

(a) the boundary of the allotment of land and its location with respect to the road that

it fronts

(b) the extent of the filling in relation to the lot boundaries

(c) the level to Australian Height Datum at 15m centres along the top extremities of

the fill and in between such extremities at a 15m grid spacing, and

(d) the origin of the datum and the Permanent Mark or State Survey Mark Number, if

applicable, used to prepare the plan and a bench mark within 200m of the

particular site if the origin of the levels is beyond that distance.
