## 5. Biodiversity, vegetation and tree preservation

Biodiversity, vegetation and trees play a vital role in preserving the natural beauty and

ecological sustainability of our city and provide many benefits by:

(a) combating the urban heat island effect

(b) improving human health both mental and physical

(c) decreasing energy costs

(d) improving air quality

(e) contributing to the character and amenity of the urban environment

(f) maintaining natural ecosystems and providing habitat for local fauna

(g) reducing erosion and absorbing stormwater runoff. In certain circumstances vegetation, including trees, require pruning or removal. Permission to remove vegetation can only be obtained through specific approval processes. These process pathways are:

• development applications

• native vegetation permit

• tree work permit.

### 5.1 Definitions

Biodiversity is defined as the variety of living animal and plant life from all sources. It includes

diversity within and between species as well as the diversity of ecosystems and life it supports. Clearing of vegetation under the State Environment Planning Policy (Biodiversity and Conservation)

2021 includes:

(a) cutting down, felling uprooting, killing, poisoning, ringbarking, burning or otherwise

destroying the vegetation, or

(b) lopping or otherwise removing a substantial part of the vegetation. Habitat includes:

(a) an area periodically or occasionally occupied by a species or ecological community, and

(b) the biotic and abiotic components of an area. Native vegetation is any of the following types of plants native to New South Wales:

(a) trees (including any sapling or shrub or any scrub)

(b) understorey plants,

(c) groundcover (being any type of herbaceous vegetation),

(d) plants occurring in a wetland. A plant is native to New South Wales if it was established in New South Wales before

European settlement. The regulations may authorise conclusive presumptions to be made of the species of plants native to New South Wales by adopting any relevant classification in an official database of plants that is publicly accessible. Permit system (for tree work and/or clearing of native vegetation) is a set of rules and

processes for ‘clearing’ trees and vegetation that is not linked to a development application and is not deemed exempt. Recommended planting list is published in Council’s Vegetation Management Plan Guideline booklet: https://www.blacktown.nsw.gov.au/Plan-build/Stage-2-plans-and-guidelines/Native-vegetation-management Trees are long lived perennial (living longer than 1 year) vegetation that have a trunk and branches

and can reach a height of more than 3 metres. Vegetation is broadly the collection of either trees, understorey plants, groundcover (being any type

of herbaceous vegetation) plants occurring in a wetland and/or grasses (plant life) whether native or

non-native that can be found in a particular area.

### 5.2. Objectives

The objectives of vegetation and tree preservation are to:

(a) ensure that all new development protects and retains existing suitable trees and vegetation and provides opportunities for the growth of native vegetation and newly planted trees

(b) ensure that significant trees and vegetation are protected to retain their special historical, cultural, environmental or amenity values

(c) identify opportunities to protect and increase tree canopy across our City to deliver ongoing environmental, economic and social benefits to our community

(d) establish the procedural framework and requirements governing the pruning, clearing, removal and subsequent replacement of trees and vegetation within Blacktown City

(e) reduce urban heat and promote urban cooling by the retention and planting of trees and vegetation

(f) provide more livable, sustainable and resilient living environments to the residents and visitors to Blacktown City

(g) ensure that development avoids and minimises negative impacts on trees, vegetation and biodiversity, particularly threatened species, populations and ecological communities

(h) ensure that developments that cannot completely avoid impacts to threatened species are offset per the New South Wales Biodiversity Conservation Act 2016.

#### 5.2.2 Native vegetation and development controls

Development and clearing controls for native vegetation are in place to support the key requirements of the New South Wales Biodiversity Conservation Act 2016. These controls establish a framework to avoid, minimise and offset the impacts of proposed development and land use change on biodiversity.

Under Chapter 2 of the State Environmental Planning Policy (Biodiversity and

Conservation) 2021, Council may issue permits for clearing of native vegetation in non—

rural areas.

#### Council may issue permits for clearing of vegetation

(a) A council may issue a permit to a landholder to clear vegetation to which this Part

applies in any non-rural area of the State.

(b) A permit cannot be granted to clear native vegetation in any non-rural area of the

State that exceeds the biodiversity offsets scheme threshold as outlined in Section

5.2.3 of this Part.

(c) A permit under this Part cannot allow the clearing of vegetation:

i. that is or forms part of a heritage item or that is within a heritage

conservation area, or

ii. that is or forms part of an Aboriginal object or that is within an Aboriginal

place of heritage significance, unless the council is satisfied that the

proposed activity,

is of a minor nature or is for the maintenance of the heritage item, Aboriginal

object, Aboriginal place of heritage significance or heritage conservation

area, or

would not adversely affect the heritage significance of the heritage item,

Aboriginal object, Aboriginal place of heritage significance or heritage

conservation area.

A permit may be granted under this Part subject to any conditions specified in the permit.

#### 5.2.3 Biodiversity Offset Scheme

The Biodiversity Offsets Scheme (BOS) provides a mechanism to avoid, minimise and offset the impacts of development and some types of clearing on biodiversity in New South Wales. It is a key requirement of the Biodiversity Conservation Act 2016 (NSW).

The Biodiversity Offsets Scheme applies to a range of developments, land clearing and

activities that meet certain thresholds, where the proposal is:

(a) likely to significantly affect a threatened species or threatened ecological community (as

determined by the five-part Test of Significance), or

(b) seeking to undertake clearing or other prescribed impacts on native vegetation that occurs

on the NSW Biodiversity Values Map (BVM), or

(c) exceeds the clearance area thresholds outlined in Table 5.1 below:

| Minimum lot size | Proposed area of clearing |
|---|---|
| Less than 1 hectare | 0.25 hectares |
| Less than 2 hectares | 0.5 hectares |
| 2-39 hectares | 0.5 hectares |
| 40-999 hectares | 1 hectare |

A Biodiversity Development Assessment Report (BDAR) is required to accompany applications to Council for all proposals that exceed the Biodiversity Offset Scheme. The BDAR is to be prepared by an Accredited Assessor and must meet the minimum requirements under the Act and its regulations. Note that for local developments, where a SAII impact is likely then Council as the Consent Authority must refuse the application as per Section 7.16 (2) of the Biodiversity Conservation Act 2016.

#### 5.2.4 Flora and Fauna Assessment

A Flora and fauna assessment report is required for all proposals that do not trigger a

Biodiversity Development Assessment Report, but do potentially impact upon:

(a) land mapped as Cumberland Plain Vegetation 2022, Conservation zoned land, Riparian

corridors and protection Areas and/or Terrestrial Biodiversity in the Blacktown GIS Map

layers, or via the links provided at section 5.2.1 in this document, or

(b) native vegetation which is potential habitat for threatened species, populations or part of an

endangered ecological community as identified in Section 1.6 of the Biodiversity Conservation

Act 2016 or the Fisheries Management Act 1994, or

(c) natural and constructed wetlands, dams or other wet areas. Note a dam dewatering plan will

also be required, or

(d) in stream creek works in riparian channels e.g., bridges, basins. The Flora and Fauna Assessment must include any Five Part Test of Significance under Section 7.3 of the Biodiversity Conservation Act 2016 that may be required. Any likely significant impact will result in the requirement for further investigation and the preparation of either a Species Impact Assessment or a Biodiversity Development Assessment Report. The flora and fauna assessment is to give regard to Clause 7.2 and 7.3 of Blacktown Local

Environmental Plan 2015.

#### 5.2.5 Vegetation Management Plan

All proposals which are adjacent to, or likely to directly or indirectly impact upon environmental protection areas on public or private lands, will require a Vegetation Management Plan (VMP). The VMP will provide details on the protection, regeneration, restoration or reconstruction works to be undertaken. All plans are to be prepared in accordance with Council’s Vegetation Management Plan Guidelines (2019), available on Council’s webpage at: https://www.blacktown.nsw.gov.au/Plan-build/Stage-2-plans-and-guidelines/Native-vegetation-management The implementation of a Vegetation Management Plan may be bonded with release of monies contingent upon regular stage reports to Council as a Condition of Consent. The implementation of the Vegetation Management Plan and the ongoing management of environmental protection lands in perpetuity may be subject to bonding, conditions of consent or a positive covenant (Section 88B) to be registered on the relevant lot title.

#### 5.2.6 Dewatering of wetlands, dams and wet areas

All development proposing dewatering of natural and constructed wetlands, dams or other wet areas are required to undertake assessments for biodiversity. This is to ensure measures are taken to reduce impacts on wildlife and to ensure humane treatment and relocation is undertaken. A dam dewatering plan also identifies and considers the risks of contaminated sediment and water disposal and protecting other water bodies from contamination and weed species. All developments seeking to de-water these areas will require a dam dewatering plan or decommissioning plan addressing the above.

#### 5.2.7 Biodiversity Management Plan

A Biodiversity Management Plan (BMP) is required on all land where proposals are likely to directly or indirectly impact upon habitat features or native fauna adjacent to or within the area of work. The Biodiversity Management Plan is to identify actions and mitigation measures to be undertaken to reduce the impact on biodiversity prior to, during and following land clearing for remediation, subdivision and construction works. Development should avoid the fragmentation of exiting native vegetation and should seek to retain, or re-use habitat features and natural resources including rock outcrops, wetland areas, topsoil, mature tree hollows and native seed sources. A Biodiversity Management Plan may include a flora and fauna report, dam dewatering plan, tree and bushland protection in the one document.

### 5.3 Vegetation and trees

#### 5.3.1 Exempt vegetation and trees

The vegetation and tree species that are exempt from requiring approval to perform works are identified in Appendix 1 and 2. Exempt trees do not need development approval or a Tree Work Permit from Council under

Part 2.7 of the State Environmental Planning Policy (Biodiversity and Conservation) 2021.

#### 5.3.2 Prescribed vegetation

Prescribed vegetation is:

(a) any collection of native vegetation, either trees, shrubs, and/or grasses (plant life)

irrespective of size that:

• occurs on Council’s Cumberland Plain Vegetation 2022, Riparian Protection Area (RPN) or

Terrestrial Biodiversity (BIO Terrestrial Biodiversity) maps, or

• meets the definition of native vegetation

(b) any collection of trees plants and grasses (plant life) whether native or non-native located

on private land irrespective of size:

• that is located on a heritage listed property, or

• that is located on a property located within a heritage conservation area, or

• that is, or forms part of, an Aboriginal object, or

• that is within an Aboriginal place of heritage significance.

Legal provisions for the preservation of prescribed vegetation are in force under State

Environmental Planning Policy (Biodiversity and Conservation) 2021.

#### 5.3.3 Prescribed trees

A prescribed tree is:

(a) any perennial tree (living longer than 1 year) or palm, whether native or introduced

species, located on private land, that:

• has a height of more than 3 metres, and/or

• has a trunk diameter of more than 200mm measured 1.4 m above ground level, and

• is growing in the ground, not in a container

(b) any perennial (living longer than 1 year) tree or palm, whether native or introduced

species, located on public land, irrespective of size

(c) any perennial (living longer than 1 year) tree or palm, whether native, or introduced

species, irrespective of size that is:

• located on a heritage listed property, or

• located on a property located within a heritage conservation area, or

• within an Aboriginal place of heritage significance, or

• forms part of an Aboriginal object.

Legal provisions for the preservation of prescribed trees are in force under State

Environmental Planning Policy (Biodiversity and Conservation) 2021.

#### 5.3.4 Significant Tree and Vegetation Register

Vegetation including trees that have been identified as having significance in Blacktown City are listed on Council’s Significant Tree and Vegetation register, available at: https://www.blacktown.nsw.gov.au/Services/Tree-management/Trees-on-public-land The trees and vegetation listed in the Significant Tree and Vegetation Register must be preserved. Removal should only be considered where all other available options have been exhausted. The community and Council have identified these trees and vegetation as they either have significant amenity, historical, botanical value or are functionally important links to threatened fauna and biodiversity. Members of the community must check with Council, prior to the design stage of any development, to determine whether or not a site has any tree or vegetation listed in the Significant Tree and Vegetation Register.

#### 5.3.6 Tree Work Permit applications

Council encourages the retention of appropriate, existing trees and vegetation to maintain and increase urban tree canopy cover and to reduce the urban heat island effect, improve liveability, sustainability and resilience. An application for a permit to remove, prune or excavate near (within 3m) trees is to be made and accompanied by a fee. Where a permit to remove a prescribed tree is sought, the appropriate Arboricultural Report is to be submitted with the application and must provide sufficient justification for tree works. Residents making tree applications should ensure that all relevant material and documentation that they wish to be considered as part of the application is included at the time of lodgement. In assessing an application to remove, prune or excavate near a tree, Council will consider, but is not limited to, the following factors:

(a) the immediately evident health and structure of a tree

(b) environmental, cultural and amenity value of the tree

(c) the likely effect of the proposed work on the health and structural integrity of the tree

(d) whether the tree shows poor form, shape and/or vigour typical of the species

(e) its location within the context of the property, its topography and buildings

(f) whether there are other viable management methods to alleviate the issues as stated on

the application

(g) whether there are other viable alternatives to open excavation available. This may include

use of alternative construction techniques, e.g. directional boring, or relocation of sub-

surface services

(h) whether the tree is located in a habitat corridor and/or provides habitat or fauna canopy

connectivity

(i) the evaluation and recommendations of an Arboricultural Report

(j) the occurrence (or lack of) other vegetation nearby

(k) conservation significance/health/longevity of the vegetation

(l) imminent risk to human life or property

(m) the likelihood of a viable and sustainable replacement planting being able to be installed

on the site.

Refer to our Tree Management Policy and Standards for report requirements.

#### 5.3.7 Non-justifiable reasons to remove a tree

The following issues typically do not warrant removal/pruning of prescribed trees:

(a) the tree is shedding leaves fruit, bark, cones, small branches or deadwood

(b) the tree is causing minor damage to structures such as footpaths, driveways and small

(<900mm) retaining walls

(c) the tree is causing blockage of pipes unless tree removal is the only viable option to

access for pipe for replacement

(d) the tree is blocking views

(e) the tree is blocking solar panels unless the panels predated the planting of the tree

(f) the tree is causing shading or overshadowing of a building

(g) the tree is habitat for wildlife. Where tree removals are approved, replacement plantings, with appropriate mature dimensions for the tree being provided, may be required as per the conditions of the tree work permit for the application. Council may specify the mature size of a replacement tree, indicate a particular tree species or leave selection up to the applicant. Native tree species are preferred however, weed species (as defined in the Biosecurity Act 2015), palms and tree species listed in Appendix 2 – Exempt trees species and tree works are not acceptable replacement tree

species. Where it is determined by Council that a tree cannot be replaced in either the same location or anywhere within the property, a financial contribution will be required to support public tree planting and increase the tree canopy throughout Blacktown City. Offset fees are listed within our Goods and Services Pricing Schedule.

(i) Development application approvals New development must retain as many mature trees as possible to protect the existing canopy cover and provide for more liveable environments for residents.

At the concept and design stage the layout and siting of a development must consider the location of existing trees and vegetation and make every effort to protect them. Where a development application proposes removal of trees and/or vegetation, justification must be provided. The siting and design of any new development, including any sub-surface services, is to

consider:

(a) the effect of that development on the landscape or scenic quality of the locality

(b) whether any trees or other vegetation on the land could be preserved

(c) the proposed location of any new trees to be planted to ensure they are viable and

sustainable and provide long-term canopy cover in the area

(d) where trees are removed from a site for development, the proposed landscape plan must

provide replacement plantings that will, at maturity, provide an equivalent area of canopy

cover to that which was removed.  Where this is not viable or sustainable, Council may

require payment of a fee to support public tree planting. Offset fees are contained within

our Goods and Services Pricing Schedule

(e) any tree species, proposed for planting within a landscape, must be assessed to ensure it

is compatible with local environmental conditions and the desired landscape character,

and

(f) the provision of deep soil zones for trees to be retained as well as trees to be planted.

Deep soil areas are, at a minimum, to comply with those published by the New South

Wales Department of Planning, Housing and Infrastructure and Environment or Council’s

specifications.

Where trees and/or vegetation are on the Register of Significant Trees and Vegetation any proposed development must aim to integrate the tree with the built form and promote its environmental values. Where a development application proposes the removal of a Community Asset (Street Tree), if approved, Council will specify the number and species of any replacement planting required. Payment of a Tree Bond, in accordance with the Goods and Services Pricing Schedule, will be required. Tree Protection Measures for Community Asset (Street Trees) will also be subject to payment of the Tree Bond. All efforts must be made to preserve hollow bearing trees, as they provide habitat for a range of native fauna. Many native species of animals and birds are dependent on hollows for breeding and/or protection from predators. As naturally occurring hollows in native hardwood trees sometimes take over a century to form, they are critical to biodiversity in the area. Further, information in regard to trees located on development sites can be found in Councils Tree Management Standards.
