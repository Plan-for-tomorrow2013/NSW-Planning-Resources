## 5. External design

The external layout of a centre must allow children to engage in healthy developmental play under the unobstructed supervision by staff. It is important that the design of outdoor areas provides:

(a) shade and undercover areas for sun protection and usable play space during wet weather.

(b) child safe fencing on all sides to create a safe environment for children and to minimise adverse impacts on the amenity of surrounding properties

(c) outdoor storage that does not  encroach on the play area or impede supervision

(d) direct access to the main indoor play areas and to toilet facilities

(e) a large sandpit that provides 0.5 sqm per child. The sandpit must be a minimum of 12 sqm in size and have a minimum depth of 0.6 m

(f) safe play spaces for children that are

(i) at ground level

(ii) away from the main entrance of the child care centre, car parking areas, vehicle circulation areas  and habitable rooms of surrounding dwellings (i.e. living room and bedroom windows)

The architect or designer certifying the proposal must address the specific requirements below.

### 5.1. Minimum requirements for outdoor play space

Outdoor play areas must:

(a) include a minimum of 7 sqm of play space per child

(b) be located north or north-east of the building

(c) allow at least 50% of play space to be shaded from summer sun:

(i) Shade must comply with the NSW Cancer Council’s Shade for Child Care Services guideline

(ii) Shading may be by vegetation, structures or play equipment.

(d) have unobstructed staff supervision at all times. This may require careful selection of screening vegetation, and design solutions that provide unobstructed visual supervision from inside the building to the outdoor areas.

(e) relate directly to the main indoor play areas and have direct access to the toilets.

### 5.2. Vegetation and landscaping

All centres must have landscaping that is suitable for children and will preserve the privacy and amenity of adjoining residential properties. Landscaping must be established prior to operation of the child care centre commencing. As a guide, the landscape design should have consideration to:

• how the design complements the building and the streetscape, and provide screening for car parking and outdoor play space

• incorporating learning and educational opportunities, such as flower beds and vegetable gardens

• providing outdoor spaces used by children with shade in summer and solar access in winter

• providing suitable screen planting in the side and rear setbacks must include suitable screen planting to protect visual privacy and reduce light and noise impacts on adjoining properties.

• incorporating vegetation that is non-allergenic and sensitive to water restrictions

• providing minimum soil depth of 06m for outdoor space and landscaped areas above basement parking areas.

#### Design

Landscaping for child care centres in residential areas must:

(a) include at least 2 m of the front setback and 1 m of the side and rear setbacks

(b) complement the existing character of the local area.

(c) preserve the privacy and amenity of adjoining residences

(d) include noise abatement measures such as mounding and fencing  (See 2.5 Fencing and gates of this guideline.)

(e) be around the boundaries of the play area to act as a buffer and to enhance the aesthetic quality of the space.

(f) retain existing features of the site, including trees and vegetation

(g) be functional, educational and visually pleasing.

#### Plant species

Plant species must be suitable to the site, easily maintained and interesting to children. When assessing plant species, consider:

(a) protection from prevailing winds

(b) screening for privacy

(c) shelter and enclosure

(d) shade in outdoor play areas

(e) reduction of reflection from bright surfaces

(f) emphasis of pedestrian and vehicular routes

(g) visibility of play areas

(h) plants that may be toxic, allergenic, prickly or otherwise unsafe are prohibited

#### Landscape plans

All applications must include a landscape plan prepared by a landscape architect or designer. The landscape plan must show:

(a) existing vegetation, plant species and trees that are to be retained or removed

(b) noise abatement measures

(c) fencing and gates

(d) shade structures

(e) structures for play and storage

(f) design solutions that provide unobstructed supervision of children by staff Additional documentation such as a flora and fauna report or arborist’s report may be required. This must be discussed with Council before lodging an application. Note: development consent is required under Blacktown Local Environmental Plan 2015 for tree removal or any lopping/topping of trees.

### 5.3. Outdoor play areas

Outdoor play areas must include:

(a) a variety of surfaces that provide an engaging environment for children, such as grass, sand, hard paving and mounding.

(i) All surface materials must have suitable heat absorption qualities and texture to create comfortable and safe play areas in all conditions. (For example surfaces such as bitumen are unsuitable due to surface temperatures exceeding 45°C in summer.)

(ii) All surfaces must be of an acceptable standard to suit the needs of children and comply with the requirements of Australian and New Zealand Standard AS/NZS 4422:1996 Playground Surfacing – Specifications, requirements and test method.

(b) an open flat area for running, active play and mobile equipment

(c) flat grassed play areas

(d) quiet play areas

(e) suitable play equipment with impact absorbent material beneath

(f) flat soft areas for babies

(g) paved surfaces suitable for safe use of wheeled toys

(h) secure fencing (fencing provision should be discussed with Council at DA stage)

(i) a large sandpit, which must:

(i) be a minimum size of 12 sqm (or 0.5 sqm per child) with a minimum depth of 0.6 m.

(ii) have a cover to prevent use by animals and protect it from debris

(iii) be suitably drained

(iv) be suitably shaded

(v) have suitable space for supervision

### 5.4. Play equipment

(a) Outdoor equipment must comply with Australian Standards, including AS1924 Playground Equipment for Parks, Schools and Domestic Use and AS2155 Playgrounds - Guide to siting and to installation and maintenance of equipment.

(b) Play equipment such as cubby houses, trampolines and slides must not overlook neighbours’ properties or impact on the privacy of adjoining residential properties.

(c) Shade structures must not:

(i) be in elevated positions close to boundary fences

(ii) cause overshadowing or visually impact adjoining residential properties.

(d) Play equipment must not constitute a hazard. Height, rough edges, stability and quality of equipment must be taken into consideration.

### 5.5. Fencing and gates

Outdoor play areas and parking areas must have child safe fencing and gates.  This creates a safe environment for children and to minimises impacts on the surrounding area. Fencing and gates must:

(a) be 1.8 m high for outdoor play areas and parking areas

(b) be designed based on the acoustic report

(c) be made from noise abating materials as recommended in the acoustic report. Materials such as lapped and capped timber, brick or masonry are recommended. We discourage use of colorbond.

(d) not allow any person or child to climb over or crawl under them

(e) not be close to play equipment if the equipment will reduce the effective height of the fence or enable the fence to climbed over.

(f) have child proof self-locking mechanisms

(g) have landscaping that enhances the aesthetic quality of the space and acts as a buffer.

### 5.6. Storage facilities

Outdoor storerooms must be provided for the safekeeping outdoor equipment. Storage facilities must:

(a) comply with the Education and Care Services National Regulation

(b) be located so that the movement and storage of equipment does not adversely affect the amenity of surrounding residential properties .

(c) have appropriate access and entry points; preferably from an inward facing direction to the site

(d) be setback a minimum of 3 m from property boundaries. Council may consider proposals for storage facilities to be setback in line with the main building if it is demonstrated to Council’s satisfaction that the storage facilities will not adversely impact on the amenity of adjoining residential properties.
