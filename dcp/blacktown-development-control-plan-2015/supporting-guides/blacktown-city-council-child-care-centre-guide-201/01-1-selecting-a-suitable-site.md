## 1. Selecting a suitable site

### 1.1. Location

Child care facilities are encouraged on sites that are close to:

(a) existing education, open space or community facilities

(b) public transport services

(c) local shops, medical centres or major employment areas Areas that have high levels of visual surveillance for security are considered to be suitable for child care facilities It is preferred that child care facilities are not located on sites:

(a) adjacent to a classified road, roundabout, T-intersection, traffic light intersection, dangerous bend, shopping centre driveway, or pedestrian laneway

(b) on roads that end in a cul-de-sac or dead-end

(c) in close proximity to approved or existing sex premises

(d) that are battle-axe allotments or where the slope of the land will impact on the provision of level outdoor play areas or impede access for people with a disability

(e) where cut/fill or retaining walls will be required for building platforms or drainage

(f) where there may be risk to children, visitors or staff including land that is flood liable, affected by local overland flooding, or bushfire prone

(g) affected by noise, odour, contamination, or other potentially offensive substances

(h) in close proximity to a railway corridor, existing or approved service station, transmission lines, or any other potentially hazardous land uses

Development Applications for child care on sites in close proximity to non-residential land uses must show how the design incorporates suitable measures to minimise health and safety risks created by any rural, commercial, industrial or potentially hazardous activities on adjoining land.
