## 7. Safety provisions

The information below is a guide only. Applicants must ensure that the building complies with the Building Code of Australia and all relevant legislation, regulations and Australian Standards. Fire safety within the building must satisfy the requirements of the Building Code of Australia for a Class 9 building. All fire safety details must be submitted with the Construction Certificate application.

### 7.1. Exits

All child care centres must have clearly defined exits and paths of travel for emergencies. Specific requirements for exit systems are in the Building Code of Australia, however the following must also be considered:

(a) The design of the exit system must allow for the:

(i) limitations of the all users (children and adults) of the childcare centre

(ii) tendency for people to exit the building via the door by which they entered

(b) At least 2 exits must be available and remain clear of obstructions at all times

(c) The door latch must not require the use of a key from the inside of the building

(d) The exit door must open by a single-handed action on a single device located between 0.9m and 1.2m from the floor

(e) Padlocks or the like are prohibited on exit doors

(f) Doors that swing against the direction of exit must have a device that can hold them completely open

(g) Any sliding doors must be easily operated and the direction of operation must be shown on the door (e.g. by an arrow). It is strongly recommended that sliding door tracks at floor level are recessed to prevent tripping.

### 7.2. Early warning systems

All child care centres must have early warning systems in place in the event of a fire or emergency, including:

(a) smoke detectors that comply with the appropriate Australian Standards. It is recommended that smoke detectors that are hard wired into the mains power system, with back-up battery to ensure uninterrupted operation

(b) a regular program of checking and maintenance of smoke detectors and batteries

(c) an evacuation or response plan.  This must be implemented when the alarm sounds, with specified staff members assuming pre-determined roles and ensuring a safe and orderly evacuation. The evacuation plan must be supported by staff training in how to act in an emergency, and regular evacuation drills. Further information may be obtained from Australian Standard AS3745 Emergency Control Organisations and Procedures for Buildings, or by contacting the NSW Fire Brigades. Emergency lighting and warning system specifics are in the Building Code of Australia.

### 7.3. Exit signs and emergency lights

Emergency lighting and exit signs must be installed to show paths of travel to exit points for the safe and quick evacuation from the building in an emergency.   They must comply with the Building Code of Australia based on area and users.

### 7.4. Fire-fighting and protection

Fire protection measures primarily involve equipment or installations to extinguish small fires or prevent the escalation of a fire. Fire-fighting measures must comply with the Building Code of Australia and equipment must be tested every 6 months under Australian Standard 2444 and Australian Standard 1851.1 for Level 1 service. It is strongly recommended that the location and type of fire-fighting facilities be discussed with Council before installation.  As a general guide however, all centres must have:

(a) appropriately located fire detectors

(b) a fire blanket kept adjacent to cooking facilities

(c) appropriately located fire extinguishers that are:

(i) installed on wall brackets near the exit door of the room they protect or outside the door if the room contains hazardous items or activities.

(ii) suitable for the hazard they are required to combat. (For example water extinguishers or hose reels are unsuitable for electrical or burning liquid fires. Also carbon dioxide extinguishers are of limited use on fires of large amounts of paper or similar products.)

(iii) regularly checked and serviced. If the seals are broken on an extinguisher or the extinguisher has been used it must be serviced immediately to ensure it is available for use in any future emergency.

(d) a regular program of maintenance so that fire equipment is kept in proper working condition

(e) furniture, floor coverings, furnishings and decorating materials that comply with specification

C1.10 of the Building Code of Australia.

### 7.5. First aid

All centres must have:

(a) a suitable and fully stocked first-aid kit that is

(i) approved by the WorkCover Authority in line with the relevant Acts and regulations, i.e. Occupational Health and Safety Act 2000.

(ii) kept in a locked cupboard, the key to which must be immediately adjacent to the cupboard and readily accessible to staff.

(b) a resuscitation flow chart that is displayed in a prominent position.
