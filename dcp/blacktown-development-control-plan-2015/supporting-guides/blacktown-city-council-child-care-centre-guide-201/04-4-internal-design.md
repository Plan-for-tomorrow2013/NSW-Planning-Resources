## 4. Internal design

The internal layout of a child care centre must:

• encourage proper maintenance

• reduce damage

• increase operational efficiency

• improve the safety of all users. Consideration should be given to items such as:

• locking devices

• location of power points

• relationship between rooms

• location and type of storerooms

• the elimination of unnecessary projections

• details that will improve the usability and safety of internal spaces. The architect or designer certifying the proposal must address the requirements below.

### 4.1. Playrooms

Playrooms must provide safe and highly functional play areas for children. The design of playrooms should incorporate:

(a) flexibility to reorganise play spaces for different activities using alcoves or relocatable shelving

(b) space for supervised private areas to allow small groups of children to carry out their own activities

(c) suitable flooring and soft areas to accommodate child needs and behaviours

(d) suitable areas for children to eat and drink

(e) easy access for each child, which does not impinge upon exits

(f) clear safety glass panelling on doors, windows and observation frames in interior walls to allow visual supervision to all parts of the building used by children including outdoor space

(g) windows and doors that enable children to see outdoors from play areas (for example by having some window sills at 500mm above the floor)

(h) smooth and level transition areas between indoor and outdoor play spaces, and toilet facilities. This will prevent trip points and unnecessary steps

(i) direct access from all indoor play spaces to toilet facilities and outdoor play areas.

(j) clear access between functional areas such as kitchens and laundries that does not cross play space.

(k) clerestory windows (where possible) to:

• allow warm air to escape in summer

• provide passive solar heating in winter.

### 4.2. Sleep rooms

Child care centres with places for children under 2 years old must provide a suitable sleep room, separate from the playroom. Sleep rooms must:

(a) contain an adequate number of cots, beds, stretchers or sleeping mats (together with waterproof covers) or other culturally appropriate forms of bedding for all children who sleep while on the premises

(b) be well ventilated

(c) not contain more than 8 cots

(d) separate cots a minimum of 0.8 m from points of access and pathways

(e) be accessible to staff at all times

(f) have unrestricted viewing windows to ensure supervision of children at all times.

### 4.3. Craft preparation facilities

Craft preparation facilities must:

(a) be in each playroom

(b) be away from food preparation areas and nappy change areas

(c) include a sink, a bench top and a lockable storage cupboard that is only for craft materials.

### 4.4. Food preparation areas

All food preparation areas must be safe and appropriate for the preparation and cooking of food, washing up and cleaning. These areas must be inaccessible to children. The design of these areas must comply with the:

• Food Act 2003 and regulations

• Australian Standard AS 4674-2004 Design, Construction and Fit-out of Food Premises.

### 4.5. Bottle preparation area

Child care centres with places for children under 3 years old must provide suitable facilities for bottle preparation and sterilisation. This area must:

(a) be in a separate enclosed area that is inaccessible to children

(b) allow for supervision of play areas by staff

(c) be separate from the nappy changing area

(d) have refrigeration facilities for infant formulae, milk and food

(e) have a dedicated cupboard for detergents and other potentially harmful substances.  The cupboard must be inaccessible to children and equipped with child proof locks.

### 4.6. Toilet and washing facilities

All child care centres must have toilet, hand washing and bathing facilities that are safe and appropriate to the ages of the children. It is strongly recommended that toilet facilities are provided at a rate of 1 toilet per 8 children, irrespective of age. Under the Building Code of Australia, child care centres (Class 9b buildings) must have toilet facilities at a minimum rate of:

• 1 junior toilet (or 1 adult toilet with a firm step and a junior seat) per 15 children

• 1 child size hand basin (or 1 adult size hand basin with a firm step) per 15 children. These areas must have:

(a) storage for cleaning products and equipment. This storage must be inaccessible to children and equipped with child proof locks.

(b) direct access from sleeping rooms or a nappy change area

(c) clear and direct access from indoor and outdoor play areas

(d) washable, non-slip flooring graded to a floor waste

(e) child size hand basins or water troughs, mounted between 570mm and 600mm above floor height

(f) at least 1 tap accessible and suitable for children with a disability. General design and construction standards for fixtures and fittings for disabled persons are in Australian Standard AS1428, particularly AS1428.3 - Requirements for Children and Adolescents With Physical Disabilities)

(g) at least 1 soap dispenser for each wash basin, We recommend liquid soap dispensers over the basin.

(h) hand drying facilities. We recommend paper towels with a bin.

(i) separate staff toilet facilities for each sex at a minimum rate of:

• 1 toilet and 1 wash basin for 40 males

• 1 toilet and 1 wash basin for 20 females.

### 4.7. Hot water facilities

All hot water facilities must be safe, and appropriate to the ages of the children at the centre.

(a) Hot water from any outlet accessible to children (e.g. bath, basin or shower) must be regulated to keep it below the temperature at which a child can be scalded or at a temperature as prescribed in the regulations, i.e. 43.5°C.

(b) New centres must (where possible) provide hot water using solar panels or other energy efficient technology.

### 4.8. Nappy change facilities

Child care centres with places for children under 2 years old must provide nappy changing facilities. These facilities must:

(a) prevent unsupervised access by children.

(b) be separate from the bottle and food preparation areas

(c) allow unobstructed supervision to adjoining areas of the centre

(d) include a dedicated cupboard for detergents and other potentially harmful substances.  The cupboard must be inaccessible to children and equipped with child proof locks.

(e) have an impervious washable surface, and a washable mat for changing nappies at a minimum rate of 1 per 10 children under 3 years old

(f) have a hand washing basin with elbow/wrist operated taps for staff

(g) include a sluice/contaminated waste disposal unit and sanitary facilities for the storage of soiled nappies pending laundering or disposal

(h) include storage facilities for clean nappies

(i) include facilities for rinsing soiled clothing and nappies, separate to the hand washing area.

### 4.9. Laundry facilities

Child care centres must have laundry arrangements and safe sanitary facilities, either on the premises or through another facility, service or arrangement. Laundry facilities must:

(a) be at least 10 sqm in size

(b) have all cupboards fitted with childproof locks

(c) have washable, non-slip flooring graded to a floor waste

(d) include a separate sluicing facility/contaminated waste disposal unit for nappies

(e) have storage for soiled clothes, linen and nappies pending laundering or disposal.

(f) include at least 1 laundry tub with hot and cold water.

(g) include a safely installed washing machine.

(h) not store flammable liquids.  The vapour may ignite from a spark by an electrical appliance, or by the pilot lamp of a gas water heater.

Food preparation areas must include:

(a) tight fitting doors or shutters to inhibit the initial spread of flame, and reduce the dispersion of smoke

(b) a smoke and heat resistant half clear-glassed door (or other suitable smoke and heat resistant barrier) that prevents entry by children and provides visual supervision to all play areas.

(c) floors, walls and ceilings that are smooth, free of cracks and crevices, and easily cleaned.

(d) floor and wall intersections covered by benches, wash hand basins and similar fixtures.

(e) machinery mounted either on wheels or on plinths 150mm in height

(f) shelving and fittings made from suitable durable materials such as stainless steel and glass

(g) a hand washing basin for personal hygiene.  This is to be supplied with dispensed soap and disposable hand towels or an electric hand dryer.

(h) a separate sink for cleaning equipment and utensils

i. The sink depth must be adequate to immerse the largest piece of washable equipment.

ii. The hot water supplied to the sink must have a minimum temperature of 77°C.

(i) a stove or microwave, a refrigerator, a hot and cold water supply, and suitable disposal facilities.

(j) a dedicated cupboard for detergents and other potentially harmful substances.  The cupboard is to be inaccessible to children and equipped with child proof locks. Food preparation areas must not:

(k) open directly to toilet and washing facilities

(l) be near sleeping areas to reduce any noise impacts
