### 2.11 ACCESS FOR PEOPLE WITH A DISABILITY

People who design, build, own, manage, lease, operate, regulate, and use premises have responsibilities and rights under the Disability Discrimination Act 1992 (DDA). The DDA is a Commonwealth Act which seeks to eliminate bias against people with disabilities and protect their rights. The DDA states that failure to provide equal access is unlawful, unless to do so would impose an unjustifiable hardship.

Objectives

O.01 Ensure that all people within the City of Parramatta are able to:

a) participate in community life; and

b) access all public spaces and premises and utilise all goods, services and facilities provided in these spaces and premises.

O.02 Ensure that applicants are aware that they have obligations under the Disability Discrimination Act 1992.

Controls

C.01 The siting, design and construction of development is to ensure an appropriate level of accessibility, so that all people can enter and use the premises. All new building work is to meet the requirements of the Disability Discrimination Act 1992 (DDA), the relevant Australian Standards and the Building Code of Australia (BCA), where applicable. NOTE: Compliance with the BCA, the Australian Standards and this DCP does not mean that a development will be compliant with the DDA and provide protection against a complaint under the DDA.
