#### 4.9.2.  Desired Future Character

The desired future character for the East Maitland HCA is as follows:

1\. Retention and conservation of the early grid street pattern and town plan established in 1829, with wide streets that terminate at public parks.

2\. Retention and conservation of the natural landform and landscape.

3\. Retention and conservation of the heritage items and contributory buildings that contribute to the historic character of the HCA.

4\. A scale and form of infill development sympathetic to the predominately single storey detached residential character.

5\. Retention of the mixture of building typologies, including residential, commercial, and government and institutional buildings.

6\. Maintain the historic small scale commercial character of Melbourne and Newcastle Streets. Particular attention should be paid to protecting and enhancing the State Heritage Items of Caroline Chisholm Cottage, Smith’s Flour Mill and each item’s associated curtilages.

7\. Retention of the formal street plantings and footpaths in and around William and Banks streets.

8\. Retention of the landscape setting of the major administrative buildings.

9\. Retention of significant landscape features and public infrastructure including cultural plantings, public parks, wide landscaped median strips, bridges, sandstone kerbing/culverts, street trees and gardens.

10\. Improvement of the public domain character to enhance the significance of the HCA.
