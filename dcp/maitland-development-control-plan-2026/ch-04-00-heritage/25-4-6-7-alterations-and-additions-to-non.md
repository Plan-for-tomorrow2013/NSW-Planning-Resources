#### 4.6.7. Alterations and additions to non-residential buildings

#### Objectives

O.69 To retain and conserve contributory commercial and civic buildings. O.70 To ensure alterations and additions to contributory commercial and civic buildings are of an appropriate design that does not adversely impact the significance or character of the building.

O.71 To encourage the removal of unsympathetic features and details on commercial and civic buildings, such as signage.

| PRESCRIPTIVE CONTROLS AND ACCEPTABLE SOLUTIONS |  |
|---|---|
| PC.1 • O.69 • O.70 | Alterations and additions to non-residential buildings in HCAs must respond appropriately to the heritage significance and historic character of the conservation area. |
| PC.2 • O.69 • O.70 | Alterations and additions to commercial and retail buildings that are contributory buildings must be designed based on a demonstrated thorough understanding of the heritage significance of the HCA. |
| PC.3 • O.69 • O.70 | Additions must be set back from the primary façade of the building so that the addition is not prominent when viewed from the public domain. |
| AS.1 • O.69 • O.70 | The design of additions should be contemporary and identifiable from the existing building. |
| AS.2 • O.69 • O.70 | Generous setbacks should be provided between old buildings and new additions, such as by using a glazed section and/or shadow lines and gaps to link the new addition to the existing building. |
| AS.3 • O.69 | The original elements and details of contributory buildings should be retained, especially on front façades. This includes original windows and doors, brick detailing and parapets, original signs, roofs, chimneys and verandahs. |
| PC.4 • O.69 | Open verandahs and balconies of contributory buildings must not be enclosed. |
