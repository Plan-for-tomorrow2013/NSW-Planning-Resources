## 5.5 Flood development controls

The provisions in this section are in addition to the controls in CBDCP 2023 Chapter 2.2 – Flood Risk Management. The flood related development controls in this section are applicable to the flood prone land areas identified in Figure 54. Any development on PMF (Probable Maximum Flood) affected lots must address the requirements of this section and Chapter 2.2.

### Objectives

**O1.** To establish clear requirements for any shelter-in-place or evacuation provisions for future development in flood prone land areas of the Belmore and Lakemba Precinct.

**O2.** To ensure that the future developments within or adjacent to floodways or overland flow paths (OLFPs) have detailed hydraulic assessment and design.

**O3.** To ensure that the development demonstrates maintenance of sufficient conveyance space for floodwaters.

**O4.** To ensure the development will incorporate design responses such as OLFP redirection or site-specific flow management strategies.

**O5.** To ensure flood risk is managed effectively particularly in areas subject to high hazard, flood emergency response limitations or development intensification.

> See Figure 54. PMF affectation within the Precinct — a map identifying PMF affected lots by category: significant impact (Spots 1, 5 and 8), floodways, and storage.

### Principles

**P1.** Avoid obstruction of the waterway by proposed development.

**P2.** Prevent adverse impacts on neighbouring properties or flood paths.

### Controls

**C1.** Development on flood prone land must address shelter-in-place guidelines (NSW Government, 2025) to manage flood risk.

### 5.5.1 Site specific flooding controls

**C1.** The affected sites located at Spot 1 are significantly affected (refer Figure 54) for the location where the flood prone land flood durations exceed 6 hours and a H5 hazard is present. Ensure that these sites are only relied upon for shelter-in-place (SIP) if the design responses address the following requirements.

**C2.** For any intensification development must maintain or replace flood storage volume and preserve overland flow path function consistent with pre-development conditions.

**C3.** For developments marked in Spot 8, the following requirements must be addressed:

  a) No SIP and evacuation from McDonald Street.

  b) Rear evacuation opportunities from Belleview Avenue or Croydon Street is subject to discussion with Council.

### 5.5.2 Emergency Response Key Areas

**C1.** Applicants seeking to redevelop within Spot 1, Spot 5 and Spot 8 must hold a pre-lodgement meeting with Council to demonstrate how the future residents on these sites can safely SIP.

**C2.** Ensure proposed development at Spot 1 and Spot 5 demonstrates robust design measures and integration with emergency response planning that includes consultation with the NSW State Emergency Service prior to the lodgment of a Development Application (DA) with Council.

> Note: Emergency Response Key Areas are locations where development may be significantly constrained due to: (a) Lack of viable evacuation routes; (b) Long-duration flooding of evacuation route (>12 hours); (c) High flood prone land hazard (H6 hazard affected sites).

### 5.5.3 Shelter-in-Place and Emergency Response Design

#### Principles

**P1.** For flood prone land affected lots shown on Figure 54, evacuation may not be reliably achieved due to flash flooding. Development should include provision for SIP.

#### Controls

**C1.** To ensure SIP spaces in new developments are designed appropriately, the NSW Government Shelter-in-place Guideline (2025) sets out 10 design criteria as listed in Table 11. Any DA that proposes Shelter-in-place must demonstrate compliance with Design Criteria 1–3. Design Criteria 4–10 must be considered by Council when setting conditions of consent.

**C2.** Council will not support the reliance on SIP for sites that are subject to long-duration of flash flooding which is greater than 12 hours.

### Table 11. Shelter-in-place design criteria

| ID | SIP Guideline (2025) Design Criteria Requirement |
|---|---|
| 1 | The floor level of the SIP should be part of the development be above the PMF. |
| 2 | Structural soundness for conditions in a PMF event, considering flood and debris forces, be verified by a suitably qualified structural engineer. |
| 3 | Area and access to the area does not rely on access to electricity and is self-directing. Have clearly marked internal access for all people on site, including consideration of access for potential occupants and/or visitors. |
| 4 | Protection from weather and appropriate heating and cooling. |
| 5 | Access to personal hygiene facilities such as toilet. |
| 6 | A minimum floor space of 2 m² per person. |
| 7 | Items for self-sufficiency that are stored, maintained and are regularly updated in an accessible location above the PMF, including sufficient drinking water and food for occupants, fire extinguishers, radios and torches with spare batteries, and a first aid kit with an automated external defibrillator (AED). |
| 8 | Centralised communal shelters may be considered but must be freely accessible internally at all times and externally accessible during events. |
| 9 | Access is provided to on-site systems that generate power the shelter-in-place location during and after the event for a full range of flood events up to the PMF. |
| 10 | Includes signage, communication or design elements to support sheltering. |

*Figures reference: Good Design Guide for Flood Affected Areas.*

### 5.5.4 Basement flood risk management

#### Objectives

**O1.** To ensure for the utilisation of design measures including elevated driveways, overland flow redirection, or waterproofing design for best practice flood management.

**O2.** To ensure basements in new developments on flood prone land affected lots are designed to preserve the safety of the occupants during a flood prone land event.

#### Controls

**C1.** Flood waters should be prevented from entering basements by using the Flood Planning Level (1% AEP + 500 mm), or the flood prone land level, whichever is greater, to set the apex level.

**C2.** Hydraulic analysis and/or water surface level checks for all proposed basement entries in flood-affected areas are required.

> See Figure 55. Essential services must be located above FPL. See Figure 56. An apex above the flood level prevents water from entering a basement car park. *Figures reference: Good Design Guide for Flood Affected Areas.*

### 5.5.5 Fill design considerations and compliance with council policy

#### Objectives

**O1.** To prevent future developments from building in Overland Flow Paths (OLFPs).

#### Principles

**P1.** Ensure that the sites that fall within OLFP have post development flood modelling, to demonstrate that flood conveyance is retained.

**P2.** Ensure the built form in areas within OLFP are designed to either maintain open flow corridors or incorporate suspended structures that allow floodwaters to pass beneath the development footprint.

**P3.** Council may support a diversion of an overland flow path within the property boundary, provided it can be demonstrated that the flow is not impeded and there are no adverse off-site impacts.

### 5.5.6 Car Parking

#### Controls

**C1.** Ensure entries to underground car parking are at, or above the Flood Planning Level (1% AEP + 500mm) or the flood prone land level, whichever is greater.

### 5.5.7 Management Plan

#### Controls

**C1.** Where finished floor levels or basement car park entries are below the flood prone land level for the site, Council will require the applicant prepare a Flood Risk Management Plan and for this to be registered on the land title as a condition of development consent. The Flood Risk Management Plan must address the following requirements:

  a) Site-specific flood risks

  b) An operational risk assessment

  c) Maintenance and reporting schedule.

  d) Implement a flood-risk management plan which has been approved by Council.
