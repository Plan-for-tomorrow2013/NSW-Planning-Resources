## 4.3 Building interface

### 4.3.1 Residential ground floor

#### Objectives

**O1.** To establish new canopy trees within setbacks that contribute to the character of the street and provide residents amenity and privacy.

**O2.** To provide ownership and identity to residents by providing individual entries along the ground floor frontage out to the public domain.

**O3.** To provide for surveillance of public and publicly accessible space.

**O4.** To encourage detailed attention to the design and to selection of materials at the lower level of residential buildings.

> See Figure 49. Key design guidance for the residential ground floor frontage — four illustrated principles: deep soil and vegetation for tree canopy and landscape buffers; ground entrances have direct access to public space; ground level apartments provide passive surveillance; servicing and basement access screened and inset behind setbacks.

#### Principles

**P1.** Provide individual courtyards to ground floor apartments.

**P2.** Existing and proposed tree canopy should be contained within front setbacks to provide residents amenity and privacy and to offer greenery to the public domain.

#### Controls

**C1.** A minimum of 3 metres depth of deep soil shall be allocated from the front boundary. Canopy trees must be planted in this area 2 metres away from the front building line to achieve at least 8 metres mature height and spread, at the rate of 1 canopy tree for every 8 lineal metres of frontage. The number of trees to be planted will be rounded up to the next whole number.

**C2.** The first tree will be planted at the intersection of the front and side boundary. The medium to large sized trees will be spaced and planted from this origin point.

**C3.** All ground floor apartments shall have individual entries and individual courtyard for private open space.

### 4.3.2 Active street frontages and edges

Active street frontages are continuous retail and business uses with frequent openings that engage with the street and public space, creating a lively, welcoming environment that encourages pedestrian activity. To create a human scale, active street frontages should feature a fine grain of narrow shopfronts with services and utilities positioned away from the primary frontage.

Active edges are incidental active uses along a frontage. While secondary to active street frontages, they contribute positively to the public domain. For example, Canterbury Road would primarily include business uses that will have less foot traffic but still provide occasional active use to service workers and pedestrians along this street. In residential areas, an active edge could include a café or corner shop, on a lane, pedestrian link or open space, with the remainder of the ground floor frontage being garden apartments with individual entries.

Active uses typically include retail, customer service areas, cafes, restaurants and outdoor dining areas.

To enhance the pedestrian comfort and encourage street life, active street frontages must incorporate continuous awnings. Awnings should also be provided along active edges, where possible, although they do not need to extend along the entire length of the frontage.

#### Objectives

**O1.** To encourage finer grained narrow frontages to create varied experiences that provide views into and out of shops.

**O2.** To ensure that Haldon Street and Burwood Road have a high quality and engaging public realm.

**O3.** To ensure that the interface and amenity of Canterbury Road is improved for pedestrians.

**O4.** To ensure frontages along key streets, pedestrian links, lanes and open spaces provide both active and passive surveillance.

**O5.** To encourage people to linger and enjoy public space.

**O6.** To ensure that building services do not dominate the pedestrian experience and are designed as an integrated part of the building form.

**O7.** To ensure ground floor active street frontage and edges are pedestrian oriented, have a high standard of design and add vitality to streets.

**O8.** To create opportunities to enjoy the positive aspects of climate and shield people from the negative such as wind, rain and heat.

**O9.** To provide secondary active edges that create vitality and interest on frontages with less foot traffic.

> See Figure 50. Design guidance for active frontages along key streets — four illustrated principles: active uses (glazed shop fronts with openings, outdoor dining and market stalls); fine grain for visual interest, human scale and varied experiences; active and passive surveillance provides 'eyes on the street'; continuous awnings for shelter and protection.

#### Principles

**P1.** Vehicle entries are located away from key pedestrian areas to ensure for safety.

**P2.** Awnings are to be used along active street frontages and edges to provide shelter and amenity.

**P3.** Shopfronts utilise lighting in the evenings for safety when people are using public space.

#### Controls

**C1.** The location of active street frontages and active edges are shown on Figure 51.

**C2.** Vehicle access and loading is to be located away from primary pedestrian areas.

**C3.** Extend the concrete structural frame to form the awning at street level to shield pedestrians on active street frontages.

**C4.** Awnings are to be provided to active frontages and extend 3m over the footway to provide protection from inclement weather and hot sun. Awnings must:

  a) Integrate with the built form;

  b) Not be double height;

  c) Be non-reflective and not glazed; and

  d) Be harmonious with adjacent awnings and gutters and ensure downpipes and lighting are recessed and integrated with the building.

**C5.** Awnings should be provided to active edges wherever possible.

### Table 5. Active street frontage design guidance

| Requirement | Guidance |
|---|---|
| Minimum active street frontage required for each frontage facing the public domain | 60% of the width of each public domain frontage |
| Active uses facing the public domain | Entries or display windows to shops and/or food and drink premises or other uses, customer service areas and activities which provide pedestrian interest and interaction |
| Minimum 'grain' of shop front tenancies | 8 tenancy entries per 100m. Where a single or larger tenancy is proposed with a single entrance, the facade must give the appearance of being visually broken up into finer grain tenancies and allow for separate tenancy entrances in the future |
| Provision of awnings | Awnings are to be provided to all active frontages |
