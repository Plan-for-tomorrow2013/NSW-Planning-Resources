## 4.5 Material and finishes

Buildings in our cities and places need to be composed of low maintenance, robust and quality materials that will last the test of time for sustainability, beauty and to reinforce a sense of place.

### Objectives

**O1.** To ensure high-quality materials and finishes are used throughout new development, with an emphasis on the ground floor, to enhance the pedestrian experience.

**O2.** To ensure material selection and finishes are robust, have longevity, are low maintenance and contribute to beautiful buildings.

**O3.** To ensure new development is compatible with the character and heritage of Belmore and Lakemba.

### Principles

**P1.** Primarily use face brickwork, masonry and express the concrete and brick structure on facades.

**P2.** Have a higher ratio of solid to void in facades in response to heritage, where appropriate.

**P3.** Insert fine detailing through the use of timber, copper and metal.

**P4.** Create simplicity through the selection of a common language of materials and finishes, rather than selecting many and creating visual complexity.

### Controls

**C1.** Buildings must include durable single finish materials such as masonry and face brick. The concrete structure of buildings should form part of the architecture and be expressed in facades.

**C2.** Painted cement render or similar painted finishes and Aluminium Composite Panel Cladding (e.g. Alucobond) should not be used. The use of glazing is not supported for awning construction or for parapet edges on rooftops.

**C3.** Elevate the pedestrian experience at street level through the use of textured materials and fine grained detailing using timber, textured brickwork and stone.
