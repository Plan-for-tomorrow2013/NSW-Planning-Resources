## 3.1 Public domain strategy

The public domain consists of streets, squares, parks, shared lanes and pedestrian links. These elements, in conjunction with the built form interface to private land, play a vital role in creating unique, vibrant and sustainable places that are attractive, inviting and inclusive for all.

The public domain strategy ensures housing growth is supported by the delivery of high-quality public spaces that encourage walking, cycling, community interaction and engagement.

This section outlines key opportunities for enhancing the public domain in the Belmore and Lakemba Precinct, as identified in Figure 23. Public domain strategy.

> See Figure 22. Designing the public domain (four-part diagram: variety of public spaces; permeable connected network encouraging walking, cycling and public transport use; tree canopy and landscaping for cool, attractive places that promote biodiversity; solar access, shade, surveillance and activation of the public domain).

### Objectives

**O1.** To improve the quality, appearance and function of the public domain.

**O2.** Provide a diverse range of quality public spaces to support residents, workers and visitors.

**O3.** Ensure a high level of permeability through the Precinct.

**O4.** Provide high quality, functional and attractive formal and informal spaces for community interaction.

**O5.** To establish a vibrant and attractive Precinct where the network of streets, shared lanes and open spaces support the increase in population density, the increased pedestrian movement and footpath trading activities.

### Principles

**P1.** Development must contribute to the delivery of public domain improvements in line with the Public Domain Strategy at Figure 23 including:

  a) **Haldon Street transformation:** Prioritise pedestrian movement on Haldon Street (between The Boulevarde and Oneata Street) building on the success of temporary road closures during the Lakemba Night Markets. Enhance surrounding streets and laneways to support a safe and walkable network by providing lighting on building facades, locating entries to buildings away from congested areas where relevant, and providing simple and legible signage.

  b) **Burwood Road revitalisation:** Re-balance Burwood Road to be more pedestrian friendly with traffic calming measures, urban greening and a safe and walkable lanes.

  c) **New open space and consolidated community facilities at the Quigg Street key site:** Create a new open space and community facility near Haldon Street, supported by a site-specific master plan, and connected via an activated pedestrian link to Haldon Street.

  d) **Connecting sports, recreation and health:** Improve access and interfaces to the Belmore Sports and Recreation Precinct. Improve connectivity between major facilities and attractions including Canterbury Hospital, Canterbury Leagues Club, Burwood Road, bus stops and Metro stations. See Figure 24 for indicative high level plan.

  e) **Greening Wangee Road:** Enhance tree canopy to support pedestrians and cyclists connections along Wangee Road linking to key community attractors, including Lakemba Mosque, local schools and Parry Park.

  f) **Leylands Parade - a green street:** Preserve and enhance the distinctive landscape character of Leylands Parade by completing the tree canopy between Belmore Sports and Recreation Precinct and an expanded Marie Street Reserve. Carefully manage traffic flow with pedestrian safety and amenity along this street.

  g) **Active transport network expansion:** Extend the active transport network initiated by the South West Metro active transport corridor, with additional infrastructure and pedestrian crossings on Lakemba Street, Wangee Road, Gillies Street, Quigg Street and Redman Parade.

  h) **Open space expansion:** Identify opportunities to widen existing open spaces, including Peel Park, Marie Street Reserve and Jubilee Park and deliver new open spaces through development uplift.

  i) **Pedestrian links:** Provide minimum 4m wide pedestrian links in long blocks to improve mid-block connectivity.

  j) **Lane upgrade:** Expand and improve lanes in E1 and E2 zones for enhanced servicing and improved pedestrian and cycle access.

  k) **Shared use of open spaces:** Encourage shared use arrangements of open space with nearby schools such as Belmore Boys High School, Belmore North Public School and Hampden Park Public School.

**P2.** Ensure that developments adjacent to the Belmore Sports and Recreation Precinct is aligned with the high level plan shown in Figure 24. Developments should be orientated to face the park and provide a positive active interface through entrances, balconies, terraces and high quality landscaping that enhances passive surveillance and amenity.

### Indicative high level precinct plan for Belmore Sports Ground

This indicative plan sets out a vision and key principles to enhance the accessibility, connectivity, functionality and activation of the Belmore Sports and Recreation Precinct. The area will continue to offer formal sporting facilities and fields, while also delivering improved informal green space, play areas and landscaping along with an enhanced pedestrian connection along the railway line to Belmore station.

> See Figure 24. Belmore Sports and Recreation Precinct Plan. Legend covers: existing through site connections; existing pedestrian and cycle underpass; aspirational future connections; potential through site link to support connection between the precinct and Burwood Road; future gateway and arrival points to provide wayfinding; enhanced connection and arrival experience; new proposed open space and new destination playground; active recreation and stormwater basin function; proposed NRL Bulldogs Centre of Excellence; existing grandstand. Numbered callouts: **1** potential future stadium arrival and entrance — flexibility for events, markets; **2** Elite Sports Use — Restricted Public Access (current use by NRL Bulldogs and Sydney Olympic Football Club); **3** community facility within grandstand; **P** limited parking areas to service day-to-day operation of stadium. Also notes future residential development should consider interface with the park, e.g. housing fronting park, landscape treatments, setbacks and building orientation.
