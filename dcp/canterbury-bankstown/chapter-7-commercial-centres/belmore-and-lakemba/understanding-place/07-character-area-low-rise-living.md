# 2.2.5 Low-rise living

The Low-rise living character areas will showcase a diverse range of one and two storey low-rise developments. This includes single dwellings, dual occupancies, terraces, townhouses, and multi-dwelling housing including some low-scale apartments.

Maintaining and celebrating the existing low-scale character of these areas is crucial to ensuring for a cohesive streetscape and protection of low scale historic character. New developments should draw inspiration from the design and materials of existing homes, reflecting the patterns of pitched roofs, face brick and masonry materials and appropriate finishes.

Limiting driveway crossovers through placing them next to each other or in rear laneways will enhance walkability and amenity in the area, contributing to a greener and shadier environment allowing for more landscaping and tree canopy along the streets and in setbacks.

Within this character area, there are two distinctly low-density zones along Dennis Street and Passey/Paxton Avenue, where the existing character should be maintained. The area surrounding Dennis Street Reserve is characterised by largely intact single storey interwar houses. Any developments adjacent to this area should be designed to respect and complement the historic character of the existing dwellings and the overall streetscape.

## Principles

**P1.** Development will maintain and integrate into the existing low-scale character and historic significance of this character area. New development will provide a sensitive transition and ensure that scale, setbacks, separation, landscape character seamlessly integrate the new with older developments. See Section 4.2 The building envelope.

**P2.** Developments are to demonstrate a range of residential options and contribute to an inviting, human-scaled street edge and enhance the landscape character of the area. See Section 4.1 Minimum lot sizes and Section 4.2.1 Building height and storeys.

**P3.** Maintain and draw inspiration from the surrounding design and materials of existing homes, reflecting existing patterns such as pitched roofs, brick and masonry structures, appropriate finishes and maintain the bulk and scale of the surrounding architectural context to create cohesion throughout. See Section 4.5 Materials and finishes.

**P4.** New development in Dennis Street and Passey/Paxton Avenue must consider the surrounding historic inter-war housing character to ensure for a harmonious outcome. The historic housing and character of Dennis Street should be maintained as predominantly intact single storey inter-war houses make up this street.

**P5.** Front setbacks should accommodate deep soil planting and support a continuous tree canopy. Driveways should be limited or consolidated where possible to improve pedestrian safety and maintain green walkable streets. See Chapter 5 Residential Accommodation of the CBDCP 2023 for detail on setback requirements and design for this character area.
