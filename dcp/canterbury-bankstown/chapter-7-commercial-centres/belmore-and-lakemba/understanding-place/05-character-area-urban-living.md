# 2.2.3 Urban living

The Urban living character area will offer architecturally inspiring apartments within slender towers on podiums that create a varied skyline and contribute to a high quality public domain and streetscape.

The area will feature green streets, well-connected pedestrian and cycle links, along with inviting public space. This design aims to cultivate a vibrant, walkable urban neighbourhood conveniently located near transport, services and amenity.

This character area will experience substantial revitalisation, featuring new apartment developments primarily ranging from 9 to 15 storeys with a four to five storey street wall that integrates with the surroundings. Key sites will facilitate high-quality developments that offer additional public benefits, including new publicly accessible open spaces and pedestrian links, enhancing the public domain and connectivity for everyone. Corner sites will be designed to accommodate landmark towers and focal points in the street.

While parts of this neighbourhood have existing residential flat buildings, others consist of detached dwellings. New development will need to consider both contexts, providing appropriate setbacks, landscaping and articulated built form.

New developments will prioritise direct street access and landscaped private open space within setbacks for ground-floor apartments and terraces, encouraging passive surveillance of the streets, pedestrian links and shared lanes. Apartments adjacent to open space will be designed to provide a high quality interface and frontage to parks. Podiums and rooftops will offer opportunities to contribute to urban greening and communal open space for residents.

This neighbourhood benefits from a well-established tree canopy along Leylands Parade, offering shade, biodiversity and a beautiful green streetscape. New tree canopy will be focused along key streets including Wangee Road, Lakemba Street and The Boulevarde. This will contribute to a cool, green and attractive network of streets.

Additionally, proposed cycleways along key streets will offer a safe, convenient and healthy way to navigate the neighbourhood while connecting to the new east-west Sydney Metro pedestrian link.

## Principles

**P1.** New taller developments must be sympathetic to the surrounding context, that includes a mix of detached dwellings, three-storey walk-ups and newer apartment buildings. In areas interfacing with lower-density zones, a sensitive transition is essential. This should be achieved through careful consideration of scale, setbacks, separation, landscaping, and privacy measures. See Section 4.2 The building envelope.

**P2.** Development adjacent to public open space will be orientated to face the park, maximise park outlook from living areas and balconies and create a positive interface that promotes passive surveillance through entrances, balconies, terraces, and high quality landscaping. Development interfacing Belmore Sports and Recreation Precinct will demonstrate consideration of the high level precinct plan in Figure 24. See Section 3.1 Public domain strategy – Indicative high level precinct plan for Belmore Sports Ground.

**P3.** High-quality pedestrian links must be free of obstructions and feature landscaping along their edges. These links should also include adequate lighting and setbacks that accommodate landscaping, as well as garden apartments with direct ground floor entries to enhance passive surveillance. See Section 3.3 Streets, shared lanes, pedestrian links and arcades.

**P4.** Encourage the provision of small scale retail, commercial, food and drink premise uses at street corners and pedestrian links to create active edges. See Section 4.3.2 Active street frontages and edges.

**P5.** Elegant towers set upon podiums will feature facades that respond to orientation, expose concrete structure and are responsive to the context using face brickwork. Podiums will include articulation and breaks to ensure they don't overwhelm the streetscape. See Section 4.2.1 Building height and storeys and 4.4 Visual diversity, articulation and fine grain.

**P6.** The use of brick and masonry will integrate with the existing brick character of the neighbourhood. High quality finishes and textures should be provided at street level. See Section 4.5 Materials and finishes.

**P7.** The landscape character of Leylands Parade, characterised by its majestic Hills Weeping Fig trees, is to be preserved, with an emphasis on extending the tree canopy. Developments interfacing with this street must incorporate landscaping that reflects this natural character and incorporate design principles that consider and protect the existing root system of the trees. Additionally, other identified key streets should strive to emulate this green canopy within setbacks, enhancing the overall greenery of the area. See Section 3.1 Public domain strategy.

**P8.** Development on the Burwood North key site is to provide improved permeability and interfaces to streets and lanes and deliver new open space. See Section 6 Key sites.
