# 2.2.4 Mid-rise living

The Mid-rise living character area will feature a harmonious blend of both new and historic housing, ranging from single detached dwellings and walk-up flats to mid-rise contemporary apartments set within a landscape context.

The character areas will be enhanced by high-quality landscaping and well designed street interfaces. New development will have front setbacks to provide for tree canopy cover and deep soil.

A 4 storey street wall in new developments will establish a lower scale that integrates harmoniously with the character of existing streetscapes. In select areas, additional storeys will be set back to preserve views, privacy, scale and sunlight access. At street level, ground-floor apartments with direct street access and welcoming front courtyards will create engaging frontages.

## Principles

**P1.** Development interfacing with lower density areas must provide a sensitive transition using scale, setbacks, separation, landscaping and privacy measures, to ensure cohesive integration of new and existing development. See Section 4.2 The building envelope.

**P2.** Developments are to demonstrate a range of residential dwelling sizes and typologies, including larger family apartments, townhouse or terrace style dwellings and smaller apartments for a mix of household types. See Section 4.1 Minimum lot sizes and Section 4.2.1 Building height and storeys.

**P3.** In areas where additional levels are permitted these levels are to be setback from the podium to ensure for a consistent street wall. See Section 4.2.3 Street wall and Section 4.2.5 Upper storey setbacks.

**P4.** Developments on streets including the Boulevarde and Lakemba Street are to provide well landscaped front setbacks. See Section 3.1 Public domain strategy and Section 4.2.4 Front setbacks.

**P5.** Development adjacent to public open space will be orientated to face the park, optimise views from living areas and balconies and create a positive interface that promotes passive surveillance through entrances, balconies, terraces, and high quality landscaping. Development interfacing Belmore Sports and Recreation Precinct will demonstrate consideration of the high level precinct plan in Figure 24. See Section 3.1 Public domain strategy – Indicative high level precinct plan for Belmore Sports Ground.

**P6.** High-quality pedestrian links must be free of obstructions and feature landscaping along their edges. These links should also include adequate lighting and setbacks that accommodate landscaping, as well as apartments with direct entries to enhance passive surveillance. See Section 3.3 Streets, shared lanes, pedestrian links and arcades.

**P7.** The use of face brick and masonry in new development will reflect the existing brick character of the neighbourhood. See Section 4.5 Materials and finishes.
