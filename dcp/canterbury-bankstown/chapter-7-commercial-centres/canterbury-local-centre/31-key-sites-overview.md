## 6. Key Sites

### Overview of Key Sites

The following sites have been identified as having a pivotal role in the realisation of the
vision and objectives for the Canterbury Local Centre. The development of the key sites will
be a catalyst for further development within the Local Centre and present an opportunity for
precedent examples of design excellence, Connection to Country and the reflection of
Canterbury's history and heritage through building form and materiality. The development on
key sites will share a common language that is placed based.

To ensure for optimal outcomes for Canterbury and an efficient process for applicants, it is
essential to engage with Council early in the Development Application process. Council will
have an expectation to see detailed analysis of site, context and an understanding of the
Aboriginal and European history of Canterbury.

To ensure for a 'fine grained' approach to key sites, Council encourages the subdivision of
large sites into smaller lots that are contextual to site. If this cannot be achieved, then a
variety of different buildings should be introduced of varying architectural character, scale
and height to provide individual identity for residents and visitors.

### Objectives

**O1.** Ensure that development is unique to the character, climate and demographic of
Canterbury Local Centre.

**O2.** Achieve Design Excellence, create inspirational key sites with high quality innovative
architecture that are sustainably built, reference Canterbury's history and heritage, offer
community benefit, provide new publicly accessible and private spaces that contribute
positively to Canterbury's sense of place and its skyline.

**O3.** Ensure that the principles of Connecting to Country are enshrined and represented in
these sites.

**O4.** Ensure development does not compromise or reduce solar access to parks.

**O5.** Integrate laneways, expand the public domain, improve pedestrian and cyclist
permeability and connect to existing and new open space.

**O6.** Provide for a range of heights on key sites to create an interesting and diverse
skyline.

**O7.** Ensure that the interface of sites activates the public domain and invites the public
into new publicly accessible spaces.

**O8.** Ensure that podiums are articulated to appear as smaller parts to create a positive
fine grained and exciting experiences at street level.

**O9.** Ensure street blocks present as a group of buildings and not as a single building.

**O10.** Ensure for a common language between buildings using materials that are long lasting
and need little maintenance such as masonry, face brick and natural building materials
generally.

**O11.** Retain significant trees and integrate them in the design of new developments.

### General Controls Applicable to All Key Sites

In the event of any inconsistency between this section of this chapter and other parts of
this DCP, these provisions prevail to the extent of any inconsistency.

**C1.** The design and delivery of key sites will take a Country centred approach in
accordance with the guidelines and controls at Section 2.3 of this Chapter.

**C2.** An Aboriginal consultant shall be engaged at the outset of the site and context
analysis and throughout the design process.

**C3.** Where appropriate, refer to section 3.3 for specifications for cycleways, laneways and
arcades.

**C4.** Key sites should provide up to 25% tree canopy and up to 50% green cover. Canterbury
Square shall provide a minimum of 40% tree canopy cover to beautify this space and provide
shade.

**C5.** Key sites will reflect the masonry heritage character of the Canterbury Local Centre
through the choice of materials and finishes in development. Face brickwork and masonry and
natural materials shall be used at a higher solid to void ratio. Contemporary design is
invited using a palette of face brickwork, off-form concrete with timber and copper detailing.
Render, Alucobond, curtain walls and glazed awnings and balustrades are not supported.

**C6.** Building articulation is to minimise different materials and colours and aim for
simplicity not complexity. A response to orientation should be implemented to provide for the
articulation of each façade. This can be achieved by the western facade being more closed and
shaded with vertical blades to control heat, while the northern facade has deep eaves and
horizontal shading and is more open to the sun.

**C7.** To mitigate urban heat, permeable paving will be used in association with understorey
planting with canopy trees. Water features including misting sprays, fountains and design
elements that store water should be provided. Publicly accessible laneways will be shaded as
will arcades.

**C8.** Building facades should show depth and shadow and exciting detailing as shown at 4.5
and 4.6 of this Chapter.
