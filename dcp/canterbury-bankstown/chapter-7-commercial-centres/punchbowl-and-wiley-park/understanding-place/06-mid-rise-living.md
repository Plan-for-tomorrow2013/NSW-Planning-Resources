# Section 2.2.4 — Mid-rise Living (Character Area)

The Mid-rise living character area is spread across the two Village Centres. These areas will
be attractive and well-connected neighbourhoods that deliver a diversity of housing and
provide a safe and comfortable street life reinforced by generous soft landscaping.

The Mid-rise living character area will accommodate significant renewal with a range of
development heights and densities. The greatest densities will be delivered in proximity to
Metro stations and services and on those sites which are appropriately scaled and
orientated to achieve high quality development outcomes.

Parts of this character area already contain residential flat buildings while others include
detached dwellings. New development will need to respond to both contexts, providing
appropriate setbacks, landscaping and articulated built form.

The character area is also characterised by several schools and places of worship that
include St Charbel's Church, Monastery and College, Punchbowl Mosque, St Nicholas
Orthodox Church, St Jerome's Church and School, and a mix of other Churches and
mosques, that form focal points within the street structure. This character area has
convenient connections to Punchbowl Park, Wiley Park Village and to Canterbury Road.

**Built form features shown in Figure 17**: 4 to 6 storey residential apartments; front
setback for trees, landscaping and garden apartments; deep soil, trees and landscaping in
side and rear setbacks; communal open space at ground, on podiums and rooftops.

## Principles

- **P1** Development interfacing lower density areas will provide sensitive transition and ensure scale, setbacks, separation, landscaping, privacy seamlessly integrate the new and old developments with varying densities and scales. See Section 4.2 The building envelope.
- **P2** Developments are to demonstrate a range of residential options including dwellings, walk up flats and mid-rise apartments with an activated and inviting street edge by providing ground floor garden apartments. See Section 4.1 Minimum lot sizes and Section 4.2.1 Building height and storeys.
- **P3** In areas that allow additional levels these are to be setback above the podium to ensure protection of streetscapes. See Section 4.2.3 Street wall and Section 4.2.5 Upper storey setbacks.
- **P4** Developments on priority streets for tree canopy are to provide well landscaped streetscapes and setbacks. See Section 3.1 Public domain strategy and Section 4.2.4 Front setbacks.
- **P5** Development adjacent to public open space, including Wiley Park, will be orientated to face the park, and create a positive interface that promotes passive surveillance through entrances, balconies, terraces and high quality landscaping.
- **P6** High quality pedestrian links must be free of obstructions and feature landscaping along their edges. These links should also include adequate lighting and setbacks that accommodate landscaping as well as apartments with direct entries to enhance passive surveillance. See Section 4.3.1 Residential ground floor.
- **P7** The use of brick and masonry, along with exposed concrete structure in podiums, will reflect the existing brick and masonry character of the neighbourhood and ensure materials are robust over time while providing for cohesion with the surrounding architectural context. See Section 4.5 Materials and finishes.
- **P8** New development adjacent to key places of worship is to foster a sympathetic relationship between the two uses and building form. Key design considerations include appropriate scale, setbacks and landscape buffers to provide a transition, as well as views, pedestrian movement and permeability.
