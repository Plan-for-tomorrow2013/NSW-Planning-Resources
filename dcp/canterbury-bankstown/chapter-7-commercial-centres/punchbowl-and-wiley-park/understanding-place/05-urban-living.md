# Section 2.2.3 — Urban Living (Character Area)

The Urban living character areas will be attractive and have high accessibility. These areas
will deliver a diversity of higher rise housing, providing a vibrant, safe and desirable street
life reinforced by generous soft landscaping and supported by infrastructure.

The area will feature green streets, pedestrian and cycle links, along with inviting public
space. This design aims to cultivate a vibrant, walkable urban neighbourhood conveniently
located near transport, services and amenity.

The Urban living character area will experience significant renewal with a range of
development heights and densities that respond to their context. The greatest densities
will be delivered in proximity to Metro stations and services and on those sites which are
larger in area and orientated to achieve high quality development outcomes.

New developments will prioritise direct street access and landscaped private open space
within setbacks for ground-floor apartments and terraces, encouraging passive surveillance
of the streets, pedestrian links and shared lanes. Apartments adjacent to open space will be
designed to provide a high quality interface and frontage to parks.

Podiums and rooftops will offer opportunities to contribute to urban greening and
communal open space for residents.

Parts of this character area contain existing residential flat buildings while others include
detached dwellings. New development will need to be sensitive to both contexts, providing
appropriate setbacks, landscaping and articulated built form.

**Built form features shown in Figure 15** (indicative building envelope diagram, with
approximate dimensions annotated on the figure): 9 to 15 storey residential apartments;
front setback for trees, landscaping and garden apartments; deep soil, trees and
landscaping in side and rear setbacks; communal open space at ground, on podiums and
rooftops.

## Principles

- **P1** New taller developments must be sympathetic to the surrounding context, which includes detached dwellings, three-storey walk-ups and newer apartment buildings. In areas interfacing with lower-density zones, a sensitive transition is essential. This should be achieved through careful consideration of scale, setbacks, separation, landscaping, and privacy measures. See Section 4.2 The building envelope.
- **P2** Development adjacent to public open space, including Wiley Park, will be orientated to face the park and provide passive surveillance through the implementation of entrances, balconies and terraces.
- **P3** High quality pedestrian links must be free of obstructions and feature landscaping along their edges. These links should also include adequate lighting as well as apartments with direct entries from the public domain to increase activity and enhance passive surveillance. See Section 4.3.1 Residential ground floor.
- **P4** Encourage the provision of small scale retail, commercial, food and drink premises, where permissible, at street corners and through site links to create active edges. See Section 4.3.2 Active street frontages and edges.
- **P5** Slender, elegant towers set upon podiums will exhibit high quality design of form that responds to context including the orientation of facades. Longer podiums will include articulation to ensure they do not overwhelm the streetscape and provide visual interest. High quality materials and finishes should be used at the interface with the public domain. See Section 4.2.1 Building height and storeys and 4.4 Visual diversity, articulation and fine grain.
- **P6** The use of brick and masonry, along with exposed concrete structure in podiums, will reflect the existing brick materiality of the character area, ensuring cohesion with the surrounding architectural context. See Section 4.5 Materials and finishes.
