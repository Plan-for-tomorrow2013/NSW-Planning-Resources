# Section 2.2.5 — Low-rise Living (Character Area)

This character area is generally located at the periphery of the Village Centres and will
accommodate moderate renewal. New development will include single dwellings, dual
occupancies, terraces/townhouses and multi-dwelling housing. The existing quiet
residential character will be retained.

The existing low scale character of these areas will be celebrated and new development
will be sympathetic to the existing scale. The streetscape will be enhanced through
increased landscaping and tree canopy within front setbacks. Driveway crossovers will be
minimised where possible.

New development will draw upon the form and materiality of existing dwellings including
the pattern of pitched roofs, generally face brick and masonry walls and landscape
character. Existing tree planting will be maintained and supplemented with additional
canopy cover.

**Built form features shown in Figure 18**: dual occupancies/duplexes; terraces; 6m front
setback for trees and landscaping within front gardens; maximise deep soil and trees in
backyards; minimise driveway crossovers for increased tree canopy, landscaping and
pedestrian movement.

## Principles

- **P1** Development will preserve the existing low-scale and in some instances, historic character in the areas and will provide sensitive transition. Ensure scale, setbacks, separation, landscaping character seamlessly integrate the new and old developments. See Section 4.2 The building envelope.
- **P2** Developments are to demonstrate a range of residential building types that consist of one to two storeys and include single dwellings, dual occupancies, terraces, townhouses, multi-dwelling housing and low-scale apartments. Create an inviting street edge and preserve the landscape character of the area. See Section 4.1 Minimum lot sizes and Section 4.2.1 Building height and storeys.
- **P3** Maintain and draw inspiration from the surrounding design and materials of existing homes, reflecting the patterns, setbacks, pitched roofs, dark brick and masonry structures, quality finishes and maintain the bulk and scale with the surrounding architectural context to create cohesion throughout. See Section 4.5 Materials and finishes.
- **P4** Setbacks are to provide landscaping and deep soil provision. Driveways are to be limited and consolidated where possible to ensure walkable green streets that allow for tree canopy and landscaping along the street and within setbacks. **See Chapter 5 Residential Accommodation in the CBDCP 2023 for detail on setback requirements and design for this character area.**
- **P5** In sloped areas, such as the northern section of the character area towards Mount Lewis Park, new developments should be thoughtfully designed to integrate with the topography. Some of the key considerations include minimising height and bulk through split-level designs, strategic placement of driveways and access points, reducing the height and visibility of retaining walls, and implementing suitable landscaping and runoff management.

## Controls

None printed for this character area — Principles only.
