# Section 4.2.4 — Front Setbacks

> See Figure 7: Key design guidance for ground level setbacks to provide amenity, contribute to deep soil and planting, provide activation and respond to context — not converted, image only. Callouts: 1 Landscape setbacks for plantings and visual privacy in residential areas; 2 Setbacks reflect context and delineate privacy and public space; 3 Urban street edge with active frontages in Village Centres.

## Objectives

- **O1** To define streets, public spaces and key elements that include heritage and landscape features.
- **O2** To allow flexibility in setbacks where appropriate, minimising impacts on surrounding properties and the public domain.
- **O3** To enable sensory engagement with the street.
- **O4** To encourage setbacks that reinforce the desired future character of the area.
- **O5** To utilise front setbacks to provide privacy and amenity.
- **O6** To create lively destinations in the Precinct that caters for the growth in population and provides space for outdoor trading and dining, lingering, and increased tree canopy.

## Controls

- **C1** Front setbacks are to be consistent with Figure 40 [printed in-page as "Figure 8" — see note in Section 4.2.3 regarding this chapter's inconsistent figure numbering].
- **C2** For land with no mapped front setbacks, the following controls apply:
  (a) For land designated in Area 1 (low-rise living character area), front, side and rear setbacks are to be provided in accordance with Chapter 5 Residential Accommodation of CBDCP 2023.

## Principles

- **P1** Reinforce the Punchbowl and Wiley Park Village Centres by creating a defined urban street edge.
- **P2** To allow flexibility in response to context only where amenity and environmental impacts are minimal.
