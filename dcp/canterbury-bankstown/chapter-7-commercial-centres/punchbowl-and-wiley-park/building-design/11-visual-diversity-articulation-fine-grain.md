# Section 4.4 — Visual Diversity, Articulation and Fine Grain

> See Figure 18: Design guidance on building facades to ensure visual diversity, articulation and fine grain — not converted, image only. Callouts: 1 Fine grain frontages and buildings; 2 Variety in architectural design for visual interest and character; 3 Long podiums visually broken to create variety and ensure human scale; 4 Articulation zone in podiums to provide depth, shadow, greenery and outdoor space.

## Objectives

- **O1** Encourage variety in architectural design and character to provide a fine grain which enriches and enlivens the Precinct's public domain.
- **O2** Ensure building mass is reduced by articulating building envelopes into smaller components.
- **O3** Ensure that the scale, modulation and facade articulation of development responds to the character within the Precinct.
- **O4** To use architectural character to provide visual interest, texture and engagement.

## Principles

- **P1** Reduce the scale of building mass through vertical or horizontal articulation and using depth in facades.
- **P2** Use articulation zones in podiums to reduce mass, provide outdoor space and greenery and create depth and shadow.
- **P3** Strive for simplicity of and not complexity in articulation. This includes choosing a simple palette of materials and finishes.

## Controls

- **C1** Ensure for articulation of buildings into vertical bays and the integration of landscape into elevations that are seen from public space.
- **C2** An articulation zone with a 1m depth should be included in the street wall to allow architectural form to develop. Outdoor space within this zone can be applied to all building uses for amenity, surveillance and to add depth to facades.
- **C3** Facades facing the street or public space should be articulated with depth, relief and shadow. A minimum relief of 150mm between the masonry finish and glazing face should be utilised to provide depth and shadow.
- **C4** The finishing of service cupboards and basement car park entry should be coordinated in dark recessive colours to minimise the visual impact to the street.
