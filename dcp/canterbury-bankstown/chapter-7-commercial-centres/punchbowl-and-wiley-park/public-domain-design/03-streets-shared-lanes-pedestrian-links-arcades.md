# Section 3.3 — Streets, Shared Lanes, Pedestrian Links and Arcades

Streets, shared lanes and pedestrian links are places of movement: to watch, to pass
through and see the movement of people. This public framework provides for social and
commercial exchange.

In urban areas, a well-designed network of shared lanes and pedestrian links are essential
for improving permeability and connecting to main streets and open space, creating
walkability and equity of access and enjoyment.

The street should be safe and inviting to pedestrians and cyclists while accommodating
vehicles and traffic and be enhanced by tree canopies that promote amenity and
sustainability. Pedestrian links prioritise pedestrians and cyclists, featuring open skies and
awnings, while shared lanes also accommodate service vehicles and driveway access.

Arcades serve as internal retail 'lanes' that enhance permeability and provide an engaging
shopping experience. These narrow, intimate spaces encourage pedestrians to come and
stay and facilitate connections through street blocks, reducing walking distances.

New development is essential for contributing to a high-quality, permeable, and connected
movement network that is green, inviting and safe for everyone. This is achieved by
thoughtfully designing how a development interfaces with streets, lanes and pedestrian
links. Setbacks, public domain improvements and new connections should be considered.

## Objectives

- **O1** To improve connectivity for pedestrians and cyclists between key destination points.
- **O2** To provide a high quality, safe, connected cycle network that enables the community to choose to ride to work, home or a destination.
- **O3** To ensure shared lanes, pedestrian links and arcades are accessible, attractive, continuous, well-lit, safe and where appropriate, open to the sky.
- **O4** To provide amenity and safety to existing and proposed shared lanes, pedestrian links and arcades and active uses to engage pedestrians, where appropriate.
- **O5** To design shared lanes, pedestrian links and arcades that provide an intimate space to linger within a fine grain spatial network.
- **O6** To provide for vehicular entries and servicing away from primary street frontages using shared lanes.

## Principles

- **P1** Large development sites and sites located in the middle of large blocks should provide pedestrian links to improve permeability.
- **P2** Development must incorporate design measures that enhance pedestrian safety and comfort, including passive surveillance from habitable rooms and private open space, well-lit pathways and clear sightlines.
- **P3** Development that interfaces with pedestrian links should consider providing an active edge, where possible.

## Controls

- **C1** Where required by Council, shared lanes, pedestrian links and arcades must be provided in the locations identified in Figure 21 Public Domain Strategy. These connections must be designed to ensure for permeability, safety and integration with the broader movement network.
- **C2** Figures 22 to 28 illustrate cross-sections of Council's vision for specific streets, lanes and pedestrian links in the Punchbowl and Wiley Park Precinct. Development in these areas must demonstrate how the built form, setback treatments and any public domain improvements align with and contribute to delivering this vision.
- **C3** Pedestrian links are to have a minimum width of 4m and cater for the movement of pedestrians and cyclists in both directions. Shared lanes are to have a minimum width of 9m. These key public domain assets are to be designed to:
  (a) Ensure that buildings or structures are not located over any proposed pedestrian link, other than awnings.
  (b) Provide lighting, paving, street furniture and tree planting to clearly indicate the priority of the pedestrian or cycling user, (to be provided in accordance with Council's specifications and design standards).
  (c) Development must demonstrate the provision of physical and visual connections with neighbouring sites, including any necessary level adjustments. Detailed plans illustrating these connections are to be provided as part of the Development Application.
  (d) Awnings should be provided for active frontages and active edges for weather protection, offering shade and shelter from sun and rain. Awnings are to be designed and delivered in accordance with the requirements specified in Section 4.3.2 Active street frontages and edges.
  (e) Stormwater management measures must be incorporated to minimise the flow of pollutants from shared lanes and pedestrian links. Treatments must be implemented in accordance with Section 3 – Stormwater Drainage Systems of the CBDCP 2023.
  (f) Shared lanes and pedestrian links should be clear of all obstructions, including stairs, escalators and columns and provide a clear line of sight from one end to the other for surveillance and accessibility.
  (g) Shared lanes and pedestrian links will be open 24 hours a day, well-lit and adhere to Crime Prevention Through Environmental Design (CPTED) principles.
  (h) Where a development has the potential to create a shared lane or pedestrian link by extending an existing or proposed lane within its site, the lane should be completed or extended within the development site. If a development can deliver part of a lane or initiate a new lane, it should make provision for it.
- **C9** Pedestrian links and new shared lanes are to be registered as an easement on title or dedicated to Council prior to issue of an Occupation Certificate. Council reserves the right to not accept dedication. Any proposed dedication must be discussed at a pre-lodgement meeting.
- **C10** The maintenance of the shared lane and pedestrian link in perpetuity including cleaning, repairing and replacing damaged or worn paving, furniture and the like and maintaining landscaping in a healthy condition is the responsibility of the land owner, not Council. A management plan must accompany any development involving the provision of a shared lane or pedestrian link. The plan is to demonstrate how ongoing access, maintenance, and functionality of the shared lane or pedestrian link will be ensured, including through any future changes in ownership.
- **C11** Arcades within retail and commercial developments are to be designed to:
  (a) Be two storeys in height or where proposed as a single storey at ground level, have a minimum floor to ceiling height of 5m.
  (b) Have widths of 4m–6m where the links are provided within a two storey height, or 5m–8m wide where provided at a single floor level.
  (c) Be located at ground level and lined with spaces to facilitate active uses.
  (d) Be designed to have access to natural light.
  (e) Be open at each end, or where air conditioned, provide entry doors that are glazed and comprise a minimum 70% of the width of the entrance.
  (f) Be publicly accessible from 6am to 10pm each day.

## Street/lane vision figures (Figures 22–28)

Each of the following figures pairs a perspective street-section illustration with a short
"vision" narrative for a specific street or link type. All are image content; the accompanying
narrative text has been converted below.

### Figure 22 — Punchbowl Road

The vision for Punchbowl Road is to enhance the amenity for pedestrians and retail
frontages while maintaining the road's essential function as a key vehicular thoroughfare
within the Precinct. While interventions for improving amenity may be limited, there are
valuable opportunities to introduce planter boxes filled with small plants and flowers. These
planter boxes will help create a natural buffer between the road and pedestrian pathways,
softening the streetscape and improving the overall visual appeal. Furthermore, careful
consideration will be given to the placement of seating areas and small gathering spots,
allowing pedestrians to pause and enjoy the environment. This approach aims to balance
the needs of vehicular traffic with the desire for a lively, pedestrian-friendly atmosphere
along Punchbowl Road.

### Figure 23 — Lakemba Street

The vision for Lakemba Street is to establish a tree-lined corridor that enhances amenity,
biodiversity, and activation around the Wiley Park Village Centre. Mature trees will be
strategically planted between parking bays, creating a natural buffer between the road and
vehicular lanes. To the north, new developments will feature front setbacks that incorporate
high-quality landscaping, deep soil, and trees wherever possible. Additionally, balconies,
terraces, and entries to garden apartments will enhance passive surveillance over the
street, fostering a safer and more vibrant environment. To the south, retail spaces will front
the street, providing opportunities for active uses along the frontage, such as outdoor
dining and engaging shop displays, contributing to a lively atmosphere.

### Figure 24 — King Georges Road

The vision for King Georges Road is to enhance the pedestrian experience and increase
greening while recognising the road's vital role as a vehicular thoroughfare through the
Wiley Park Village Centre. There is a significant opportunity to transform the road into a
green boulevard, featuring landscaping and tree canopies along the existing centre verge
and at the western side. This transformation will enhance amenity, support biodiversity, and
create a buffer between residential areas and the road. On the western side, a landscaped
edge with small shrubs and flowering plants will improve the interface with retail uses,
creating a more inviting and vibrant atmosphere for both pedestrians and shoppers. New
residential development will include setbacks allowing for landscaping and potential for
tree canopy to further improve the interface along this road.

### Figure 25 — Residential tree lined street

The vision for tree lined streets in residential areas is to increase amenity and tree canopy
through the provision of new trees and landscaping in the road reserve between parking
lanes. In addition, a generous pedestrian pathway with high quality paving and
opportunities for intermittent seating. Within residential areas, new development will
provide front setbacks that incorporate high quality landscaping, deep soil and trees as well
as balconies, terraces and entries to garden apartments to provide passive surveillance
over the street. Tree lined streets are identified on Figure 21 Public Domain Strategy.

### Figure 26 — Residential tree lined street with an off-road cycleway

The vision for tree lined streets with a cycleway in residential areas is to increase amenity,
walking, cycling and tree canopy through the provision of a dedicated cycleway, new trees
and landscaping in the road reserve between parking lanes and include a generous
pedestrian pathway with high quality paving and opportunities for intermittent seating.
Within residential areas, new development will provide setbacks that incorporate high
quality landscaping, deep soil and trees, as well as balconies, terraces and entries to
apartments to provide passive surveillance over the street. Tree lined streets are identified
on Figure 21 Public Domain Strategy.

### Figure 27 — Stanlea Parade

The vision for Stanlea Parade is to transform the existing lane into a vibrant pedestrian link
that incorporates a dedicated cycleway and pedestrian path as part of the East-West
pedestrian/cycle link planned by Sydney Metro. This transformation will also feature
landscaping, tree canopy and opportunities for activation. To the north, the Wiley Park
Village Centre West, Key Site 3, will interface with Stanlea Parade, featuring a retail setback
that allows for outdoor dining and other active uses. This design will enhance amenity and
activation at the heart of Wiley Park, creating a lively atmosphere adjacent to the Metro
station and along the improved link.

### Figure 28 — Pedestrian link

The vision for pedestrian links is to enhance pedestrian and cycle connectivity across long
blocks in residential areas, facilitating better access and improved amenity in close
proximity to the Village Centres and the Metro station. These links will feature high-quality
paving, uninterrupted sightlines, be free of obstructions and be complemented by adequate
lighting for safety and surveillance, providing clear and accessible pathways. Additionally,
setbacks will allow for garden apartments that interface with the links, incorporating
high-quality landscaping, tree canopies, and terraces and balconies that promote passive
surveillance. An active edge along the links will create opportunities for corner shops or
cafés, fostering convenience, amenity and a sense of community. New links are required to
be delivered through new development on land identified in Figure 21 Public Domain
Strategy.
