# DEVELOPMENT CONTROLS

The objectives and controls for the development of the subject site are set out in this
section. These controls also refer to the indicative structure plan outlined in Figure 6b.

## 6.5 Access and movement

### Objectives

**O1** To provide a clear and legible site access and movement network with through-site
connectivity that is as safe as possible for residents, workers and visitors of the
development at all hours.

**O2** To ensure pedestrians and cyclists receive priority movement within and around the
site.

**O3** To change the current outlook of the site from a standalone hotel to a mixed-use
precinct with an appropriate balance between residential and commercial
development that is consistent with the development standards contained in the LEP.

### Development controls

**C1** The application must be accompanied by urban design and traffic and transport
studies to address the following:
(a) Proposed site access, internal streets (if applicable following advice from
TfNSW) and pedestrian connections including any easements/right of way,
where required;
(b) Basement car park layout to indicate proposed ingress/egress for different
users and services including residential, commercial, service vehicles, drop off
areas, etc;
(c) Detailed assessment of the impact of the development on the receiving road
network and on the proposed site circulation and functionality of the site;
(d) Provision of any publicly accessible open spaces on the ground floor.

**C2** Vehicular egress and ingress on the site must be facilitated by traffic management
devices to minimise the impact upon the existing Hume Highway traffic flow and to
appropriately accommodate traffic flows from the site onto Hume Highway. All costs
associated with the provision of pedestrian protection measures and traffic
management devices must be borne by the applicant.

**C3** Commercial, residential and service vehicle traffic should access the site via TfNSW
approved access point(s) along Hume Highway.

**C4** Although all traffic (residents, commercial and service vehicles) may enter the site via
a single access point (subject to TfNSW approval), commercial and residential traffic
must be separated from service vehicles at the basement of the building for effective
traffic circulation, safety and for waste collection purposes.

**C5** Proposed site access and circulation must not encroach and/or affect deep soil zone
onto the front landscape buffer zone (6m) along Hume Highway where possible,
except where the entry and exit points into and from the site are located.

**C6** Development must optimise the opportunities for active street frontages along Hume
Highway for highway related commercial purposes and streetscape design by:
(a) limiting the number of vehicle accessways to those approved by TfNSW; and
(b) minimising the extent of retail functions along Hume Highway to be consistent
with the objective of Zone E3 Productivity Support.

**C7** The preferred parking within the site is underground parking, the provision of which
must not extend/encroach on the deep soil zone within the building setback areas.

**C8** On-site parking provision must comply with Chapter 3.2 of the Canterbury-Bankstown
Development Control Plan 2023.

**C9** Public domain design must incorporate appropriate provisions for on-site bicycle
parking on the ground floor consistent with Chapter 3.2 of the Canterbury-Bankstown
Development Control Plan 2023.

**C10** Hume Highway is a classified State Road. Prior to lodging an application that proposes
redevelopment of the site, the applicant must consult with Transport for NSW
(TfNSW) to confirm the following:
(a) Detailed design of a deceleration lane and all associated road infrastructure
requirements to accommodate the deceleration lane. The application for the
site must include TfNSW approved drawings of a deceleration lane and all
associated road infrastructure required to accommodate the deceleration lane.
(b) Confirming the scope for a Transport Impact Assessment Report (TIA) including
but not limited to:
  (i) Access arrangements (including emergency vehicle access)
  (ii) Potential street upgrade requirements including network and intersection assessment
  (iii) Demand management measures and trip generation rates and requirements
  (iv) Cumulative impact of the proposal on the surrounding road networks and Road network capacity
  (v) Public transport access and pedestrian accessibility
  (vi) Potential road safety requirements
  (vii) Air Quality and Noise Impact Assessment.

**C11** Consistent with Council's DCP, each individual building would require a separate
loading dock which must be separate from parking circulation or exit lanes to ensure
safe pedestrian movement and uninterrupted flow of other vehicles in the circulation
roadways.

> **Cross-reference:** clauses C8 and C9 reference "Chapter 3.2" of this DCP for parking
> and bicycle parking standards — see `chapter-3-general-requirements/`.
