# Chapter 11 — Key Development Sites: council-wide sub-documents

Each Chapter 11 sub-chapter is a stand-alone, site-specific precinct DCP for a single
named site (e.g. "11.2 Undercliffe Bridge Precinct, Earlwood"). "Council-wide" here follows
this repo's usual meaning: the sub-document does not split internally by **former LGA** —
it does not follow that its controls apply broadly across the council area, since by
definition each Chapter 11 sub-chapter's controls apply only to its own named site. This
mirrors the treatment already used for the corridor-specific sub-chapters 8.2/8.3
(Employment Lands) and the precinct-specific sub-chapters 7.5/7.6/7.7 (Commercial
Centres): scoped to a specific place, but not an LGA split, so it sits under `general/`.

**11.14 is a partial exception to the framing above** — see its own flags in
[`11.14-riverwood-estate/_index.md`](11.14-riverwood-estate/_index.md). It has been placed
under `general/` for consistency, but it is a much larger, multi-block precinct structure
plan rather than a single-site DCP, and its `general/` classification should be treated as
provisional pending the repo owner's confirmation.

**11.13 is also worth reading with care** — see its own flags in
[`11.13-former-wsu-campus-milperra/_index.md`](11.13-former-wsu-campus-milperra/_index.md).
Like 11.14 it is a large, multi-section precinct DCP (58 pages, 7 sections), but unlike
11.14 (and unlike every other Chapter 11 sub-chapter converted so far) it has a genuine
internal **zone-based** split driven by its own Structure Plan — R1-family residential,
B1/E1 local centre, RE1 public open space and C2 conservation zoning all within the one
site — rather than a scale-transition or development-type variation only. It has been
placed under `general/` on the same basis as every other sub-chapter (no former-LGA
split), but the zone mix is closer to a genuine LEP zoning pattern than any other
Chapter 11 site converted to date.

## Note on internal former-LGA splits

None of 11.1–11.14 have former-LGA content. 11.2, 11.3, 11.5, 11.7, 11.8, 11.9 and
11.11 are single-section-in-spirit or otherwise undifferentiated single-site precincts with
no zone or LGA-based internal variation at all (unlike, say, 10.4's internal
Bankstown/Canterbury setback split). 11.4 does have an internal split, but it is by
**zone** (E1 local centre / R4 high density residential), not by former LGA — the same
pattern already established for 8.1 (E3/SP4). 11.1 has no internal zone or LGA split
either, though its controls do vary by location within the site (e.g. lower scale toward
Milton Street, taller toward WH Wagener Oval) as a function of urban design transition
rather than a zoning or LGA boundary. 11.5 is a former golf course site regulated mainly
through environmental/ecological studies (flora, fauna, flood, bushfire, acid sulfate
soils, heritage) rather than zone or LGA variation. 11.6 splits internally by
**development type** (dwelling houses / dual occupancies and attached dwellings /
residential flat buildings / community housing) as defined by its own Land Application
Map, not by zone or former LGA — a new internal-split pattern not seen in 11.1–11.5.
11.7, 11.8 and 11.9 have no internal split at all — each is a single undifferentiated
site-wide control set (11.9 does restrict its *applicability* to hospital development
only, but doesn't split internally once that scope is set). 11.10 has an internal
storey-height transition (six storeys at the site centre, tapering to four along
Waterloo Road/Norfolk Reserve and two along the southern boundary) driven by the LEP's
Height of Building Map rather than a zone or LGA split, and separately references Zone
R2 (south) and Zone SP2 (north) purely to calibrate setback distances. 11.12 has no
internal split either — it is a single-zone (E3 Productivity Support), single-site
hospital precinct; its setback and building-height controls vary by boundary/frontage
(the latter as a function of the site's topography, per Section 5 clause 5.1) rather
than by zone or LGA. 11.11 has no internal zone or LGA split either — it is a single
amalgamated-lot precinct whose only internal variation is by building height/storey
count (4–5 storeys, per Figure F.1), driven by the same kind of scale-transition logic
as 11.1, not a zone or LGA boundary. 11.14 also has no former-LGA split, but unlike
every other sub-chapter it does have an explicit, mapped **storey-height gradient across
the whole precinct** (4/6/8/12 storeys per Figure 5) that is closer in scale to a
mini-LEP height map than to any other Chapter 11 site's internal variation — see its own
notes for why its scope may not be directly comparable to 11.1–11.13.

**11.13 introduces a genuinely different kind of internal variation again.** Unlike any
prior sub-chapter, 11.13's Concept Plan/Structure Plan splits the site by an actual
**mix of LEP-style zones** — multiple R1-family residential categories, a B1/E1 local
centre (Section 5), RE1 public open space (Section 3.5), and C2 Environmental
Conservation (Cumberland Plain Woodland, Sections 3.4, 3.6, 3.9, 6, Appendix A) — rather
than a single alternate variable (storey height, development type, or scale transition)
layered on an otherwise uniform residential zone as in 11.4, 11.6, 11.10, 11.11 or 11.14.
See [`11.13-former-wsu-campus-milperra/_index.md`](11.13-former-wsu-campus-milperra/_index.md)
for a flag regarding an apparent legend inconsistency on 11.13's own Concept Plan (three
different housing typologies all labelled "R1 Zone").

**11.16 has no former-LGA split either, and — unlike every other Chapter 11 sub-chapter
converted so far — states no zone code at all anywhere in its own text**, relying entirely
on its site boundary map (Figure 3) and a generic reference to "the LEP." Its only internal
variation is a height gradient like 11.10/11.11/11.14's, but expressed as a **per-block
storey map** (Figure 25: 2 up to 18 storeys across different blocks) rather than a
transition rule stated in the text controls — the text controls (Section 3.1) only ever
state a general "2–3 storey street wall" and an "approximately 18 storeys" tallest-tower
figure, never the intermediate block-by-block values the map actually shows. See
[`11.16-1-leicester-street-chester-hill/_index.md`](11.16-1-leicester-street-chester-hill/_index.md).

Whether any future Chapter 11 site will turn out to have an internal former-LGA split is
unconfirmed until each is supplied and checked individually, per the conversion guide's
instruction not to assume a pattern from one converted sub-chapter. Only 11.15 (Marco
Avenue) remains to check.

11.16 does not follow 11.13/11.14's restarting-O#/C#-per-subsection convention at all —
its Objectives, Principles and Controls each run their own count per numbered section
(1.1, 1.2, 2.1, etc.), the same convention used by 11.1–11.10 and 11.12.

## Note on internal structure/numbering (new as of 11.6–11.8; extended for 11.14; corrected for 11.13)

11.1–11.5, 11.7, 11.8 and 11.11 all present development controls as sequentially numbered
clauses within each section (e.g. 1.1, 1.2, 1.3…). 11.6 breaks this pattern: apart from its
introductory Section 1 (which uses the standard Objectives O1–O4 format), Sections 2–5
present controls as sub-headed bullet/paragraph blocks with no clause numbers at all.
This means clause-level citation isn't possible for 11.6 Sections 2–5 — cite by section
and sub-heading instead. See `11.6-former-potts-hill-reservoirs-potts-hill/_index.md` for
detail, including a further internal inconsistency between the Land Application Map's own
"Section" legend numbering and the document's actual Section 1–5 headings.

11.14 introduced a third, distinct numbering pattern: Objectives (O1, O2…) and Controls
(C1, C2…), with both counters restarting at 1 in *every* sub-section (17 independent O#/C#
counters across the document, plus 3 more within the 4.3 heritage sub-subsections). At the
time this was recorded it was noted as "the first Chapter 11 sub-chapter to use O#/C#
labelling at all" — **this is now known to be incorrect.**

**Correction, per 11.13's conversion:** 11.13 also uses the Objectives (O#) / Development
controls (C#) format, with both counters restarting independently in *every* one of its
own numbered sub-sections (3.1, 3.2, 3.2.1 plus its three further "Development controls –"
sub-groups, 3.3–3.9, 4.1–4.7, 5, 6, 7 and 7.1) — at least 20 separate restarting O#/C#
sequences, more than 11.14. Because 11.13 was converted later in real time than 11.14
despite its lower chapter number, the original "first" framing reflected conversion order,
not the sites' actual drafting history — it is not possible to say from the two DCP
documents alone which of 11.13 or 11.14 was *drafted* first using this convention. Citation
in both sub-chapters must always include the sub-section (and, for 11.14's 4.3, the
sub-subsection) alongside the O/C label, e.g. "11.13 3.2.1 C9" or "11.14 3.3 C3", never
just the bare O#/C#. 11.13's Appendix A additionally introduces a fourth, unrelated
labelling convention ("G#", restarting under each of its own four headings) — see
[`11.13-former-wsu-campus-milperra/_index.md`](11.13-former-wsu-campus-milperra/_index.md).

**11.16 reuses the "G#" label, but promotes it into the main O/P/C-style hierarchy.**
Its Section 1.5 (Connecting to Country) pairs Objectives (O1–O3) with "Design Guidance"
G1–G6 in place of Controls — the first time "G#" has appeared as a main-hierarchy label
(alongside Objectives, replacing Controls) rather than confined to an appendix as in
11.13. 11.16 also introduces its own numbering quirks distinct from both 11.13 and
11.14: Section 3.3 (Private Communal Space) prints "Principles" before "Objectives" (the
only inversion of the O→P→C order seen anywhere in Chapter 11 to date), and Section 4.4
(Waste Management) restarts its *Objectives* numbering once under two prose sub-headings
("Construction," "Operation") rather than numbered sub-sections. This is a much narrower
version of 11.13/11.14's pattern (which restarts *both* Objectives and Controls, in every
numbered sub-section across the whole document) — 11.16 restarts only Objectives, only
once, and does so under plain prose headings rather than the numbered sub-section scheme
11.13/11.14 use.
See [`11.16-1-leicester-street-chester-hill/_index.md`](11.16-1-leicester-street-chester-hill/_index.md).

## Amendment-date cohorts and a shared naming inconsistency (updated as of 11.16)

11.1–11.9 and 11.11 all carry the cover-page date "June 2023 (Amended August
2024)." 11.10 and 11.12 both instead carry "June 2023 (Amended April 2025)" — previously
recorded as a different amendment date shared by exactly these two sub-chapters and no
others converted so far. **11.13 also carries "June 2023 (Amended April 2025)"** — this is
now a three-sub-chapter cohort (11.10, 11.12, 11.13), not two. 11.10 and 11.12 also both
mis-name the *State Environmental Planning Policy (Housing) 2021* as "State Environment
Planning Policy (Housing) 2021" (missing "-al"), whereas 11.8 (clause 1.11) renders it
correctly — 11.11 does not cite this SEPP at all, so it can't be checked against either
naming variant. 11.14 cites the SEPP correctly ("State Environmental Planning Policy
(Housing) 2021," clause 4.1 C4), consistent with 11.8's correct rendering rather than
11.10/11.12's typo. **11.13 also cites the SEPP correctly** (Section 3.3 C3) — despite
sharing 11.10/11.12's amendment date, it does *not* share their SEPP naming typo. This
weakens rather than confirms the earlier theory that the shared amendment date and the
SEPP typo reflect a common drafting template shared by exactly 11.10/11.12: a third
sub-chapter now shares the date without the typo, so the date and the typo do not appear
to travel together as reliably as previously thought. Treated as an open question rather
than resolved either way.

11.14 breaks the amendment-date cohort pattern entirely — its cover is dated **August
2025 ("Final Version")**, over a year later than either the "August 2024" or "April 2025"
cohorts, and it carries no per-page footer date at all (unlike every other sub-chapter
converted so far, which all repeat their amendment date on every page footer, including
11.13). Its internal document name ("...DCP **2023**") doesn't match its August 2025 cover
date either. Treat 11.14's dating as unresolved — see its own `_index.md` for detail.
Worth checking any future Chapter 11 sub-chapter with an August 2025 (or later) date for
the same footer-less, internally-2023-but-covered-2025 pattern before assuming it's
unique to 11.14.

**11.16 breaks the pattern again, in a different way.** Its cover reads **"March 2026"**
only — no day, no "Amended" wording, and (unlike 11.14) no reference to an original 2023
version at all, anywhere found in the supplied text. Per the conversion guide, a
month/year-only date with no amendment table is omitted from front matter rather than
guessed, so 11.16's section files carry no `effective_date`/`amended_date`. Unlike 11.14,
11.16's per-page footer *does* repeat "Canterbury-Bankstown Development Control Plan
2023" (matching the parent document's usual footer), so 11.16 does not share 11.14's
missing-footer anomaly — its anomaly is instead that the cover date has no visible
relationship to a 2023 base version at all. Worth checking 11.15 (the last outstanding
sub-chapter) for whichever of these two 2025/2026-era dating patterns it follows, if
either.
