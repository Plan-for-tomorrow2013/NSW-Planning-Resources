# 4.5 Amenity

## 4.5.1 Solar Access

### Objectives

O1. Ensure that new development provides an appropriate amount of direct sunlight access to
    open space, the public domain or neighbouring properties.

### Controls

C1. For development to which this DCP applies, solar access is to be provided in accordance
    with the ADG (including maisonette apartments).

C2. Solar access to neighbouring development must demonstrate compliance with solar access
    provisions as contained with Chapter 5.1 of the Canterbury Bankstown DCP 2023 or ADG
    (where applicable) to the neighbouring development.

C3. Solar modelling at DA stage shall demonstrate that the northern elevations of the
    existing permitted built form envelopes of the Waldron Road properties are to receive a
    minimum of 2 hours of direct solar access at winter solstice. For this test it can be
    assumed that the building facades on the Waldron Road properties are set back from the
    centre line of Frost Lane by 12m.

## 4.5.2 Wind effects

### Objectives

O1. Ensure the development satisfies the wind standards in this DCP to keep conditions
    comfortable for pedestrians, protect nearby buildings, and support the growth of street
    trees.

### Controls

C1. Any development application for a structure that exceeds 45 metres in height, must be
    accompanied by a wind effects report that has been prepared by a suitably qualified
    engineer and consider design solutions in Figure 36, for other buildings a report is at
    the discretion of the Council. The report is required to adhere to the following
    criteria:

    (a) Grounded in wind tunnel testing, it should conduct a thorough comparison and
        analysis of the existing wind conditions and the anticipated wind conditions
        resulting from the proposed structure.
    (b) Outline the probable impacts of wind on the pedestrian environment at the ground
        level within the site and in the public domain.
    (c) Present design solutions to address potential wind impacts.
    (d) Demonstrate how the proposed development minimises the impact of wind on both public
        and private domains.
    (e) Establish the alignment of the proposed building and its stipulations with the
        solutions outlined in this Development Control Plan (DCP).

> See Figure 36 in the source PDF — Tall Buildings Design Guidelines, City of Toronto (2013)
> PP.65, illustrating wind flow issues (accelerated wind speeds near windward corners, low
> building pressure areas, canyon effects between towers) and solutions (stepped-back base
> buildings, horizontal canopies, sloped parapet walls, colonnades) — not converted; see
> `appendices/_index.md`.

C2. Wind effects induced by development activities should not surpass the following
    thresholds:

    (a) 10 metres/second in streets featuring an active frontage (refer to Figure 8 —
        Proposed Active Frontage Control Diagram: Location of Active Frontages).
    (b) 16 metres/second in all other streets.

C3. Building design should prioritise the reduction of adverse wind effects on open spaces
    within developments.

C4. To guarantee usability and comfort, the impact of wind on balconies should be minimised
    through:

    (a) Implementation of partially recessed screens, pergolas, and shutters.
    (b) Provision of recessed balconies on tall buildings where feasible.
    (c) Utilisation of operable screens when high winds prohibit alternative solutions.
