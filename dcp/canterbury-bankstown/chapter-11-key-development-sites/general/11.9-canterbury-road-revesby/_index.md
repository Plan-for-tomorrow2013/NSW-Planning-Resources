# 11.9 — 297–299 Canterbury Road, Revesby

Site-specific precinct DCP for a proposed **hospital** development at 297–299 Canterbury
Road, Revesby (Lot 9, DP 663160 and Lot 202, DP 840245), near the existing
Bankstown-Lidcombe Hospital. Covers setbacks, built form/design excellence, landscaping
and healing-environment open space, parking/transport/pedestrian access, and
flooding/drainage/WSUD (the overland flow path on the site's western side). Carries the
chapter-level precedence clause. No former-LGA content — sits under `general/`.

Given 6 internal sections, kept as its **own folder** per the conversion guide's 2+
section rule.

## Notes / anomalies

- **Scope explicitly restricted to hospital development** — the "Land to which chapter
  applies" clause states: *"This chapter applies to development for the purposes of a
  hospital. For other development types, the other parts of the DCP apply."* This is a
  sharper version of the use-restricted scoping already seen in 11.8 (which applies to
  "residential accommodation purpose including residential care facilities"), but 11.9 is
  the first Chapter 11 sub-chapter to say explicitly that *other* development types on the
  same land fall back to the rest of the DCP rather than to this chapter at all. Worth
  keeping in mind when answering questions about this site for non-hospital proposals.
- **Legally described by lot/DP** (Lot 9, DP 663160 and Lot 202, DP 840245) rather than
  only by street address — the only Chapter 11 sub-chapter so far to give a formal legal
  land description alongside the street address in its applicability clause.
- **Mandatory State Design Review Panel referral** (clause 3.1) — a control not seen in
  any other Chapter 11 sub-chapter converted so far. Worth flagging as a distinctive,
  hospital-specific procedural requirement rather than assuming it's DCP boilerplate that
  might apply elsewhere.
- **A second, narrower precedence-type statement** appears in clause 2.2: where the
  27m northern-boundary setback (which incorporates an existing right of way to 299A
  Canterbury Road) is inconsistent with the terms of that right of way, *"the terms of the
  right of way prevail."* This is the same pattern as 11.1's second, narrower
  precedence statement scoped to its waste-management section — a chapter-level
  precedence clause plus a second, more specific one elsewhere in the same sub-chapter.
- **Cross-reference to "Chapter 3.2 of this DCP"** for on-site parking provision (clause
  5.7) — this is the same Chapter 3.2 cross-reference flagged as unverified in 11.8.
  **11.9's own text doesn't name Chapter 3.2's title**, but see 11.10 (clause 3.12: *"Chapter
  3.2 Parking of the Canterbury-Bankstown Development Control Plan 2023"*), converted
  alongside this sub-chapter, which does — this corroborates that Chapter 3.2 is indeed
  titled "Parking" and makes the 11.8/11.9 cross-references to it look correct rather than
  a stray/mismatched reference. See the top-level council index and Chapter 11 index for
  the updated cross-chapter reconciliation note.
- **PDF extraction spacing artifacts**: the supplied source PDF's text extraction dropped
  spaces at a number of word boundaries (e.g. "amix" for "a mix", "openspace" for "open
  space", "throughimprovements" for "through improvements", "cater forstaff, patients
  andvisitors" for "cater for staff, patients and visitors"). These have been silently
  corrected as ordinary text-reconstruction rather than treated as source typos, since
  they are clearly extraction artifacts rather than anything printed in the original
  document. Flagged here for transparency rather than in each individual clause.
- **Two unconverted figures**: Figure 1 (an aerial site map) and Figure 2 (a four-image
  photo collage of *external* healing-environment/landscaping reference precedents from
  Queensland Children's Hospital and Melbourne Children's Hospital — not photos of this
  site). See `appendices/_index.md`.
- **Recurring ecological-sensitivity theme**: like 11.5 (Georges River foreshore) and
  11.6 (bush regeneration zone adjoining Potts Hill Reservoir), 11.9 doesn't itself abut a
  named ecological reserve, but does require flood-emergency and stormwater
  consultation with the NSW State Emergency Service and preservation of an overland
  flow path — a milder version of the same "adjacent sensitive natural/hazard feature"
  pattern recurring across Chapter 11 sub-chapters.
- No former-LGA content; no B2/E1 zone-naming issue found in this sub-chapter; zones
  not stated anywhere in the supplied pages (omitted from front matter accordingly).
