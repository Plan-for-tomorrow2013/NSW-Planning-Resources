# Section 9 — Livable housing

> Source: DCP 2023–Chapter 5.2 (Amended March 2026), pages 133–135

## Explanation

*Connective City 2036* encourages homes that enable the community to age-in-place and are easier to use for all occupants including seniors, people with temporary injuries, families with young children, and people with disabilities and their families. A key action is to provide a mix of housing products where the rooms and layout are of a size that are usable by a broader cross-section of the community. This section applies to the construction of new dwellings. It does not apply to modifications to existing dwellings.

## Objectives

- **O1** To meet the changing needs of residents throughout their lifetime and to better enable residents to age-in-place.
- **O2** To ensure dwellings are easy to enter, easy to navigate in and around, and be capable of easy and cost-effective adaption.

## Development controls

C1 Development must comply with the following requirements:

| Development types | Development controls |
|---|---|
| Dwelling houses | New dwellings are to provide:<br>• capability for a safe and continuous path of travel from the street or car parking area into the dwelling;<br>• internal doorways to rooms on the entry level used for living, dining, bedroom, bathroom, kitchen, laundry and sanitary compartment purposes with a minimum clear opening width of 820mm. Internal corridors/passageways to these doorways should provide a minimum clear width of 1,000mm to facilitate comfortable and unimpeded movement between spaces;<br>• a toilet at the ground floor level;<br>• a bathroom that contains a hobless shower recess;<br>• reinforced walls around the toilet, shower and bath to support safe installation of grabrails at a later date;<br>• stairways that are designed with handrails and safe access.<br><br>The above dimensions and clearances are to be demonstrated on a floor plan.<br><br>Should the National Construction Code apply livable housing design requirements to class 1a buildings in NSW, the new dwelling must achieve the Silver Standard of the Livable Housing Design Guidelines. |
| Secondary dwellings | New secondary dwellings are to provide:<br>• capability for a safe and continuous path of travel from the street or car parking area into the dwelling;<br>• internal doorways to rooms on the entry level used for living, dining, bedroom, bathroom, kitchen, laundry and sanitary compartment purposes with a minimum clear opening width of 820mm. Internal corridors/passageways to these doorways should provide a minimum clear width of 1,000mm to facilitate comfortable and unimpeded movement between spaces;<br>• a bathroom that contains a hobless shower recess;<br>• reinforced walls around the toilet, shower and bath to support safe installation of grabrails at a later date.<br><br>Should the National Construction Code apply livable housing design requirements to class 1a buildings in NSW, the new dwelling must achieve the Silver Standard of the Livable Housing Design Guidelines. |
| Dual occupancies and semi-detached dwelling | New dual occupancy dwellings are to provide:<br>• capability for a safe and continuous path of travel from the street or car parking area into the dwelling;<br>• internal doorways to rooms on the entry level used for living, dining, bedroom, bathroom, kitchen, laundry and sanitary compartment purposes with a minimum clear opening width of 820mm. Internal corridors/passageways to these doorways should provide a minimum clear width of 1,000mm to facilitate comfortable and unimpeded movement between spaces;<br>• a toilet at the ground floor level;<br>• a bathroom that contains a hobless shower recess;<br>• reinforced walls around the toilet, shower and bath to support safe installation of grabrails at a later date;<br>• stairways that are designed with handrails and safe access.<br><br>The above dimensions and clearances are to be demonstrated on a floor plan.<br><br>Should the National Construction Code apply livable housing design requirements to class 1a buildings in NSW, at least one dwelling must achieve the Gold Standard; and the second dwelling must achieve the Silver Standard of the Livable Housing Design Guidelines. |
| Multi dwelling housing and attached dwellings | Where there are five or more dwellings, at least 20% of new dwellings are to provide:<br>• capability for a safe and continuous path of travel from the street or car parking area into the dwelling;<br>• internal doorways to rooms on the entry level used for living, dining, bedroom, bathroom, kitchen, laundry and sanitary compartment purposes with a minimum clear opening width of 820mm. Internal corridors/passageways to these doorways should provide a minimum clear width of 1,000mm to facilitate comfortable and unimpeded movement between spaces;<br>• a toilet at the ground floor level;<br>• a bathroom that contains a hobless shower recess;<br>• reinforced walls around the toilet, shower and bath to support safe installation of grabrails at a later date;<br>• stairways that are designed with handrails and safe access.<br><br>A further 20% of dwellings are to have:<br>• a bedroom at the ground floor of 10m²;<br>• increased corridor dimensions to 1,200mm;<br>• a front door landing of 1,350mm x 1,350mm;<br>• a clear 850mm opening for any doors at the ground floor;<br>• a minimum 1,200mm between the walls of the ground floor bathroom or between amenities if located in a combined bathroom;<br>• a 1,200mm clearance in front of the toilet from the door opening;<br>• a straight stairwell that is at least 1m wide and adjacent to a load bearing wall.<br><br>The above dimensions and clearances are to be demonstrated on a floor plan.<br><br>Should the National Construction Code apply livable housing design requirements to class 1a buildings in NSW, a minimum 40% of new dwellings must comply with the Livable Housing Design Guidelines (Livable Housing Australia), to be split as follows:<br>• a minimum 20% of new dwellings must achieve the Silver Standard; and<br>• a minimum 20% of new dwellings must achieve the Gold Standard. |
| Residential flat buildings and shop top housing | A minimum 40% of new dwellings must comply with the Livable Housing Design Guidelines (Livable Housing Australia), to be split as follows:<br>• a minimum 20% of new dwellings must achieve the Silver Standard; and<br>• a minimum 20% of new dwellings must achieve the Gold Standard.<br><br>However, it is noted that shop top housing will not deliver dwellings at the ground floor as this would be inconsistent with the LEP definition.<br><br>Council may vary the Livable Housing Design Guidelines (Design Element 1–Dwelling Access) if it is demonstrated to Council's satisfaction that it is not possible to achieve step-free pathways. |
| Boarding houses and co-living housing | A minimum 20% of new boarding rooms or private rooms must achieve the Silver Standard of the Livable Housing Design Guidelines (Livable Housing Australia).<br><br>Council may vary the Livable Housing Design Guidelines (Design Element 1–Dwelling Access) if it is demonstrated to Council's satisfaction that it is not possible to achieve step-free pathways. |
