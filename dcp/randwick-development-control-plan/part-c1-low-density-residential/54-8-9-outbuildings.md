### 8.9. Outbuildings

Definition Outbuildings are freestanding buildings that are not attached to any dwelling on the site, that may or may not be enclosed on the side elevations. They are considered as non-habitable floorspace and include cabanas, sheds, gazebos, greenhouses, and the like.

Objectives

1\. To ensure that outbuildings present as ancillary structures to any dwelling/s located on the lot

2\. To ensure that outbuildings do not significantly impact upon the privacy and amenity of neighbouring properties

3\. To ensure outbuildings achieve acceptable levels of building design and materiality

4\. To ensure appropriate setbacks for landscaping and to retain existing deep soil permeable surfaces and open space of the overall lot.

Controls

a) Locate outbuildings behind the alignment of the front building façade

b) Position outbuildings to optimise backyard space and do not locate them within the required deep soil permeable surface area

c) Except for laneway development, outbuildings must be single storey only, and must not exceed a maximum height of 3.6m and a wall height of 2.4m

d) Outbuildings may be constructed to the side and rear boundaries where:

i. The external walls are finished and do not require frequent maintenance; and

ii. There are no windows or openings facing the adjoining allotments; and

iii. Adequate solar access to the adjoining dwellings is maintained.

e) For sites with secondary street frontages, a nil setback is only permitted if the outbuilding adjoins a building constructed on the boundary. In all other situations outbuildings are to comply with the setback requirements as listed under section 3.3 of this DCP.

f) Where there is an existing detached garage at the rear of the allotment, a first floor addition may be considered subject to the following measures:

i. If the upper floor level is contained within the roof form of the outbuilding as an attic storey; and

ii. The facades are suitably articulated; and

iii. An integrated landscape design with screen planting is provided to visually soften the outbuilding; and

iv. If it does not create excessive structural bulk as viewed from the adjoining properties; and

v. Adequate solar access is maintained to the adjoining dwellings; and

vi. Adequate privacy is maintained to the adjoining dwellings.

g) Outbuildings must not be used as a separate business premises.
