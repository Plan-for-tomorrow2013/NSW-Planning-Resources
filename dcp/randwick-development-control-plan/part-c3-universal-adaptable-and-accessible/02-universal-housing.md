## 2. Universal housing

Definition Universal housing

incorporates elements that are ‘designed -

in’ at the construction stage, thus

not requiring subsequent modification or adaptation through the lifecycle of occupants. It ensures homes are practical, functional, and adaptable for people of all ages and abilities, supporting aging in place and inclusive communities.

Objectives The objectives for universal housing are to:

1\. Incorporate universal design principles at the planning and construction stages, ensuring accessibility features are built-in rather than retrofitted

2\. Ensure the Liveable Housing Design Standard elements are achieved across new developments

3\. Ensure dwellings are visually integrated into the surrounding streetscape, avoiding segregation or stigma

4\. Increase the supply of universal housing within Randwick City

5\. Promote sustainable development by extending the usability and lifespan of dwellings

6\. Ensure housing is safe, functional, and accessible for people of all ages, abilities and life stages.

Controls

a) Proposed development must incorporate the following universal design measures for all ground floor dwellings:

i. An accessible continuous path of travel from the street entrance and/or parking area to dwelling entrance, incorporating step-free access

ii. At least one ground level entrance into the dwelling with a clear opening width minimum of 1m

iii. Internal doors and corridors with widths that allow comfortable movement, ideally minimum 820 mm doorways and 1m corridors

iv. A toilet on the ground (or entry) level that provides adequate circulation space for accessibility

v. Reinforced walls around the toilet, shower and bath to support the safe installation of grab rail for future adaptability

vi. Hob-free and step-free entry into the shower

vii. A continuous handrail on one side of any stairway where the rise is more than 1m

b) Where proposed, all universally designed dwellings must be clearly identified on the submitted DA plans

c) For new dwelling house, semi-detached dwelling and dual occupancy development, consider incorporating the universal design measures outlined in control 2(a)

d) Additional best practice provisions:

• Provide level thresholds at all external doors.

• Ensure kitchen layouts allow for wheelchair circulation and adaptable workspaces.

• Include accessible power points and switches at appropriate heights.

• Design bathrooms and laundries for future adaptability, including provision for front-loading appliances.

• Ensure parking spaces for adaptable dwellings comply with AS2890.6 (Accessible Parking).

Note Contral a) is adapted from the Livable Housing Design Standard 2022. Further information is available at: https://ncc.abcb.gov.au/resource/standard/livable-housing-design-standard

Note Variations to control 2(a), will only be considered where it can be demonstrated that site conditions would preclude achieving the controls (e.g. sloping sites with steep gradients, narrow allotments, etc.)
