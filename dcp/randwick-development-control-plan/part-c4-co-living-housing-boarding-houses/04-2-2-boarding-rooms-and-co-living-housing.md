### 2.2. Boarding rooms and co-living housing rooms

Explanation The design and layout of individual rooms in boarding houses and co-living housing developments directly affect the comfort, privacy, and wellbeing of residents. Ensuring adequate room size, access to natural light and ventilation, and appropriate facilities supports a high standard of living and long-term adaptability of the development.

Controls

a) All habitable rooms must comply with the minimum room size requirements specified in the Housing SEPP. In addition, rooms must have a minimum internal width of 2.6m to avoid poor-quality long-and-narrow room layouts and to support functional living spaces

b) Co-living developments must achieve a range of room sizes as follows:

i. A minimum of 20% of rooms must have a minimum floor area of 25sqm

ii. No more than 20% of rooms may be provided at the minimum sizes prescribed under the Housing SEPP being12sqm for a single room and 16sqm for a double room; and

iii. A minimum of 50% of all rooms must include a private balcony designed in accordance with the Apartment Design Guide for studios, with a minimum area of 4sqm and minimum depth of 2 metres

iv. Where balconies are provided, their design and location must prioritise:

- north facing rooms to maximise solar access

- smaller single rooms to enhance amenity for occupants

- rooms facing a primary street to support passive surveillance, contribute to active street edges and enhance the public domain and streetscape.

c) Developments must include a mix of room typologies, including cluster arrangements, to support diverse living needs and promote social interaction

d) Rooms must to be designed and oriented to maximise direct sunlight and ensure adequate levels of daylight throughout the day contributing to occupant amenity, comfort and well-being

e) Each room must include a window opening or external opening to  provide natural light and ventilation. Windows must have minimum glass area equivalent to at least 10% of the

room’s total floor area. Where balconies or terraces are provided, they are to be directly accessible from the room and contribute to improved amenity

f) All double occupancy rooms must include an en-suite bathroom. En-suite bathrooms also strongly encouraged in single occupancy rooms to enhance privacy and amenity Kitchenettes are encouraged to support independent living. Gas appliances must not to be installed within individual boarding rooms and co-living rooms due to health and safety risks associated with gas use in small spaces (which are typically smaller than an ADG compliant studios).  and high density living.

Note: Skylights or windows opening to an internal hallway, corridor or lightwell cannot be used as the sole source for light and air circulation for any habitable room. En-suite bathrooms are to be a minimum of 2.1sqm which must include a hand basin and a shower that is a minimum of 08sqm. Accessible bathrooms must meet the requirements of Part C3 of this DCP and the relevant Australian Standards for accessible bathrooms. Any kitchenette with appliances or capable of catering to portable appliances must provide adequate mechanical ventilation to ensure smells and toxic fumes do not impact the amenity and health of occupants.
