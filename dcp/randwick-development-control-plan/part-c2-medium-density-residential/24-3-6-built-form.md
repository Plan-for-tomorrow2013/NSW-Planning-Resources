### 3.6. Built form

#### Explanation

Built form refers to the ‘three dimensional’ appearance or character of medium density residential areas, encompassing the aesthetic quality, shape, scale and configuration of individual buildings, and their relationship to other buildings, and to streets and the public domain. Objectives and controls focus on achieving an appropriate scale for new development so that buildings achieve a coherent, harmonious and visually appealing urban environment, that enhances the public realm. Where redevelopment proposals vary from the built form objectives and controls due to unique or extreme site conditions, the development proposal will be assessed on merit.

#### Objectives

The objectives for built form within medium density residential areas are to:

1\. Ensure built form is compatible with the desired future character of the street, block or surrounding area, in terms of building bulk, scale and massing

2\. Promote coherent and orderly redevelopment of land, avoiding the isolation of sites

3\. Ensure development reinforces the urban structure and street hierarchy through responsive design

4\. Ensure development respects the existing siting, scale, form and character of adjoining properties

5\. Avoid large, bulky and unarticulated ‘wall-like’ building form that visually dominate the public realm

6\. Achieve a scale transition between medium density residential buildings and surrounding residential areas to protect residential amenity

7\. Design development to optimise solar access, protect privacy, maintain view corridors and enhance visual amenity for neighbouring properties, public spaces and communal areas in accordance with best practice urban design principles

8\. Ensure that the number of storeys in a development aligns with the maximum permissible building height in metres under the RLEP 2012 allowing for ADG compliant floor-to-floor heights, internal amenity, roof top structures (such as plant rooms or lift overruns) and to allow for flexibility for future changes of use (particularly at ground floor level)

9\. Allow sufficient space between floors for building services and acoustic attenuation

10\. Development carried out under the Housing SEPP, including the Low and Mid-Rise Housing (LMR) provisions and the Infill Affordable Housing bonus, must be designed to minimise adverse visual impacts and appropriately manage building bulk and massing, having regard to the character and scale of the surrounding area.

#### Controls

a) Incorporate upper-level building setbacks in accordance with Tables 2, 3 and 4, and Figures 5, 6 and 10, to reduce the scale of buildings, establish a consistent street wall height, minimise overshadowing of the street and other buildings and create a cohesive streetscape environment

b) Development proposals utilising the Housing SEPP Low and Mid Rise (LMR) and/or the Infill Affordable Housing Bonus legislation, and that are subject to the requirements of DCP part B1 Local character areas, are to provide a report that details how the proposed site planning, building design and landscaping, addresses the Character Principles and Design Guidelines for the relevant Local Character Area in DCP part B1.

#### Lot amalgamation

a) Site amalgamation must not compromise the future development potential of adjacent or intervening sites e.g. the potential to realise ADG compliant development at the maximum building height (HOB) and density (FSR) permissible under the RLEP

b) Where a development proposal unavoidably results in an isolated site, the applicant must demonstrate that reasonable efforts have been made to avoid this outcome. The following must be submitted with the DA:

i. Evidence of written offer/s made to the owner of the isolated site and any responses received prior to the lodgement of the DA

ii. Schematic diagrams demonstrating how the isolated site is capable of being redeveloped in accordance with relevant provisions of the RLEP and this DCP to achieve an appropriate urban form and an acceptable level of amenity

iii. Schematic diagrams showing how the isolated site could potentially be integrated into the development site in the future in accordance with relevant provisions of the RLEP and this DCP to achieve a coherent built form outcome for the block.

#### Minimum frontage width

Analysis indicates that development of 6 to 8 storeys on sites with a frontage width of less 20m (single or amalgamated lots), is unlikely to achieve acceptable urban design and residential amenity outcomes and is not supported. Narrow frontage sites constrain building design and typically result in poor compliance with the Apartment Design Guide (ADG), including challenges in achieving:

• adequate building separation

• reasonable solar access

• effective natural cross-ventilation

• appropriate visual and acoustic privacy for habitable rooms and balconies

• functional and usable communal open space In addition, limited site width restricts opportunities for deep soil planting, tree canopy establishment, and meaningful front, side and rear landscape zones. Such sites also tend to necessitate inefficient building and basement configurations, including reliance on car stackers or automated parking systems, and result in dominant basement entries, exposed ramps, and oversized garage doors that adversely impact the streetscape quality. Accordingly, a minimum site frontage of 20 metres is required for development of 6 to 8 storeys to ensure that built form, landscape and amenity outcomes are consistent with Council’s objectives.

#### Objectives

1\. Ensure development sites are of sufficient width to accommodate 6–8 storey built form while achieving high-quality urban design and residential amenity outcomes

2\. Facilitate compliance with the Apartment Design Guide (ADG), including requirements for building separation, solar access, natural ventilation, and privacy

3\. Enable adequate provision of deep soil, landscaping and tree canopy to support environmental performance and neighbourhood character

4\. Avoid inefficient building and basement layouts that adversely impact streetscape quality.

#### Controls

a) Development proposing buildings of 6–8 storeys must be located on sites with a minimum street frontage of 20 metres

b) Site amalgamation is required where necessary to achieve the minimum frontage width specified in C1.

For corner sites:

c) A minimum frontage of 20 metres is required to the primary street

d) The secondary frontage must be of sufficient dimension to achieve appropriate building separation, articulation, solar access and landscape outcomes

e) Development on sites that do not meet the minimum frontage requirement is not supported where it would result in:

i. non-compliance with the requirements of the ADG, where applicable

ii. inadequate solar access, natural ventilation or visual or acoustic privacy for adjoining residents

iii. insufficient provision of deep soil planting zones, landscaping and tree canopy

iv. excessive building bulk or scale resulting in adverse streetscape outcomes, including visually dominant built form, or excessive basement openings and garage interfaces.

#### Building heights

a) The maximum Height of Building (HOB) that can be achieved on a site is shown on the RLEP Height of Building Map

b) Additional building height may be permitted under Housing SEPP legislation, where Low and Mid Rise and other affordable housing bonuses may apply subject to certain exemptions from the legislation, and the proposed development must satisfy compliance with mandatory design requirements

c) The maximum number of storeys permitted on a site must comply with the following:

i. on sites with a maximum HOB of 9.5m – 3 storeys

ii. on sites with a maximum HOB of 12m – 3 storeys

iii. on sites with a maximum HOB of 16.5m and 17.5m – 5 storeys

iv. on sites with a maximum HOB of 19.5m – 6 storeys

v. on sites with a maximum HOB of 23m and 24m – 7 storeys

vi. on sites with a maximum HOB of 26m – 8 storeys

d) Where a property is identified by Council to be flood affected, this may require a ground floor habitable space to be raised above the existing ground level (above the 1 in 100-year flood level, plus 0.5m freeboard). In such cases, the additional height should be absorbed into the overall height of the building, whilst maintaining ADG floor to ceiling standards and not exceeding the required LEP maximum building height. In this case the full number of storeys stated in c) above may not be able to be achieved.  Council may at its discretion consider a minor exceedance in height where required to meet the Floor Planning Flood Level.

#### Definition

Under RLEP, the ‘maximum building height’ is defined as: The vertical distance between resultant ground floor height and the highest point of the building, including plant rooms, lift overruns, but excluding communication devices, antennae, satellite dishes, masts, flagpoles, chimneys, flues and the like.

#### Street walls

a) When an existing street wall height is established by a heritage or contributory building or there is a predominant existing street or city block street wall, new buildings are to respond to the street wall height in the design of the new building

b) Where there is no established street wall height, new buildings are to establish a street wall in accordance with the desired future character of the precinct or city block, and according to the setback requirements in Table 2 and Figure 5.

#### Building setbacks Explanation

Two types of building setback controls apply to new development in medium density residential areas within the Randwick LGA:

#### Apartment Design Guide (ADG) Building Separation Requirements:

The NSW Government Apartment Design Guide (ADG) sets minimum building separation distances for residential flat buildings and shop top housing that are three or more storeys in height and contain four or more dwellings.

Applicable development must demonstrate compliance with the ADG, with separation distances determined by the height and relationship of adjoining buildings, and the type of rooms affected (habitable or non-habitable). Refer to Section 3.7 Building Separation of this DCP Part.

#### Randwick Local Setback Controls:

In addition to ADG requirements, Randwick City Council specifies minimum building setbacks for the front, side and rear of development, including upper-level setbacks, to ensure appropriate streetscape presentation, amenity, and landscape outcomes. Refer to Section 4.3 Setbacks of this DCP Part.

#### Objectives

1\. Ensure new development in Low and Mid Rise (LMR) areas provide an appropriate transition in scale, height and bulk to adjoining lower density development

2\. Protect the amenity of existing 1–2 storey dwellings, including solar access, privacy and outlook

3\. Minimise visual bulk and overshadowing impacts at side and rear boundaries.

#### Controls

a) Developments must provide building separation in accordance with the ADG, to ensure appropriate levels of solar access, limit overshadowing, provide adequate natural ventilation, an appropriate transition in building scale, and ensure that visual and acoustic privacy standards are met

b) Development in LMR areas and Transition Areas must provide a clear transition in height and bulk to adjoining 1–2 storey development

c) Where development adjoins land containing existing 1–2 storey dwellings:

i. The building height at the side and rear boundary interface must not exceed 2–3 storeys (8–10m) within the first 6m of the boundary

ii. Additional storeys above this height must be stepped back a minimum of 3m from the lower built form

iii. Upper levels (above 4 storeys) are to be located toward the centre of the site to reduce visual prominence

d) Side and rear setbacks must:

i. Meet or exceed the minimum ADG building separation distances

ii. Be increased where necessary to protect solar access to adjoining properties, particularly to living areas and private open space

iii. Provide sufficient space for deep soil planting and canopy trees

e) Development must demonstrate that:

i. At least 3 hours of solar access between 9am and 3pm is retained to the principal private open space and living area windows of adjoining dwellings (where feasible)

ii. Direct overlooking is minimised through building separation, setbacks, screening or layout

f) Front, side and rear setbacks are to comply with Section 4.3 Setbacks and be designed as part of an integrated built form and landscape response that reduces bulk and improves streetscape character

g) Where pedestrian links or street setback strips are required (refer to Section 3.2 Through-site Links / Mid-block Connections of this DCP), these areas are to be dedicated to Council at no cost as part of the development. This requirement will be imposed as a condition of development consent

h) For the purposes of calculating floor space ratio (FSR) and deep soil zones, the site area is to be based on the original site area prior to the dedication of land required for pedestrian links and setback strips

i) Where development results in an exposed party wall, the wall is to be treated to enhance visual amenity and reduce adverse streetscape impacts. Treatments may include high-quality architectural articulation, vertical landscaping, or public art (subject to Council approval), and are to be designed as an integrated component of the building

j) Treatments must be durable and capable of being maintained over time, and are to remain in place until the adjoining site is redeveloped.

#### Building depth

a) The residential component of a development is to have a maximum building depth of 20m, including balconies. A maximum building depth of 22m, may be permitted on merit, subject to ADG compliance for floor to ceiling height, solar access and cross ventilation and where it contributes positively to internal amenity and environmental performance.

#### Note

Building depth refers to the dimension measured from the front to the back of a building's floorplate. It has a significant influence on internal residential amenity such as access to light and air. For residential development, narrower building depths generally have a greater potential to achieve optimal natural ventilation and solar access than deeper floor plates.
