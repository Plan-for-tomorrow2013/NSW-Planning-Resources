### 5.5. Building façade

#### Explanation

The treatment and detailing of building facades play a critical role in shaping the perceived scale, proportion and architectural quality of a development. Well designed facades contribute positively to the character of the streetscape and quality of the public domain. A skilful façade design involves the thoughtful arrangement of building elements, including openings, textures, materials and colours. These elements should be arranged to reflect the building’s internal layout and structural logic while providing strong visual interest and reducing the apparent bulk and scale of the development. High‑quality façade design should respond to the surrounding built context and contribute to a cohesive and legible streetscape, while also enhancing residential amenity through appropriate articulation, shading and environmental performance.

#### Note

For heritage items or buildings in Heritage Conservation Areas (HCA), it may be desirable to distinguish old and new works. Refer to the Heritage section of this DCP for further details.

#### Objectives

The objectives for building façades are to:

1\. Ensure building facades are well articulated to contribute positively to the streetscape, reduce perceived bulk and reinforce the scale and character of the surrounding neighbourhood

2\. Require façade design to respond to the existing and desired future character of the area through composition, materials and detailing

3\. Encourage contemporary and innovative architectural design that enhances the visual quality of development and contributes to a coherent and evolving neighbourhood character, particularly in areas undergoing change.

#### Controls

a) Development must be designed to address all street and laneway frontages with active, articulated and visually engaging façades that contribute positively to the public domain

b) Buildings are to be oriented so that the primary front wall alignments are generally parallel to the street property boundary or respond to the established street pattern

c) Building facades must be articulated to reflect the function of the building, present a human scale, and contribute to the proportions and visual character of the streetscape. This may be achieved through a combination of the following design strategies:

i. Defining a base, middle and top section that responds to its overall scale and massing

ii. Expressing the internal layout or structural system of the building through visible elements, such as columns, beams, floor slabs and party walls or structural bays

iii. Providing a coherent arrangement of window types and openings that reflect the internal uses (for example, a living room window versus a bathroom window) and enhance façade rhythm and composition

iv. Incorporating balconies or terraces that respond to the residential amenity, building orientation, outlook including cantilevered balconies, partially or fully recessed balconies, and Juliet-style elements or French balconies as appropriate

v. Designing balustrades to reflect the type and location of the balconies. Carefully consider whether balconies should be transparent or solid, considering the integration of air conditioning units and the required level of solar access and privacy

vi. Incorporating weather and sun protection devices appropriate to the orientation of the building elevation, including eaves, awnings, hoods, louvres, pergolas

vii. Highlighting building entries through architectural elements such as canopies, porticos or awnings

viii. Expressing vertical circulation elements (such as stairwells and lift cores) through façade articulation, with recesses, projecting elements, blade walls, bays and the like

ix. using a coordinated palette of materials, finishes and textures to create visual interest and cohesion

x. Incorporating integrated planting such as vertical gardens or green walls (landscape planting mounted on building elevations) where appropriate

d) Development must avoid large, continuous and unarticulated blank walls. Building elevations are to be articulated to provide visual interest and reduce perceived bulk, including by dividing building facades into sections, bays or modules of exceeding 10m in length, and/or by incorporating changes in wall plane, such as recesses, projections or staggered elements

e) Building services, including pipes and downpipes, are to be integrated into the building design and concealed within the fabric of the building such as within balcony slabs, drop panels, columns, or wall cladding to maintain a high quality façade presentation

f) Alterations and additions to existing residential flat buildings must achieve a cohesive and integrated architectural design outcome. New works are to be designed to be consistent with or complement the existing building in terms of materials, finishes and detailing; maintain a unified façade composition and architectural expression and ensure the development is perceived as a single, coordinated building form.
