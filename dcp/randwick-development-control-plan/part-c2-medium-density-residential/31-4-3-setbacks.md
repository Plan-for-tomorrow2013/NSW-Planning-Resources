### 4.3. Setbacks

#### Explanation

Setbacks define the outer extremities of the building envelope, defined by the front, side and rear boundaries. The front setback control is formulated to maintain any established building alignment and proportions of the street. Side and rear setbacks are devised to ensure an adequate level of building separation, and to provide for access, landscaping, privacy and natural lighting and ventilation for both the new development and the adjoining properties.

#### Definition

Setback distances are measured perpendicular (that is, at 90 degrees angle) from the boundary to the outer face of the building elevation, excluding eaves, gutters, semi-basement car park, terraces, decks or landings not more than 1.2m above ground level (finished) and minor projecting features, such as awnings, sun hoods, screening devices and bay windows.

#### Note

Setbacks are to be in accordance with TfNSW requirements in relation to classified roads and signalised intersections.

#### Objectives

The objectives for setbacks are to:

1\. Define the street edge and establish or maintain consistent rhythm of street setbacks and landscaped front gardens that contribute to the scale and local character of the streetscape

2\. Ensure new development responds appropriately to the established street wall height including those defined by a heritage or contributory buildings or prevailing built form within a street block

3\. Ensure adequate separation between buildings for visual and acoustic privacy, solar access, air circulation to support landscaping in deep soil and tree canopy

4\. Reserve contiguous areas for the retention or creation of open space and deep soil planting

5\. Minimise visual bulk and scale as viewed from neighbouring properties and the public domain.

6\. Ensure new development that includes apartments (RFB and shop top) of three or more storeys, with four or more dwellings, complies with the ADG requirements for building separation

7\. Require new development in Low and Mid Rise (LMR) areas, and other Transition Areas, are to address the significant scale change from the existing 1 and 2 storey dwellings in adjoining properties e.g. existing houses, to the potential new 6 to 8 storeys of new LMR apartment redevelopment. New development is to moderate the scale difference through including building front, side, rear and upper-level setbacks in accordance with Tables 2, 3 and 4, and Figures 5, 6 and 10

8\. Ensure side, front and rear setbacks contribute to an appropriate built form, scale in the streetscape, maintain privacy, reduce overshadowing and support a coherent relationship between buildings and the public domain.
