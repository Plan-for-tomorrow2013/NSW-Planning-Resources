### 3.7. Building separation

#### Explanation

Building separation i.e. building-to-building setbacks, is a fundamental planning control that contributes to quality, liveability and environmental performance of medium density residential development. , Appropriate separation distances support access to sunlight and ventilation, protect privacy and reduce visual bulk of development as experienced from adjoining properties and the public domain.

#### Objectives

The objective of building separation controls is to protect future and neighbouring resident amenity, including:

1\. Ensure adequate access to sunlight for living areas and communal open space

2\. Facilitate natural cross ventilation to dwellings

3\. Protect visual and acoustic privacy for habitable rooms, private outdoor spaces (such as balconies, terraces and communal open spaces)

4\. Minimise overshadowing of habitable rooms, communal open space and the public domain

5\. Reduce visual bulk and scale as viewed from adjoining properties and surrounding streets.

#### Controls

a) New development for residential flat buildings and shop-top housing that are three storeys or more storeys, and contain four or more dwellings, must comply with the Apartment Design Guide (ADG) Part 2F Building separation as follows:

i. Up to four storeys (approx. 12m):

- 12m between habitable room windows

- 9m between habitable to non-habitable room windows

- 6m between non-habitable room windows

ii. Five to eight storeys (approx. 25m):

- 18m between habitable room windows

- 12m between habitable and non-habitable room windows

- 9m between non-habitable room windows

iii. Nine storeys and above (over 25m):

- 24m between habitable room windows

- 18m between habitable and non-habitable room windows

- 12m between non-habitable room windows

b) In addition to the ADG requirements development must comply with the side setback controls specified in Table 3 and Figure 6

c) Development must be designed to protect the visual and acoustic privacy of adjoining properties. Building elevations are to incorporate appropriate articulation and design measures, including:

i. careful placement of windows and balconies

ii. use of recessed and projecting elements (e.g. sawtooth façades)

iii. screening devices or other suitable treatments

d) Each façade is to be designed holistically to ensure privacy is achieved at all potential interfaces

e) Where site constraints or irregular site conditions exist, variations to the above controls may be considered on merit in exceptional circumstances where it can be demonstrated that the development achieves equivalent or improved outcomes in relation to:

i. visual and acoustic privacy

ii. solar access and overshadowing

iii. natural ventilation

iv. visual bulk and building massing.
