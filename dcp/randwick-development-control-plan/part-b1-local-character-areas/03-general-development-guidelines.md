## 3. General Development Guidelines

To inform the outcomes of desired future character, development across all Local Character Areas must satisfy the following controls:

a) Locality

i. Development affecting heritage items, contributory buildings and heritage conservation areas must retain and conserve significant built form, setting and streetscape elements, including original building fabric, architectural detailing, and established streetscape character

ii. Development in proximity to, or adjoining, heritage items, contributory buildings and heritage conservation areas must be designed to respond to and complement their identified significance and setting. This includes consideration of building scale, height, massing, setbacks, proportions, architectural detailing, materiality and streetscape relationships, ensuring new development does not dominate or diminish the significance of the heritage context

iii. Development must reflect the historical development pattern and architectural character of the locality

iv. Ensure that new development does not negatively impact the public domain, public amenity and the enjoyment of public spaces through excessive overshadowing, inappropriate driveway location, vehicle and pedestrian conflict, reduced casual surveillance, and reduced impacts on walkability and opportunities for active transport

v. Sightlines and connections beyond the site boundaries are important considerations for building placement, orientation, paths of movement and framed views

vi. Minimise impacts on residential amenity by managing overlooking and, where reasonable, limiting the loss of significant views from surrounding properties.

b) Built form elements

i. Development must respect the predominant building height and streetscape presentation, incorporating hipped, pitched or gable roof forms where these present as dominant character elements in the streetscape and wider Local Character Area

ii. In low density residential areas, first floor massing must be setback from the ground floor front building facade to minimise visual bulk at upper levels and ensure the building presents as two distinct levels, with a dominant ground floor and a recessive upper level, to maintain compatibility with single storey dwellings in the streetscape

iii. In low density residential areas, basement or lower ground levels must be designed to respond to site topography and must not result in the presentation of three storey street facade

iv. Development must minimise demolition of building elements that contribute to the character of the streetscape and local character area, including original facades, roof forms, materials and architectural detailing

v. Alterations and additions must retain the predominant scale, massing and original roof form of the front elevation as viewed from the primary street frontage

vi. To ensure modern contemporary designs integrate with traditional pitched and gabled built roof forms, in residential areas, flat roof buildings shall feature slender roof profiles to minimise building and visual massing at roof level

vii. Development is required to respond to prominent datum lines represented by original and intact streetscapes to ensure visual continuity between buildings

viii. In residential areas, development on larger lots must provide front setbacks that are consistent with the prevailing streetscape and of sufficient depth to accommodate landscape treatment that reinforces the established character of the locality

ix. Development in areas subject to increased building heights or density, including where planning controls permit mid-rise or higher density housing, must:

- demonstrate a graduated transition in built form scale, height and density between higher intensity development and adjoining lower density residential areas

- be designed so that taller elements are set away from lower scale interfaces, with building height and bulk stepping down towards adjoining properties

- incorporate articulated building forms, modulation and recessive upper levels to reduce perceived bulk and maintain a human-scale streetscape

- provide setbacks and landscaping that reinforce the established street rhythm, subdivision pattern and landscape character, even where building height and density increase

- ensure that where a change in built form is anticipated, development contributes to an evolving local character, by: o maintaining consistent building alignment and street definition o reflecting the established grain, spacing and proportions of development o integrating landscape elements, including canopy planting, that maintain continuity of the streetscape character.

c) Landscaping

i. Retain mature street trees and expand canopy tree planting within public and private land to strengthen streetscape character, enhance residential amenity, and increase urban tree canopy coverage, reinforcing green grid and biodiversity connections throughout the Local Character Area

ii. Retain and enhance existing landscapes, their topography and natural ground levels

iii. Design built form and landscapes that minimise the use of cut and fill

iv. Retain existing retaining walls in the public and private domain where they form part of the historical subdivision pattern, respond to the natural topography, or contribute to the built form character of the locality

v. Fencing shall be low and transparent to maintain visual connection between the street and the dwellings, including gardens

vi. Retain and enhance generous front and rear setbacks in residential areas to accommodate deep soil zones and canopy tree planting, supporting continuous urban tree canopy, green links between street blocks, and reinforcing the established subdivision pattern and landscape character of the locality.

d) Materiality

i. Ensure high quality and contemporary design references without mimicking existing building design and materiality

ii. Where masonry construction, stucco rendering, sandstone, slate, and terracotta roof tiles are characteristic of the street or Local Character Area, new development is to incorporate these materials to reinforce and maintain the established local character

iii. Masonry (brick) facades are to be implemented, notably at street facing ground level, where this forms the predominant material of the streetscape

iv. Fences shall be constructed of materials and a style that is complementary to the dwelling architectural style and era, as well as predominant material and style within the streetscape.

e) Car parking and movement

i. Car parking in the front setback is discouraged where it requires significant amendments to an original dwelling, fence and gardens that make positive contributions to the streetscape

ii. In residential areas, garages and carports are to be designed and sited as visually recessive elements that do not dominate the dwelling or primary street elevation. Their location, scale and materials must respond to the established streetscape character and contribute positively to the architectural rhythm of the area

iii. Basement parking in residential areas must be non-discernible from the street

iv. Ensure development contributes to a safe, legible and well-connected network of pedestrian and cycle routes within the Local Character Area by providing and enhancing connections to surrounding streets, open spaces and key destinations, supporting the broader active transport network.

f) Land use and subdivision

i. New development must respond to the established development pattern by having regard to prevailing lot sizes and configuration, setbacks, and building siting, including alignment with typical building footprints and envelopes

ii. New development must reinforce the scale, rhythm and orientation of surrounding development, having regard to the prevailing subdivision pattern, and ensure that building width, alignment and form are consistent with this pattern when viewed from the street.

g) Mixed-use and non-residential development

i. Ensure ground floor retail and commercial uses that reinforce the established land use character of each centre

ii. Development is to retain original parapets contributing to the streetscape and building. Where retention is not feasible, new parapets are to replicate the form, scale and rhythm of existing parapets in the streetscape

iii. Flat-roofed buildings must feature well-articulated parapet designs along street-facing elevations

iv. Flat roof buildings must reflect and enhance the distinctive architectural character of commercial buildings within the Local Character Area.
