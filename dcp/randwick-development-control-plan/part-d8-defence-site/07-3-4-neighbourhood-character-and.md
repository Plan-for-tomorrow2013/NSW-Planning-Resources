### 3.4. Neighbourhood character and architectural quality

Objectives

1\. Ensure buildings are of high visual quality, enhance the streetscape and complement good quality development in the neighbourhood

2\. Promote high quality contemporary architectural designs that avoid inappropriate historical copies and inappropriately remote styles

3\. Integrate appropriate environmental design and building orientation with architectural character

4\. Arrange buildings in a manner that addresses the street and enhances personal and property safety and security

5\. Ensure that garages, parking structures and parking areas are located and designed so they do not dominate the street frontage

6\. Ensure fences on street frontages are designed to address the amenity of the street, surveillance and safety, security of private property, and the use of front garden space.

Controls Building appearance and neighbourhood character

a) Building fronts and entries are to be readily apparent from the street and convey a sense of address. Buildings fronting the public street must have their main entrance and windows from some habitable rooms facing the street. Building detailing and articulation must enable individual dwellings to be identified from the street

b) Buildings are to be aligned predominantly parallel to the street boundary and predominantly to the street setback line

c) Building facades are to provide environmental amenity through sun shading devices, privacy screens and noise barriers combined with useable outdoor areas

d) Where the ground floor units of multi-unit housing address the street, at least 50% of those ground floor units are to have a separate direct entrance from the street

e) The maximum unarticulated building length is 9m along the primary street frontage (Figure 5) and 15.6m for secondary street frontages (Figure 5). Punctuation by bay windows, verandas, balconies or wall offsets is considered to be adequate articulation

f) At least one third of the face area of podium walls more than 1.2m above ground are faced with mechanically fixed sandstone (anticipates 100mm to 75mm minimum thickness). Adhesive fixing must not be used

g) Building facades to streets are to incorporate the following design characteristics:

i. Well-proportioned and spaced windows appropriate to their orientation

ii. Architectural features at ground level that reinforce dwelling address such as entrance porches

iii. Well balanced projected and recessed sections of balconies

iv. Use of appropriate environmental controls such as verandas, sliding screens, window hoods and the like

v. Coordinated and compatible materials and finishes where neutral colours predominate with strong colours limited to accent elements up to a maximum of 10% of the façade area

h) Building design is to achieve the architectural consistency principles indicated on Figure 4 . “Integrated manner”, where referred to on Figure 4, means that development shall be designed to be compatible with the existing development on adjoining sites within the relevant block or with future development in accordance with this section on those adjoining sites

i) Buildings should avoid the use of applied historical façade elements and their combination

j) Buildings should generally accord with existing or future neighbouring developments in terms of:

i. Wall and building heights

ii. Setbacks

iii. Scale of elements

iv. Overly discordant building forms are discouraged.

Roof design

k) Solar collectors, mechanical plant (including lift plant), communications devices, water storage tanks and other similar elements located on roofs must be either flush with the roof or integrated into the built form, so they are screened from view from the public street.

Garages

l) Carports and garages fronting public streets are to:

i) Have a maximum opening width of 6 m or 1/3 of the width of the lot, whichever is less

ii) Be set at least 1m behind the street edge of the primary building zone.

m) Carports and garages are to be located off private rear lanes or in basements where lots have frontage width less than 9m

n) Carports and garages fronting public streets are to be integrated with building design. Fences

o) Solid fences facing the street or between the street and the primary building zone are to be no higher than 1.2m. This may be increased to 1.8m where the fence has openings that make it at least 50% transparent

p) Solid front fences up to a maximum of 1.8m are only permissible where the site fronts Avoca Street. Design of such fences should be modelled and integrated with landscape treatment and appropriately signify building entry

q) Solid fences to walkways, easements and lanes (that are not also primary frontages) are to be no higher than 1.8m.
