### 2.2. Balcony dining over footpaths

Explanation The use of airspace above footpaths, roads and public spaces by the conversion of existing awnings to balconies for the purpose of first floor dining is generally not encouraged by Council and requires the creation of a strata lot on the footpath.

Note

A DA is required for any development proposal to use the footpath airspace, including the creation of a strata lot for above ground dining space proposals. A lease from the relevant road authority is required for use of the airspace above a road owned by it. Council is the roads authority for local and public roads except for classified roads where the authority is Transport for NSW.

Proposals for the construction of a new first floor balcony awning and use for outdoor dining must be located within an E1- Local centre or E2- Commercial Centre zone only. An applicant must demonstrate compliance with the following criteria:

• Has a current approval for first floor dining in the contiguous indoor premises

• Is not located adjacent to a residential premises or residential zone

• Is located in a continuous retail strip, where awnings are an integral feature of the architectural elements of the streetscape.

Objectives The outdoor balcony dining objectives are to:

1\. Allow for the use of airspace over public roads and public land for dining, where appropriate

2\. Retain the amenity of the existing streetscape and promote safety of pedestrians and vehicles

3\. Consider the heritage significance of an item or conservation area, where applicable.

Controls

a) Design of any balcony for outdoor dining must contribute positively to the streetscape and be compatible with the architectural integrity of the building

b) Applications for use of balconies for outdoor dining must demonstrate that the proposed design and construction:

i. Will not adversely affect the heritage significance of a heritage item or a heritage conservation area

ii. Will not adversely affect the amenity and safety of the footpath below or the adjacent road

iii. Will not obstruct the visibility of traffic signs and signals or pedestrian crossings

iv. Will not hinder the use by pedestrians and vehicles of the footpath and adjacent road

v. Will not adversely affect the amenity and safety of adjoining properties in terms of noise, overlooking or security

vi. Will not adversely affect views

vii. Is not located on a corner site unless visibility, traffic, safety and aesthetic issues are not adversely affected.

Note

Awnings over public land must comply with Section 142(1) of the Roads Act 1993 requiring owners to maintain them in a satisfactory state of repair.
