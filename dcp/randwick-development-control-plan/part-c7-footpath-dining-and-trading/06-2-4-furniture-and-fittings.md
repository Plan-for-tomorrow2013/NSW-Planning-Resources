### 2.4. Furniture and fittings

Controls General requirements Applications for outdoor dining must include details of proposed furniture and fittings. These proposals must demonstrate that:

a) All furniture and fittings can be comfortably accommodated within the approved footpath trading area, allowing adequate space for patron comfort and pedestrian movement

b) Items are safe, sturdy, (but not bulky), waterproof and weather resistant, and easily removable at the close of business each day. They must not damage the footpath or

other public infrastructure or pose a trip/fall hazard or cause obstruction to the public

c) Items such as umbrellas or A-frames must be appropriately weighted down or otherwise secured to prevent dislodgement by wind or accidental contact

d) Furniture must visually complement and be physically aligned with existing street furniture (including adjacent footpath trading areas) and adjacent public utilities

e) The footpath trading area may be defined by using removable elements such as planter boxes and flowerpots, bollards or screens provided they:

- do not exceed 1.2m in height and 1.8m in length

- are located entirely within the approved trading area

- are removed at the close of business or otherwise designed as an integrated feature of the public domain

- are supplied, installed and maintained at the

applicant’s expense

f) Full height solid or plastic screens or any form of enclosure that fully separates the trading area from the public domain are not permitted

g) All furniture and fittings must comply with Part B12

– Signage and Advertising.

Note

Design details of the proposed furniture shall be submitted to the Council for approval as part of the DA. Fully enclosed café structures are not encouraged. This can privatise the public place which results in reduced accessibility, visibility, amenity and safety to the public. Council reserves the right to require the replacement of inappropriate tables and chairs as a condition of the licence.

Umbrellas Where umbrellas are proposed as part of outdoor dining, they must comply with the following controls:

h) Umbrellas must have a minimum vertical clearance of 2.6 metres from the footpath when open, and be consistent with adjacent umbrellas or shade structures (if applicable)

i) Umbrellas must not obstruct views of:

- traffic signals, signs or pedestrian crossings

- heritage facades or valued streetscape vistas where such views and contribute to the character of the area

j) Umbrellas must be securely anchored using a safe fixed anchor point that does not pose a trip hazard when the umbrella is not in use. Anchoring systems must be approved by Council and must not damage public infrastructure

k) Umbrellas must not overhang any part of the roadway

l) Umbrellas must be constructed from durable, weather resistant and fire-retardant materials, particularly if located near heating devices. Only market style umbrellas are permitted. Beach-style umbrellas are not acceptable due to their instability and informal appearance

m) Umbrellas must not be installed where a building awning exists, unless it can be demonstrated that both can be safely and appropriately accommodated without cluttering the public domain or obstructing pedestrian movement.
