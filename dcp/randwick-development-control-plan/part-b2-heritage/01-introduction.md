## 1. Introduction

Randwick City’s heritage is rich and diverse and includes buildings, structures, Aboriginal and archaeological sites, parks and reserves. They are valued because they are associated with significant phases of history, or with important people and events in Randwick’s development and cultural heritage. Collectively, this heritage contributes to the community’s cultural life, sense of place, and shared identity, fostering pride and continuity across generations. It is important to recognise that the legal owners of a state or local heritage-listed property are not merely owners, but custodians entrusted with the care of a significant cultural asset. While they have a legal responsibility, they also have a unique opportunity to help preserve and celebrate the significance and character of these places for future generations. By maintaining and respecting heritage features, owners contribute to a shared sense of identity and help keep the stories of our community alive. Heritage is a living part of our environment, and its preservation is a collaborative effort that benefits everyone. This section of the DCP applies to heritage listed items and heritage conservation areas (HCAs) identified under Schedule 5 of the Randwick Local Environmental Plan 2012 (RLEP) and should be read in conjunction with other sections of the DCP for specific development types, locations or sites, if relevant to the application. Where there are inconsistencies between this section and any other DCP sections, this section will prevail.

#### General objectives

The general objectives of this section of the DCP are to:

1\. Retain and conserve

Randwick’s rich and diverse environmental heritage .

2\. Provide detailed guidelines to manage change to the environmental heritage of Randwick as defined and identified in RLEP

3\. Ensure development proposals consider the impacts to the cultural significance of heritage items, HCAs, archaeological sites, and places of Aboriginal cultural significance.
