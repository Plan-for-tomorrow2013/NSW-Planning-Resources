### 2.6. Detailing

Explanation The significant features and elements of a heritage item or heritage conservation area are often expressed through fine details such as windows, doors, decorative timberwork, metalwork, brickwork, stonework and cement render. These elements contribute to the cultural significance and distinctive character of the place and are to be carefully conserved. Objectives

1\. Ensure that significant fabric, and both internal and external features and detailing, are retained and maintained in good repair

2\. Ensure the reinstatement of original architectural elements and detailing where these have been lost

3\. Ensure alterations, additions and new development incorporate detailing that is appropriate to the architectural character and style of the heritage item or heritage conservation area setting

4\. Ensure the pattern, proportions, and scale of door and window openings are consistent with original fenestration and detailing of the original heritage fabric

5\. Ensure that the alteration to, and addition of, window and door grilles, bars and shutters are visually discreet and do not detract from the character of the building.

Controls All development

a) Only detailing that is known to have been part of the original building is acceptable. Decorative elements that were never part of the original design are not to be introduced

b) Roller shutters will not be permitted to windows or doors

c) External security bars/ grilles and shutters on front doors and windows are discouraged. Where there is no alternative, the installation of external security bars is permitted provided they consist of simple unembellished rectangular bars in a vertical pattern or a pattern that reflects the configuration of the glazing bars of the window or door frame. They should be painted a dark recessive colour or preferably the same colour as the door to reduce visibility

d) Clear plastic security film may be attached to the internal face of glazing. Heritage items and contributory buildings

e) Retain and repair significant fabric and internal and external features. This included but is not limited to chimneys, hearth and fireplaces, mantels, ceilings, staircases, balustrade, doors, windows, original sunhoods, awnings, gable detailing, verandahs and balconies, and other decorative elements to principal elevations. Original leadlight and coloured glass panes should also be retained and repaired

f) Where original windows, doors and façade detailing have been removed and replaced with modern materials, consideration should be given to reconstructing these original features based on available evidence

g) Authentic reconstruction of lost elements is encouraged. Decorative elements must not be introduced unless documentary or physical evidence indicates the decorative elements previously existed. Thorough research should be undertaken before attempting to reconstruct lost detail and elements

h) Alterations and additions should incorporate new doors and windows which are compatible with the position, size, and proportions and detailing with original windows and doors

i) Alterations and additions should complement the significant fabric and generally be less elaborate than the original, to ensure that the primacy of the original design is maintained.

Non-Contributory Buildings

j) Decorative elements, detailing, or features should be restrained and not replicate or imitate inappropriate or inauthentic heritage architectural/design style. Any new detailing must be clearly distinguishable from that of heritage items and contributory buildings.
