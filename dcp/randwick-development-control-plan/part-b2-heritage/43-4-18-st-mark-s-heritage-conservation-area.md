### 4.18. St Mark’s Heritage Conservation Area

A fine collection of residential buildings, including nineteenth century villas and terraces and twentieth century cottages, adjacent to an important area of remnant bushland. The area extends west from Glebe Gully to include parts of Dutruc Street, St Marks Road, Rae Street and Wood Street, Randwick.

#### 4.18.1. Character values

Development must retain and enhance the outcomes expressed in the statement of significance for the HCA and as summarised below.

a. Landscape and public domain elements - Glebe gully, now Fred Hollows Reserve has natural heritage value as a rare surviving example of a well vegetated watercourse in an intensively developed residential area. Maintain heritage landscape elements and schemes. Retain existing street planting and internal views and vistas

b. Subdivision - Varied subdivision pattern including larger and smaller lots. Glebe gully, now Fred Hollows Reserve has natural heritage value as a rare surviving example of a well vegetated watercourse in an intensively developed residential area

c. Scale and Form - Diversity of scale including two storey villas and single storey detached, semi-detached and attached dwellings

d. Siting and Setbacks - Diversity of setbacks including smaller setbacks for cottages and larger setbacks for villas. Maintain the existing setting of the heritage item, informed by the historic pattern, form, scale, bulk, setback and height

e. Roofs - Traditional pitched roofs

f. Materials - Walls are stucco for Victorian buildings, face brickwork for Federation buildings. Terracotta tiles, slate roofing. Original fabric is highly significant and should be maintained

w) Detailing - Decorative metalwork and timberwork. Protect inlaid concrete footpath signage and encourage provision for street names embedded in footpaths. Maintain the historic aesthetic/character of the item and area (e.g. paint scheme, materiality, style, landscape elements). Retain of existing decorative elements and colour schemes

g. Verandahs and Balconies - Verandahs and balconies integral to each of the architectural styles which are represented in the area

h. Carparking - Wider lots provide access for carparking to the rear. Narrower lots do not allow for onsite carparking

i. Fences - Front fencing is sympathetic to style of dwelling.

#### 4.18.2. Guidelines for change

Alterations and additions Alterations and additions should respond to the existing pattern of development, with careful consideration of the setting (form, scale, bulk, setback and height). New alterations and additions should respect the historic aesthetic/character of the item and area (e.g. paint scheme, materiality, style, landscape elements). Future uses for this HCA should be compatible with its historical functions/ associations. The St Marks heritage conservation area comprises a range of building types including single storey and two storey villas, two storey terraces and attached and semi-detached cottages. Rear additions should not be prominent in the streetscape nor compromise the integrity of the original roof. Rear additions to attached and semi-detached cottages should be consistent with the scale and form of surrounding rear wings. Outbuildings to the rear The scale and bulk of outbuildings to the rear should not dominate the main building on the site. Outbuildings should be of a 1 ½ storey scale with upper floor accommodation within available attic space. The maximum wall height of outbuildings is to be 3.5m and roof pitch is to be consistent with that of the main building on the site. Carparking Most of the properties within the conservation area have rear lane access allowing for carparking at the rear of the site. Where rear lane access is available, carparking to the front or side of the property will not be supported. New development must not incorporate new driveways, garages, carports, or carspaces across the front boundary, forward of the front building line, where these elements are not supported by historic evidence and uncharacteristic of the area’s established streetscape.
