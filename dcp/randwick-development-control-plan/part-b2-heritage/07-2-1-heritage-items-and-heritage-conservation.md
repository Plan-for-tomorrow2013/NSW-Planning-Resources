## 2. Development controls

### 2.1. Heritage items and Heritage Conservation Areas

Explanation This section contains objectives and controls to protect and enhance Randwick City’s heritage items and HCAs. It aims to ensure that development affecting heritage items or properties within HCAs or within the vicinity of an item or a HCA is sympathetic to the heritage values by achieving a balanced approach between contemporary design expectations, environmental sustainability, embodied energy, character, and protecting cultural significance. Buildings and sites within HCAs are identified on the DCP map as being contributory, neutral, or detracting to the character and significance of the HCA. Contributory buildings provide good evidence of the main development period(s) and make a positive contribution to the character and/or heritage significance of HCAs. They have a collective significance, and their retention is essential if the character of the area is to be maintained. Non-contributory (neutral and detracting) buildings may have limited historic values but nonetheless respects the surrounding context or display qualities which do not add to the character of the heritage conservation area. They are not to be considered as a precedent for new work when assessing the merit of an application. Non-contributory buildings may be demolished and replaced by new development sympathetic to the character of the heritage conservation area. Objectives

1\. P

rotect and enhance Randwick City’s heritage items and HCAs

2\. Ensure development and alterations and additions to heritage items and/or within an HCA respects and does not distort, obscure or detract from the cultural significance of the place or its interpretation and appreciation

3\. Ensure the adaptive reuse of heritage items enhances its historic and cultural significance and does not result the unnecessary removal of significant fabric.

Controls All development

a) All new development in an HCA or in the vicinity of a HCA or a heritage item must respect the design, scale and character of neighbouring buildings and the key heritage values of the item and the area

b) All development proposals within, adjacent to, or in the vicinity of a heritage item or HCA must demonstrate that they have considered the likely impact on cultural significance and fabric of the item and/or HCA

c) Proposals to alter or add to a heritage item or a building in an HCA must consider assessment of the significance of individual building elements, the broader area, and the relationship to neighbouring properties and the streetscape

d) Alterations and additions to heritage items and contributory buildings must be designed and sited to retain contributory features of the building and maintain the streetscape character of the HCA

e) A new building, or additions visible from the street or an HCA, must respect and respond to the HCA and streetscape character. A detailed streetscape analysis must be included in the site analysis submitted with any DA

f) When proposing adaptive reuse of a heritage item, the proposal must ensure that the significance and original fabric of the heritage item is not adversely affected by the change of use

g) Alterations and additions to group items must consider the impacts on the entire group, in particular the existing pattern of rear additions and location of alterations.

Neutral buildings

h) Alterations and additions to a neutral building must:

i) remove unsympathetic alterations and additions, including inappropriate building elements

ii) respect the original building ’s bulk, form, scale and height

iii) minimise removal of heritage fabric and building elements

iv) use appropriate and sympathetic materials, finishes and colours that complement the HCA and do not diminish its significance.

i) Demolition of neutral buildings will only be considered where it can be demonstrated:

i) restoration of the building is not reasonable

ii) the replacement building will enhance and not compromise the significance of the HCA.

j) Where demolition of a neutral building is approved, Council may require a photographic archival record of the building to be submitted to Council as a condition of consent.

Detracting buildings

k) Development on sites containing detracting buildings must aim to improve the contribution of the site to the character of the HCA

l) Alterations and additions to, or redevelopment of, detracting buildings must:

i) remove inappropriate and unsympathetic additions and modifications that are intrusive to the significance of the HCA

ii) respect the prevailing bulk, form, scale and height of the surrounding streetscape.

m) Contemporary design is acceptable where it is sympathetic to the character of the HCA and/or nearby heritage items.
