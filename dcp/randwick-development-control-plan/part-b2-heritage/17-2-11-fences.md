### 2.11. Fences

Explanation Front fences are a highly significant element of the streetscape in Heritage Conservation Areas with each architectural style typically associated with a distinctive fence type. These fences contribute to the visual rhythm, character, and historical integrity of the streetscape and form an important element of its heritage significance. Objectives

1\. Ensure the retention, repair and reconstruction of original fencing

2\. Promote fencing that is consistent with and complements the character of original buildings

3\. Support and maintain consistent fencing where these are a defining characteristic/feature of the Heritage Conservation Area

4\. Encourage side and rear boundary fencing that reflects the scale, height and materials of original fencing.

Controls All development

a) New and replacement front fences must not obscure building facades. High solid front fences are not permitted

b) The height and form of new fencing must be appropriate to the character of the heritage item, or the Heritage Conservation Area

c) Lych gates must not be introduced unless there is evidence that they originally and/or historically existed on the site

d) Side fencing forward of the building line must be simple in design, and its level of detailing, materials, and height must be compatible with the heritage item, contributory building or Heritage Conservation Area

e) Side and rear boundary fences should be of traditional timber construction, or where appropriate, masonry.  Colorbond or similar metal sheet fencing is not appropriate.

Heritage items and contributory buildings

f) Original fences and retaining walls should be retained, repaired or reconstructed where possible

g) Where an original fencing has been lost, new fencing should be designed to reflect or replicate the original style based on available evidence.

Non-contributory buildings No additional controls apply beyond those set above.

Note Researching the original fence style may involve reviewing old photographs, architectural drawings, or examining fencing on houses of similar age and style.
