### 2.3. Design and character

Explanation The design of new development should achieve a sympathetic blend of old and new, maintaining the heritage character of the area while allowing for contemporary expression. This can be achieved by respecting the established scale, form, siting, setbacks, materials and finishes of the streetscape without being overly imitative. Careful attention must be paid to adjacent development within the vicinity of heritage items and the character of HCA. Objectives

1\. Promote high quality design that complements and enhances the streetscape character and significance of the heritage item or HCA

2\. Ensure new development does not adversely impact on the setting, streetscape character or views associated with any heritage item or HCA

3\. Ensure additions or changes to the external appearance of heritage items and contributory buildings within HCAs respect the original, built form, architectural style and character

Controls All development

a) Development must demonstrate how it respects or responds to the heritage values of the heritage item or the HCA (including within the statements of significance of the item or the HCA and its key characteristics outlined in Section 4 of this DCP)

b) A streetscape analysis must be prepared to demonstrate how the development has identified, incorporated, and enhanced the contributory character elements and features of the streetscape such as view corridors, built form, scale, setbacks, fencing styles, extent of soft landscaping, significant trees/landscape elements and garages and driveway locations. A streetscape analysis must be prepared and submitted to Council for review at DA stage

c) New development should be consistent with important horizontal elements of buildings in the streetscape, in particular ground floor levels and eaves lines, where appropriate

d) Large blank areas of brick or rendered walls should be avoided. Where this is not possible, contrasting building materials and treatments must be used to break up the visual mass

e) Alterations, extensions, or additions to the building, and its grounds, that are considered visually or architecturally incompatible with the original character of the site must be removed. Unsympathetic elements should be replaced with new works that are designed to be in harmony with the original building style, scale, materials, and detailing. Original features should be reinstated using materials, construction techniques, and forms that closely match or replicate the original design, ensuring the heritage significance and visual integrity of the property are preserved or enhanced.

Heritage items and contributory buildings

f) Street-facing elevations and visible side elevations must not be significantly altered. Additions must be located to the rear or side of the building to minimise visual impact on the streetscape

g) Additions or alterations must complement the existing building in scale, form and detailing while remaining visibly distinguishable from the original fabric on close inspection. New and old design are not to be confused, or the boundaries/junctions blurred.

h) New work must respect and retain the significant heritage fabric, including internal and external features and proportions, chimneys, fireplaces, doors, windows, openings, balconies, and verandas.
