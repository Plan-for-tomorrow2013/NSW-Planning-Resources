### 2.9. Verandahs and balconies

Explanation Verandahs and balconies on street frontages are characteristic features of   Australian architecture that provide a transitional space between the building and the street or public domain. They contribute to shading, visual interest and the depth and articulation of the front façade, and are important to the heritage character of the streetscape. Objectives

1\. Ensure original verandah and balcony forms are retained, and encourage their reinstatement where removed

2\. Ensure alterations and additions do not result in the demolition, removal, obscuring or diminution of the significance of original verandahs and balconies.

Controls All development

a) Where verandahs and balconies are characteristic of the Heritage Conservation Area, new development must incorporate these elements in a manner that is compatible in scale and form with the established streetscape character.

Heritage items and contributory buildings

b) Original front verandahs and balconies must be retained and conserved. This includes elements such as verandah posts and bases, joinery and bargeboards, tiling, cast iron lace, brackets, decorative features and glass panes

c) Verandahs must not be demolished or altered to facilitate, or allow for, car parking

d) Enclosure or infill of front verandahs and balconies is not supported. Original enclosed verandahs and balconies must be reinstated. The enclosure or infill of front verandahs and balconies is not supported. Where original verandahs and balconies have been enclosed, their reinstatement is required

e) New verandahs and balconies must not be sympathetic to original design of the building, and must not compete with its architectural form. Their design should be informed by existing detailing or an understanding of appropriate styles for the building’s period.

Non-Contributory Buildings No additional controls apply beyond those set above.
