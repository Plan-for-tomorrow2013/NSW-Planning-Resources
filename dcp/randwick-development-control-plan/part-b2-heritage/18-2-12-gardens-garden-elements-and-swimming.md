### 2.12. Gardens, garden elements and swimming pools

Explanation Period gardens and landscaped front setbacks enhance the relationship between a dwelling house and its setting, contributing to the streetscape character of Heritage Conservation Areas. These landscaped areas soften views of buildings, highlight architectural features and help screen unsympathetic buildings, including alterations and additions. Objectives

1\. Retain or reinstate landscaped settings and elements, including pathway location and materials, for heritage items and buildings within Heritage Conservation Areas

2\. Encourage front garden areas that are attractive and reflect the character of original houses

3\. Enhance the streetscape character setting of all buildings in the Heritage Conservation Areas and development within the vicinity of heritage items or Heritage Conservation Areas.

Controls All development

a) Significant trees and landscape elements such as pathways, garden beds and structures must be retained

b) Large areas/expanses of hard paving are not permitted. Hard paved areas must be minimised to preserve soft landscaping

c) Garden and ancillary structures must be appropriate in scale, form, style, materials and design to the principal building

d) Swimming pools must be located at the rear of the property and designed to retain significant trees and areas of soft landscaping. Swimming pools must not result in substantial changes to ground levels or loss of tree canopy or urban greenery on the site.

Heritage items and contributory buildings No additional controls apply beyond those set above. Non-contributory buildings No additional controls apply beyond those set above.

Note Guidelines on garden styles and elements typical of different architectural periods are available on Council’s website
