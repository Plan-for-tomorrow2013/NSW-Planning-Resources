### 2.8. Roofs and chimneys

Explanation Roof forms and detailing of heritage items and buildings within a Heritage Conservation Area vary according to building type and architectural style. This diversity contributes significantly to the aesthetic value and visual richness and complexity of heritage items and Heritage Conservation Areas. Fireplaces and chimneys are prominent architectural elements particularly in buildings constructed up to the mid-20th century, and contribute to structural expression and

integrity, local character, and rhythm of the roofscape/skyline.

Objectives

1\. Retain the characteristic roof forms of heritage items and buildings within Heritage Conservation Areas.

Controls All development

a) Attic rooms must be contained within the existing roof form and must not dominate the street elevation or visible side elevation.

Heritage items and contributory buildings

b) Roofs must not be re-pitched or have their eave lines raised to accommodate attic rooms

c) Chimneys and associated elements, including but not limited to fireplaces, must be retained.

Non-contributory buildings

d) Roofs of new development must reflect the predominant roof type (i.e. gabled, hipped), pitch, eaves, and ridge height of the Heritage Conservation Area.

Note Where roofing materials such as tiles or slate, are difficult to source, consider relocating intact materials from less visible areas.  For example, good quality tiles or slates from the rear or sides of the building can replace missing or damaged ones in the front. New materials used at the rear should match the original appearance as close as possible.
