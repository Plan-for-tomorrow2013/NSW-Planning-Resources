### 2.4. Scale and form

Explanation Bulk and scale refer to the height and size of a building, while form and massing relate to the arrangement of its component parts.  In heritage contexts, these elements must be carefully managed to ensure new development does not dominate or detract from existing heritage significance. Objectives

1\. Ensure that alterations and additions to heritage items and contributory buildings and other development, including buildings, structures, and works within and in the vicinity of HCAs are consistent with the scale and form of these items or contributory buildings, and do not dominate or compete with the heritage significance, including fabric, design, form, scale, character, and setting

2\. Ensure that scale and form of new development is consistent with the heritage significance and character of the HCA, and adjacent heritage items and/or contributory buildings

Controls All development

a) In streetscapes characterised by consistent single storey development, upper-floor additions are only appropriate if not readily visible from the street/public domain. Ground floor rear additions are the preferred form of development

b) Attic style additions may be permissible, where they are sympathetic to the original roof form and not visible from the street/public domain especially for previously unaltered buildings or semi-detached pairs

c) Front dormer windows are discouraged on heritage and contributory items and on relatively unaltered semi-detached pair or terrace rows as they detract from the integrity of the original roof forms and consistency of the streetscape

d) Dormer windows and skylights must not be located on street elevations or where they would be visually prominent from a public domain or detract from the original roof form. Dormer windows must be sympathetic in scale and form, proportion, and detailing to the architectural style of the building.

Heritage items and contributory buildings

e) Additions to existing buildings, and other development on the same site, must be subordinate to the original building, and must not visually dominate, compete with, or obscure its form and massing, including significant roof forms

f) Additions to heritage items, and other associated development, must not introduce prominent design elements that compete with or detract from the architectural features, materials, or detailing of the existing building

g) Single storey rear additions to dwelling houses must be designed so as not to compromise the form and integrity of the main roof, and must be lower in scale and visually subordinate to it

h) Development proposal to the main roof of single storey dwelling houses may be acceptable where they are wholly contained within the existing roof space and do not alter the roof form, pitch, or eaves height

i) Additions to single storey dwelling houses, particularly two storey additions, should adopt a pavilion-style form, comprising a secondary addition connected to the original building by a lower-scale link, to maintain the visual primacy of the original building. Additions to any single storey dwelling house should preferably use pavilion-type forms, with a lower scale link between the original building and any new two storey addition

j) Where a pavilion-style form is not feasible or appropriate, an upper floor addition may be acceptable if it is:

• located significantly to the rear of the building

• designed to minimise visual impact on the principal roof form

• discreet or not visible when viewed from the public domain

k) Unless site-specific controls provide otherwise, within a Heritage Conservation Area, an upper-level addition must not be visible above the ridge line or parapet of the existing building when viewed from the opposite side of the primary street

l) Where rear lanes exist, additional floor space should be provided in a rear outbuilding, rather than an upper-level addition to the principal dwelling

m) Rear additions to semi-detached dwellings must not compromise the symmetry or integrity of the front elevation, and must not visually dominate the adjoining dwelling in the pair, including but not limited to its form, massing original roof form

n) Rear additions to attached dwellings (e.g. terrace houses) must not compromise the integrity of the front elevation or the form of intact rear wings, including but not limited to original roof forms

o) Development must not compromise the integrity of an intact or original roof form of heritage or contributory items.

Non-contributory buildings

p) The scale and form of new buildings must be compatible with the streetscape character, typically single storey, or single storey at the front with two storeys to the rear. The roof form and articulation must also be sympathetic and enhance the heritage and streetscape character.
