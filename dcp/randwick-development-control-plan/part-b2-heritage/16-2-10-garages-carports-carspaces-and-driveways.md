### 2.10. Garages, carports, carspaces and driveways

Explanation Early residential buildings in the Randwick Local Government Area were typically designed without garages or carports forward of the building line. The architectural character of this period prioritised a cohesive streetscape with front gardens and building facades forming the primary visual elements when viewed from the public domain.   In later periods, driveways and garages were introduced as ancillary structures, often discreetly located to the side and rear of properties to minimise their visual impact in the streetscape. The introduction of hardstand car parking spaces, driveway crossings, and garages, within the front setback is visually intrusive and incompatible with the established heritage character of the Local Government Area. These elements disrupt the rhythm and continuity of landscaped/garden settings, obscure significant architectural and heritage features, and erode the traditional relationship between buildings and the street. Physical constraints such as narrow

lot widths and shallow front setbacks further limit the feasibility of off-street carparking without compromising heritage significance. Accordingly, the provision of off-street carparking must be carefully managed to avoid detracting from the heritage significance of the building, the integrity of the Heritage Conservation Area, and character of the streetscape. Alternative solutions, such as locating carparking to the side or rear of dwellings are strongly encouraged to preserve the visual prominence of the dwelling and its landscaped/garden setting. While some heritage streetscapes within Randwick Local Government Area have been compromised by the introduction of visually intrusive garages and hardstand carparking areas, intact front gardens and yards remain as important evidence of the original character and subdivision pattern and planning of these areas. These surviving elements must be actively protected and retained, as they provide tangible evidence of the historic development and streetscape patterns and contribute to the cultural significance of the locality. Objectives

1\. Minimise the visual impact of car parking on heritage items, Heritage Conservation Areas and the streetscape

2\. Ensure carparking structures and paved areas are visually discreet, subordinate, and do not dominate or compete with heritage items or buildings within the Heritage Conservation Areas.

Controls All development

a) Driveways, garages, carports, and car spaces must not be located forward of the front building line or within the front setback

b) Where available, rear lane access or side street access must be used to accommodate carparking structures

c) Existing building fabric, including verandahs and balconies, must not be altered or removed to accommodate carparking structures or open hardstand car parking spaces

d) Open hardstand carspaces must not visually dominate the setting of the building and must not result in the loss of deep soil planting, opportunities for landscaping, or significant features such as fencing or retaining walls

e) Carparking structures must be located behind the main building line and remain visually subordinate to the principal building

f) Where an existing vehicular crossover is present and a minimum side setback of 3m is provided carparking structures may be located to the side of the principal building

g) The form and materials of carparking structures must complement those of the principal building. Contemporary design is supported where it is of a high quality and must not imitate historic style and design. Bulky pitched roofs and other design elements that increase the visual prominence of these structures are not supported

h) Existing driveways comprising two separate wheel strips must be retained as they contribute to the character of the streetscape

i) Extensive areas of concrete must be avoided. Alternative materials such as dry laid brick pavers, gravel, two separate wheel strips, or permeable paving must be used to maintain and enhance the landscape and streetscape character

j) Structures originally constructed as stables, coach houses or interwar motor garages should be retained and conserved wherever feasible as they contribute to the historical narrative, historic character and built form of the area

k) Where carparking proposals involve works to public assets and traffic and pedestrian management, the development must respect the established streetscape character and landscape character elements. Any proposed work must not result in the loss of such

character elements. In particular, this applies to elements such as split footpaths, road cuttings and embankments, steps and stairways, retaining walls (including sandstone and/or brick), established fencing and railing arrangements, stone trim/base elements, stone or brick kerbing and guttering, and natural stone and rock outcrops

l) Vehicle entries and exits must not adversely impact pedestrian safety and movement, traffic flow, Council infrastructure, utility services infrastructure, or result in the loss of existing or potential urban tree canopy

m) Sandstone street kerbing and gutters must be retained in situ. Where removal is unavoidable or there is risk of damage or demolition, they must be carefully dismantled and salvaged for reuse

n) Carparking structures on rear lanes must be sympathetic to the historic scale, form, streetscape character and architectural style and materials of the principal building on the site.

Heritage items and contributory buildings No additional controls apply beyond those set above. Non-contributory buildings No additional controls apply beyond those set above.
