### 2.17. Aboriginal cultural heritage

‘Due Diligence Assessment Report

Explanation This section provides guidance for development of land that may contain an Aboriginal objects, relics or places of cultural significance. The level of assessment required will depend on the nature of the proposal and the findings of a

’ and an

‘Aboriginal

Cultural Heritage Assessment Report’ (ACHAR) as outlined below.

Due Diligence Assessment Report is required for –

• subdivision of land where work is required

• developments involving excavation or ground disturbance

• development on land identified by Council as containing, or being in proximity to, land which has been identified as Aboriginal Cultural Heritage place or contains Aboriginal Cultural Heritage objects.

Aboriginal Cultural Heritage Assessment Report (ACHAR) is required for the following types of development:

• subdivision of undeveloped or undisturbed land where the scale of the development is likely to result in significant ground disturbance

• sites containing rock outcrops, caves, platforms or cliffs

• Development where otherwise required by Council or relevant authorities.

Applicant must undertake assessments and prepare documentation in accordance with the Due Diligence Code of Practice for the Protection of Aboriginal Objects in New South Wales (Department of Climate Change, Energy, the Environment and Water (DCCEEW)) or its most current amendment/version. This should include documented research of the Aboriginal Heritage Information Management System (AHIMS) for known/registered Aboriginal objects and places within or in the immediate vicinity of the development application site.

On certain sites, an assessment under the Due Diligence Code of Practice for the Protection of Aboriginal Objects in NSW is not considered to be a substitute for an ACHAR. The Due Diligence process does not include Aboriginal community consultation or detailed archaeological

assessment and may not adequately assess impacts on Aboriginal cultural heritage. In such circumstances, an ACHAR will be required. Objectives

1\. Ensure compliance with the requirements of the National Parks and Wildlife Act 1974 and associated regulations and guidelines relating to Aboriginal objects and Aboriginal places of cultural significance

2\. Conserve and manage Aboriginal cultural heritage in consultation with the Aboriginal community

3\. Ensure Aboriginal objects and places are identified and appropriately managed through the development application process, with impacts avoided or, where avoidance is not possible, appropriately minimised and mitigated

4\. Ensure that new development respects and responds appropriately to the cultural significance of Aboriginal cultural heritage.

Controls All development

a) An assessment of Aboriginal Cultural Heritage must accompany any development application on lands that contain, or is likely to contain, Aboriginal objects or place of cultural significance. The assessment report must be prepared by a suitably qualified archaeologist or heritage consultant

b) A Due Diligence Assessment Report and/or an assessment of Aboriginal Cultural Heritage must accompany any development application involving undisturbed land where:

i) the proposal involves excavation or ground disturbance

ii) the site is within 200 metres of a known or recorded Aboriginal object or Aboriginal place of cultural significance or

iii) the site is within 200 metres of a previously recorded or known Aboriginal object or Aboriginal place including but not limited to cultural landscapes, existing or former ceremonial grounds, burial sites or cemetery, story places or mythological sites, former Aboriginal reserves or historic encampments, or archaeological sites of significance.

c) Developments, including landscaping and associated works, should be designed and located away to avoid impacts on known or potential Aboriginal cultural heritage values.

Non-contributory buildings No additional controls apply beyond those set out in this section.

Note In preparing reports, consultation will be required with the Aboriginal community, as advised by Council and/or Heritage NSW. For further information on Due Diligence Code of Practice refer to Heritage NSW website -https://www.environment.nsw.gov.au/publications/due-diligence-code-practice-protection-aboriginal-objects-new-south-wales

For further information on disturbed land refer to Due Diligence Cod of Practice.

It is recommended that a 200-metre buffer be applied to AHIMS searches to account for potential location inaccuracies and to provide a broader understanding of Aboriginal heritage in the area, the nature and distribution of Aboriginal objects and places within the immediate area.
