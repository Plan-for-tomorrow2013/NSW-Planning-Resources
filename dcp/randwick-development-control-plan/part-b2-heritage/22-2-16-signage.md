### 2.16. Signage

Explanation This section provides guidelines for signs and advertisements (signage) that contribute positively to the public domain through high quality design. Signage must be appropriately located and designed to protect the architectural character of buildings, the streetscape, and the city skyline. Within Heritage Conservation Areas and on heritage items, signage must enhance, rather than detract from, the visual and cultural significance of the place.

Objectives

1\. Ensure signage is compatible with the architecture, materials, finishes, and colours of heritage items and Heritage Conservation Areas

2\. Ensure signage does not detract from the amenity or visual quality of heritage items and Heritage Conservation Areas

3\. Prevent visual clutter having regard to existing signage, neighbouring buildings, streetscape character, and the cumulative impact of signage.

Controls All development

a) A detailed signage plan or strategy must be submitted with all new signage applications within a Heritage Conservation Area

b) Existing signage that has heritage significance must be retained and conserved in its original location

c) Signage must not obscure architectural features, including fenestration, or detract from the architectural quality of heritage items or buildings within Heritage Conservation Areas

d) Shopfront windows must remain unobstructed to allow clear views into the premises from the public domain

e) A maximum of two signs is permitted per elevation visible from the public domain.

f) Signage located above the awning line, or the first-floor level and parapet for any building is not permitted

g) Where appropriate to the heritage style and period, traditional style hand painted signage on window glazing at ground floor level may be permitted subject to consent where the area of lettering/text does not exceed 0.1 sqm, to maintain a high level of visual transparency into the premises

h) Signage must be designed and located, to ensure the character of the building and streetscape or HCA remain the dominant visual element

i) The design, style, materials, colours, images, finishes and lettering of signage must be of high quality and consistent with the relevant heritage style and period. Preferred materials include bronze, brass, and stainless steel. Plastic material, including coloured or clear acrylic, are not supported except as ancillary components/materials

j) Corporate branding and colour schemes are not encouraged and must be adapted to suit and respond to the character and significance of the heritage item and/or Heritage Conservation Area

k) New internally illuminated signage is only permitted where:

i. it reconstructs an original significant sign

ii. it can be demonstrated that internally illuminated signage is integral to the significance of the Heritage Conservation Area or heritage item; or

iii. the illumination is low voltage and visible through cut out lettering in high quality metal consistent with the heritage character.

l) Externally illuminated signage is only permitted where:

i. the design of the signage achieves a high degree of compatibility with the significance of the HCA or heritage item

ii. lighting is provided from concealed or energy efficient source; and

iii. all cabling and conduits are fully concealed.

Heritage items and contributory buildings

m) A signage plan must accompany any development application for signage on a heritage item or in an HCA prior to the granting of development consent

n) Council may require a SOHI to accompany a development application for signage on a heritage item or in an HCA

o) New signage must have minimal impact on the character of the heritage item or HCA

p) Original and early signs must be conserved and not be covered or painted over by a new signage

q) Building name signs on the pediments and parapets of the facades are encouraged where historically accurate and appropriate

r) Signage design must incorporate traditional materials, colours, fonts and size, that are appropriate to the building’s architectural style and period, and be of a high standard of materials, design, construction and graphic quality

s) Signage must be installed in a reversible manner that does not damage significant fabric of heritage items and must be designed to be of high quality. Where signage is attached to stone or brick walls, it is to be attached with non-corrosive fixings to mortar joints rather than to the stone or brickwork. Projecting and cantilevered signage must use a bracketed system that avoids bulky structural components attached to heritage fabric.

Non-contributory buildings No additional controls apply beyond those set out in this section.
