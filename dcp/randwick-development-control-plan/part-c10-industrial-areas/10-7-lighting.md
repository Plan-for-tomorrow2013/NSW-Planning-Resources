## 7. Lighting

Explanation Lighting is an essential component of industrial development, contributing to safety, security and site functionality. However, if not appropriately designed and managed, lighting can result in adverse impacts on surrounding land uses, including glare, light spill and intrusion into adjoining properties and the public domain. Lighting must be carefully designed, located and controlled to minimise impacts on nearby development, particularly residential areas and road users. This includes directing light downward, avoiding unnecessary illumination, and incorporating shielding or other design measures to contain light within the site. Appropriate lighting design ensures adequate illumination for operational and safety purposes, while maintaining the amenity of adjoining land and the surrounding area.

Notes

• Lighting should also address the principles of Crime Prevention through Environmental Design (CPTED) to ensure safety and security in industrial areas.

• AS/NZS 2293 Series: These standards outline the requirements for emergency lighting and exit signs, ensuring that buildings have adequate illumination during emergencies to facilitate safe evacuation.

Objectives The objectives for lighting are to:

1\. Provide adequate illumination for safety, security and operational requirements within industrial sites

2\. Ensure external lighting contributes positively to the visual quality of the development and does not adversely affect the amenity of the locality

3\. Minimise light spill, glare, and sky glow through appropriate design, orientation, and control of lighting systems.

Controls

a) External lighting must be designed and installed to ensure that light sources are directed away from adjoining properties, particularly residential uses and other sensitive receivers

b) Lighting must be designed and located to minimise light spill, glare and reflection beyond the site boundary, and must not result in a nuisance to surrounding properties or the public domain

c) All external lighting must be shielded, baffled or otherwise designed to prevent upward light spill and unnecessary sky glow

d) Lighting levels must be appropriate to the intended use of the area and avoid over-illumination, particularly in areas adjoining residential zones

e) Security and operational lighting must be controlled by timers, motion sensors or similar devices, where appropriate, to minimise unnecessary illumination outside of operational hours

f) Development adjacent to residential areas must submit a lighting plan prepared by a suitably qualified professional, demonstrating that lighting impacts are appropriately mitigated.
