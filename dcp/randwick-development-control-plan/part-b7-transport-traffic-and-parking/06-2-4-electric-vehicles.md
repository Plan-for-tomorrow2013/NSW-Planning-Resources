### 2.4. Electric vehicles

Explanation The integration of electric vehicle (EV) charging infrastructure in developments plays a vital role in supporting the transition to cleaner sustainable transport. In line with the National Construction Code (NCC), all parking spaces in Class 2, 3, 5, 6, 7b, 8, and 9 buildings, including residential apartments, commercial buildings, and retail premises, must be equipped with dedicated electrical distribution boards for EV charging. As demand for EV continues to grow Council is committed to expanding this infrastructure across suitable locations. In addition to public facilities, it is essential that private properties are equipped to support EV charging. Consequently, all new major developments, as well as significant alterations or additions to existing major developments, will be required to provide the capacity for EV charging. Objectives The objectives for EVs are to:

1\. Provide EV infrastructure to facilitate residents, occupants, contracted car share companies and visitors of buildings to charge their electric vehicles

2\. Provide the infrastructure required to facilitate increased usage of electric vehicle charging equipment in allocated parking spaces.

Controls

a) Where a development has non-private parking spaces, EV charging equipment is to be installed at the following rates.

i. The lesser of:

• 10% of the total number of all parking spaces in the development, or

• the total number of non-private parking spaces.

Numbers are rounded up, if 0.5 or more, to the nearest whole number.

Note Control (a) is a method of determining the number of non-private parking spaces for which EV charging equipment is to be installed. This provision does not relate to private parking space.

b) Each parking space for which EV charging equipment is required, is to be supplied with EV charging equipment with a minimum rated power output of 7kW (32A single phase)

c) EV charging equipment installed may include a combination of EV chargers of different power levels, at the discretion of the developer, subject to the minimum power level noted above

d) EV charging equipment is to be compliant with Australian Standards, marked with Regulatory Compliance Mark (RCM) markings, and equipped with OCPP 1.6J (or higher) communications capability

e) In accordance with the EV Distribution Board requirements of the NCC, the wiring enclosure system is to be identified to allow each car space to install an EV charger point. This system must allow for future installation of cabling to EV charger points and allow internet access using one of the following:

i. Cable tray of a size designed and calculated to be sufficient, is to be installed from the EV Distribution Board to within 5m of all parking bays allocated for in the EV Distribution Board, on that storey

ii. An alternative to a cable tray may be provided, if it demonstrates the same or better than the requirements of provisions e (i) above.

f) EV charging equipment must include a billing mechanism that enables cost recovery for residents and visitors using the EV charging equipment. Development applications must clearly define how sub-metering or cost allocation will be managed by the incoming o wner’s corporation when they take over operation of the site. Service agreements must not include vendor lock-

in clauses and a clear pathway must be provided for the owner’s

corporation to make alternative billing arrangements without a punitive exit clauses from a service provider.

g) Development must demonstrate the capability of tracking interval data of the building electrical consumer mains. This may be achieved through the installation of a smart meter or equivalent technology. Residential developments must install a minimum of

two ‘Level 2’ AC fast EV charging

points within common parking areas. The circuit is to be suitably located to provide convenient, shared access for residents (and where relevant, commercial users). The charging infrastructure must:

i. Be equipped with 62196-2 Type 2 socket

ii. Provide up to 22kW or 32A three phase charging port

iii. Be installed on a dedicated circuit

iv. Support monitoring and individual billing via OCPP-compatible software

v. Include a dedicated parking space for EVs to park and charge safely and conveniently.

h) Commercial developments must provide EV charging infrastructure in accordance with the National Construction Code (NCC).  A minimum of 10% of all car parking spaces in public parking areas must be equipped

with ‘Level 2’ AC fast charging EV charging

points. The circuit is to be suitably located to provide for convenient, shared access for commercial users. The charging point must:

i. Be equipped with 62196-2 Type 2 socket

ii. Provide up to 22kW or 32A three phase charging per port

iii. Be installed on a dedicated circuit

iv. Support monitoring and individual billing via OCPP-compatible software

v. Include dedicated EV parking spaces for safe and convenient access to park and charge.

i) All EV charging infrastructure must be installed by appropriately licenced professionals and comply with relevant standards and regulations including Section J9D4 'Facilities for electric vehicle charging equipment' in NCC 2022 Volume One - Building Code of Australia, which outlines requirements for provision and design of distribution boards to support electric vehicle charging in car parks

j) A statement prepared by a suitably qualified person must be submitted with the DA confirming that the proposal will be able to meet the electric vehicle requirements of this DCP and the applicable provisions of the NCC.
