### 4.8. Mechanical Parking Devices

Explanation

Mechanical parking devices, such as vehicle turntables and automated parking platforms, may be considered on constrained sites where conventional on-site manoeuvring cannot be achieved. While these systems can improve access efficiency and reduce the need for extensive driveway areas, they also introduce operational, safety, and public domain considerations that require careful assessment.

The operation of mechanical parking systems has the potential to influence pedestrian and vehicle movements on the adjoining road and footpath, particularly in situations where cycle times, queuing, or system malfunctions may cause vehicles to pause or encroach into the public domain.

In addition, noise and vibration generated by mechanical components may affect neighbouring properties if not appropriately mitigated.

Objectives

1\. Ensure mechanical parking devices are only used where necessary and operate safely, efficiently and reliably.

2\. Minimise impacts on the public domain, including pedestrian and vehicle movements, and prevent queuing or encroachment onto roads and footpaths.

3\. Protect the amenity of adjoining properties by minimising noise, vibration and visual impacts.

Controls

a) The use of mechanical parking systems e.g. car lifts, car stackers and turntables, should be minimised especially where queuing or safety concerns may arise.

b) Mechanical parking devices may only be permitted where it is clearly demonstrated that their installation and operation will not adversely impact the public domain, adjoining properties, or traffic and pedestrian movements.

c) All mechanical parking devices must be located wholly within the site.

d) Development applications proposing mechanical parking devices must include an assessment prepared by a suitably qualified traffic engineer addressing:

i. the operational characteristics of the device, including cycle times and capacity

ii. Technical specifications of the proposed system (the onus is on the applicant to demonstrate the mechanical device can be accommodated within the design space available)

iii. potential for vehicle queuing and any risk of encroachment onto the road or footpath

iv. pedestrian and vehicle safety impacts

v. noise and vibration impacts

vi. sightline and visibility considerations for vehicles entering or exiting the site.

vii. Swept paths based on the B99 design template in AS 2890.1 demonstrating satisfactory ingress and egress can be achieved into the mechanical device

e) All manoeuvring associated with the mechanical parking device must occur within the site and must not rely on the public road or footpath.

f) The device must be designed and installed to ensure safe, reliable and efficient operation at all times, including during peak demand periods.

g) A maintenance and operational management plan must be submitted with the application which outlines servicing requirements, procedures for breakdowns, and measures to ensure the device does not obstruct the public domain in the event of malfunction.

h) Where car lifts are proposed to enter basement carparking;

i. A passing bay will be required in front of the car lift unless it can be satisfactorily demonstrated by way of a queueing analysis that the probability of vehicle conflict is minimal.

ii. Car lifts are to be in accordance with AS 1735.1

iii. Car lifts accessing further mechanical devices such as stackers or turntables will generally not be supported.
