### 4.7. Access to dwellings elevated above retaining walls

Explanation The historical subdivisions in the coastal areas of Randwick City have created several urban blocks that are elevated above public roads due to the sloping topography. The frontage to

these allotments is supported by masonry block (often sandstone) retaining walls aligned with the carriageway boundaries, and with public footpaths running above. The provision of off-street parking to these sites often proves to be problematic as it necessitates significant demolition and modification to the retaining walls. In some circumstances, the height of the retaining wall does not provide sufficient clearance for parking facilities as required by the Australian Standard. The public footpaths above may need to be raised along the frontage of the development site, which further compromises the visual integrity of the retaining walls, streetscape character and pedestrian accessibility. Any successive developments for vehicular access would incrementally remove wall sections and lead to cumulative visual impacts. Due to the likely adverse implications on streetscape amenity, the partial demolition of existing retaining walls within the public domain for the sole purpose of gaining vehicular access to a private property will generally not be supported. Objectives

1\. Protect the integrity, character and continuity of existing retaining walls, particularly those of heritage or streetscape significance

2\. Maintain the visual amenity and consistency of streetscapes in areas with elevated lots and established topography

3\. Ensure safe and accessible pedestrian environments, including preserving existing footpath levels and usability

Controls

a) Any provision of vehicular access to dwellings must minimise demolition, modification and damage to existing retaining walls within the public domain

b) Double width driveway and entry to on-site parking involving full or part removal of retaining walls in the public domain is not permitted

c) Development must not involve any significant change to the existing gradients of public footpaths above the retaining walls, except to facilitate equitable access

d) The creation of an access driveway must not jeopardise the safety of pedestrians and vehicles

e) Pedestrian and cyclist entry points must be separated from vehicle accessing driveways, to ensure safety of vulnerable road users and to avoid vehicle conflict

f) Driveway access and front yards should be separated by physical barriers (e.g. wall, fence, etc)

g) Sight distances at driveways are to be compliant with AS 2890.1 and AS2890.2

h) Works that require alteration or replacement of landscape elements and structures (such as handrails) adjacent to the public footpaths situated above retaining walls must be compatible with the streetscape character.
