### 2.2. Bicycles

Explanation Environmentally sustainable, healthy and vibrant cities support alternative transport modes such as cycling, which require safe, well-designed infrastructure and connected bicycle routes. Key activity generators within Randwick City- including the University of New South Wales, Randwick Hospitals Campus, Centennial Park and coastal destinations - create significant demand for non-car transport options. By improving facilities for cyclists and pedestrians there is an opportunity to reduce car dependency, promote active transport and enhance public health outcomes. This section sets out bicycle parking requirements for various development types. Where a specific land use is not listed, a merit-based assessment will be applied to ensure bicycle parking is appropriately provided. DAs requiring bicycle facilities must consider the following documents:

1\. Australian Standard AS 2890.3 Parking Facilities - Part 3: Bicycle parking

2\. Austroads Guide to Traffic Management - Bicycle Parking Facilities

3\. Cycling Aspects of Austroads Guides (AP-G88-17)

4\. TfNSW Guide to Transport Impact Assessment Objectives The objectives for on-site bicycle facilities are to:

1\. Support active, healthy lifestyles through the provision of cycling infrastructure

2\. Promote cycling as a safe, convenient and low emission mode of transport

3\. Ensure equitable access to bicycle parking and end-of-trip facilities. Controls

a) All new development must provide on-site bicycle parking in addition to other parking requirements, in accordance with the minimums specified in Table 3

b) Bicycle parking facilities must be designed and constructed in accordance with AS2890.3

c) Bicycle parking must address the following user categories:

i. Secure, covered all-day parking for employees and students

ii. Co-living or hostel developments with low car parking provision

iii. Permanent bicycle storage for residents

iv. Short term visitor parking for shopping centres, commercial offices, industrial and other public and private buildings

v. All day parking at transport interchanges.

e) Bicycle parking for residents and staff must be located close to building entry/exits and lifts and prioritised over other parking types to ensure visibility, convenience and usage. Avoid locating bicycle parking in hidden niches, at the end of aisles and under staircases, etc

f) Where bicycle parking is in basement levels, it must be placed on the uppermost basement level close to exits and/ ramps and as near to ground level as possible

g) Bicycle paring areas must be clearly marked, easily accessible and avoid reliance on lifts for access. Ramp access is preferred for bicycle parking located at basement level/s

h) Bicycle parking spaces must be well-lit, allow for secure locking of both frame and wheels and be subject to passive surveillance.

i) Bicycle parking must be secure, weather protected, and constructed of durable, tamper resistant materials.

j) Bicycle parking must be designed to accommodate a variety of bicycle types, including cargo bikes, e-bikes, and other micromobility devices.

k) One-wheel racks are not acceptable nor are facilities that require a wheel to be removed

l) A safe and direct path of travel must be provided from bicycle parking areas to building entry/exit points. Paths must be marked and have a minimum width of 1.5m wide, include adequate turning space and maintain clear sight lines for safety

m) Visitor bicycle parking must be provided in an accessible on-grade location near major public entrances, away from pedestrian desire lines and clearly sign posted

n) Locker and end-of-trip facilities must be provided in accordance with the NSW Bicycle Guidelines to ensure good bicycle parking arrangements

o) End of trip facilities, where required, must be proportionate to the scale and nature of the development and incorporate secure lockers, showers and change rooms

p) End of trip facilities, where required, must be integrated into the building layout, not treated as leftover or residual space and be clearly signposted for easy access

q) Bicycle parking and end-of-trip facilities are to be provided in accordance with the rates in Tables 3 and 5, and part 3.3, Control b)

r) Where swimming pools and similar amenities are proposed in residential developments, bicycle parking should be co-located to allow shared use of facilities (such as showers and changing rooms) as end-of-trip facilities

s) A minimum of 25% of bicycle parking spaces must be capable of E-bike charging with appropriate power outlets provided. Elevated rack storage is not acceptable for E-Bike charging spaces.

Note While there are no requirements stated for a dwelling house or semi-detached dwellings, the inclusion of a suitable internal/covered bike space is encouraged.
