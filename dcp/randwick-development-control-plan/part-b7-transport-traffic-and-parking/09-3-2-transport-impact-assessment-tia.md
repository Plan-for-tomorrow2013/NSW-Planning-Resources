### 3.2. Transport Impact Assessment (TIA)

Explanation A TIA is a technical investigation into the transport and safety issues that might arise from a development. This includes impacts to pedestrians, cyclists, motorists and passengers, public transport users, and freight and servicing vehicles. It also assesses the transport related impacts on the surrounding transportation network that are generated by a development and how those impacts are to be managed. Such a study recognises the role of traffic within a broader transport system that includes public transport, walking and cycling. Objectives

1\. Ensure comprehensive assessment of transport impacts for new development.

2\. Support integration of development with existing and planned transport infrastructure.

3\. Maintain safe, efficient and accessible movement for all users.

4\. Minimise adverse impacts on the road network, parking supply and surrounding amenity.

Controls The TfNSW Guide to Transport Impact Assessment should be used to inform the scope and detail of the TIA, which complements the matters listed in this DCP. A TIA assessment must include the following:

a) The accessibility of the site by various transport modes including car, public transport, walking and cycling

b) Capacity of the public transport network to service the site during peak, off peak and weekend periods

c) Mode share targets and strategies to reduce car dependency

d) Measures to minimise car travel and maximise other modes including public transport use, cycling or walking

e) Compliance with the requirements of the LEP and DCP

f) Justification of car parking provision and site servicing arrangements in accordance with the objectives of the Randwick LEP and DCP

g) Proposed allocation of parking spaces by dwelling types for residential developments

h) Access provisions for people with mobility impairments

i) Trip generation estimates and impacts of trips on the road transport network

j) Integration of trips generated with existing infrastructure and identification of necessary improvements or upgrades to public transport services and infrastructure (e.g. bus shelters), pedestrian systems, bicycle routes, and the road network

k) Mitigation strategies for any adverse impacts of the development on movement systems

l) Means of improving access to the site having regard to vehicular, pedestrian, cycle and public transport access

m) Impacts on and means of improving pedestrian accessibility to public transport (including proximity to services), shops, schools, open spaces, community centres and the like

n) Pedestrian safety, including appropriate locations of access driveways

o) Availability of on street parking and potential controls to discourage long term residential parking demand generated by the development.

p) Trip generation must be assessed using the TfNSW Guide to Transport Impact Assessment which provides standard vehicular trip generating rates for both daily and peak hour vehicle trips. Comparative surveys of similar developments to the proposal, can also be used. The vehicle trip generation assessment must consider:

i. The peak activity periods of the proposed development

ii. The peak activity on the adjacent road network

iii. Whether road upgrades, traffic management or pedestrian safety measures are required to accommodate the increased movement on the system.

q) The TIA must include a comparison of vehicle trip generation rates contained in the ‘ TfNSW Guide to Transport Impact Assessment’ , parking availability, access to public transport and proximity to neighbourhood shopping centres, community facilities and open spaces.  Adjustments factors for each land use may include:

i. Mode split by time period

ii. Persons per vehicle

iii. Trip purpose

iv. Availability of on-site parking.

r) Where necessary, traffic mitigation measures can be incorporated to ameliorate the impact of traffic and parking generated by the development including traffic signals, signs, pedestrian crossings, channelisation, roundabouts, angled parking, and traffic calming devices, storage days and median islands.
