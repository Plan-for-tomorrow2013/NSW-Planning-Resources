## 2. Sustainable transport

### 2.1.  Public and active transport

Explanation Randwick City’s public transport system is predominantly bus-based complemented by the South East Light Rail, which provides key connections to and from the Sydney CBD. Local and regional bus services also play a key role in connecting the local area to surrounding destinations, such as Bondi Junction, Waverley, Maroubra Beach, and Coogee Beach. Access to public transport is essential for sustainable urban mobility. It relies not only on well-located services but also on supporting infrastructure - such as bus and light rail stops, pedestrian crossings, bicycle paths, footpaths

– that are safely and effectively integrated with

pedestrian and cycling networks. Seamless connectivity between these elements is essential to ensure inclusive access for all users. Walking and cycling, collectively referred to as active transport, represent the most sustainable modes of travel. They contribute to healthier lifestyles, cleaner environments and support vibrant public spaces. At the State level, the NSW Active Transport Strategy sets an ambitious goal to double the number of people walking and cycling in NSW over the next 20 years. While acknowledging the continued need for car travel, this DCP promotes the transition toward more sustainable and integrated transport solutions. The NSW Government, through Transport for New South Wales (TfNSW) introduced the Electric and Hybrid Vehicle Plan aiming for net zero transport emissions by 2050 and investing in electric vehicle infrastructure and services. Transport for NSW is also undertaking reforms to improve the operation and management of shared e-micromobility services such as shared e-bikes across New South Wales. These reforms aim to establish a clearer and more consistent regulatory framework that supports safe, accessible, and well-managed services for communities. More information on e-micromobility can be found here: https://www.nsw.gov.au/driving-boating-and-transport/bikes-e-bikes-e-scooters/e-micromobility-action-plan

This DCP part supports active and sustainable transport initiatives by enhancing infrastructure for walking and cycling, encouraging the uptake of electric vehicles and facilitating the integration of these measures into new developments and the broader public transport network. The goal is to create a more connected, cleaner and future ready City.

Objectives To promote sustainable transport by:

1\. Integrating development with the broader public transport network

2\. Encouraging the take up of sustainable transport modes including walking, cycling, and public transport.

Controls

a) All development must address transport, access and parking requirements by incorporating measures that increase the share of trips made by public transport, walking and cycling

b) Developments should include flexible hardstand areas to support bicycle maintenance and repair activities

c) Where a variation to the DCP Car Parking rates is proposed, proponents must respond directly to the provisions in Section 8 Exceptions to Parking Rates of this DCP

d) A Green Travel Plan (GTP) must accompany all DAs for new buildings and substantial alterations to existing buildings. The GTP is to include:

i. Mode share targets, aimed at reducing private car travel

ii. Travel demand management strategies to encourage sustainable transport choices

e) Development proposals must include initiatives to implement and monitor travel measures such as car share and bike share programs

f) Development proposals must include provision for electric charging stations in accessible on-site locations.
