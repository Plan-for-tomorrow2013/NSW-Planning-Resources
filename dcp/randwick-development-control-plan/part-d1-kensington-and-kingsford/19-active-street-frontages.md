## 19. Active street frontages

### 19.1. Explanation

Active frontages are street frontages that provide a high level of visual, physical and commercial interaction between pedestrians in the public domain and the uses within buildings. They typically comprise retail or business uses that open directly onto the footpath and operate throughout the day and evening. Active frontages play a critical role in:

• Encouraging greater pedestrian movements

• Supporting business viability

• Enhancing safety, passive surveillance and improving amenity

• Strengthening the character and competitiveness of commercial centres. In Kensington and Kingsford town centres, active frontages are fundamental in reinforcing and enhancing the role of Anzac Parade and Gardeners Road as primary economic corridors, and are also encouraged along side streets within these centres, to further support economic vitality and pedestrian activity. Objectives The objectives for active street frontages are to:

1\. Ensure ground floor development delivers continuous retail and commercial uses that contribute positively to the economic function of the town centres

2\. Reinforce Anzac Parade and Gardeners Road as the principal retail and commercial spines, consistent with the intent of the Active Frontages Clause of the Randwick LEP 2012

3\. Promote pedestrian activity, safety, natural surveillance and interest through pedestrian entries, transparent glazing, display windows and active uses

4\. Ensure that new development is designed to support long term economic function and resilience of town centres by incorporating flexible design for future tenants

5\. Create lively, interesting, inclusive and attractive streets and public spaces consistent with the vision of supporting a day and night economy

6\. Promote flexible design and early delivery of active uses, including through temporary activation measures where permanent uses are not yet established.

Controls

a) Active frontages must be provided strictly in accordance with RLEP 2012 (Clause 6.20) and the Active frontages Map. Development must demonstrate continuity and effectiveness of the active frontages

b) Preferred active frontages are provided in accordance with Part B – Block Controls of this DCP. Proposals must demonstrate how active frontages enhance pedestrian connectivity and commercial/economic vitality

c) A minimum of 80% of the street frontage on Anzac Parade and Gardeners Road must incorporate transparent glazing allowing direct views between internal uses and the public domain

d) Ground floor development is to:

i. limit residential uses to essential access and servicing only

ii. maximise pedestrian entries, display windows and operable windows

iii. provide at least one pedestrian opening per 5 metres of facade along primary and secondary streets

iv. wrap shopfront around corners to reinforce continuity, visibility and business opportunities at intersections

v. provide at least one pedestrian entry where fronting a public plaza

vi. ensure a minimum activation depth of 6 metres behind the façade with publicly accessible or visually engaging spaces to avoid shallow spaces and unusable tenancies

e) Development fronting laneways must provide continuous active frontages with at least 1 pedestrian entry per 10 metres of façade to support activation, safety and secondary business opportunities

f) Development fronting mid-block links or arcades must provide active, publicly accessible frontages, with at least one 1 pedestrian entry per 15 metres of façade and be designed in accordance with CPTED principles

g) Blank walls greater than 10m2 visible from the public domain are strongly discouraged. Where unavoidable, at least 50% of such wall must incorporate high quality treatment such as green wall, public art or other visually engaging treatments to the satisfaction of Council

h) Entrances to internal retail or commercial arcades must:

i. have a minimum of 6 metres

ii. be clearly visible from the street

iii. incorporate active uses, lighting and signage that encourage entry and extend activity into internal spaces

i) Solid non-transparent roller shutters are strongly discouraged. Where security measures are required, they must:

i. be installed at least 1 metre behind the glazing line

ii. be of lattice or perforated design allowing views into the tenancy

iii. allow visibility into the tenancy, including after-hours lighting and displays

j) Outdoor dining, trading and display areas must be provided in accordance with Part C7 Footpath Dining and Trading

and Council’s Footway Dining Guide

k) Active frontage tenancies are to be designed with sufficient depth, ceiling height and services to support a range of uses over time, minimising vacancy risk and supporting long‑term economic adaptability

l) Development must demonstrate, through design and layout, how non-residential uses will:

i. support a diverse mix of uses, including supermarkets on suitably sized sites where they can contribute to street activation, as well as retail, commercial, food, service and community-oriented uses that contribute to daily needs of the community and local employment

ii. provide opportunities for small and medium sized businesses to support business diversity

iii. contribute positively to the overall economic performance, attractiveness and function of the town centre

m) Where supermarkets or other large-format uses are proposed, development must demonstrate how:

i. primary street facades incorporate active uses, entries or display areas

ii. servicing, storage and blank walls are minimised along public frontages

iii. ancillary uses (such as cafés, pharmacies or services) can be supported through street-level activity where feasible

n) Development is encouraged to identify proposed uses or temporary activation measures (such as pop-up uses, display spaces or community uses) where permanent uses are not yet established.

Note Clause 6.14 of Randwick LEP 2012 (RLEP 2012) prohibits new Dwelling houses and Residential flat buildings in the E2 Commercial Centre zone, limiting permissibility to Dwelling houses and Residential flat buildings existing before the commencement of RLEP.
