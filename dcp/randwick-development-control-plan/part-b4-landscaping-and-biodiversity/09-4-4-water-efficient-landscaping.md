### 4.4. Water efficient Landscaping

Explanation High quality landscaping plays a critical role in influencing both the quality and quantity of stormwater runoff from a site, as well as the amount of water required for irrigation. Water efficient landscaping reduces potable water use, improves stormwater management and urban cooling and biodiversity outcomes. For residential development, such measures assist in achieving BASIX water conservation targets. For non-residential development, they support broader water conservation and integrated water management outcomes consistent with Part B8- Water Management. Objectives

1\. Minimise potable (mains) water consumption for landscape irrigation.

2\. Reduce stormwater runoff and improve on-site infiltration through effective landscape design and plant selection.

3\. Promote drought-resilient and climate-responsive planting

4\. Ensure landscape design contributes to integrated water cycle management Controls

a) Development must be designed to maximise rainfall infiltration and minimise runoff, by:

i. reducing the extent of impervious surfaces

ii. directing overland flow to permeable areas such as garden beds in line with the requirements of Part B8 – Water management

iii. using semi-pervious materials for paved surfaces

b) Plant species must be selected to have low water requirements once established, be suited to local climatic and soil conditions and include deep rooted species to improve soil stability and drought resilience

c) Development should avoid extensive lawn areas, which typically require more water and fertiliser than native groundcovers, shrubs, and trees

d) Lawns will only be considered in rear yards of detached dwellings. Alternative low water-use native groundcovers should be used in all other areas to enhance water efficiency and visual amenity

e) Water features must utilise recirculating systems and operate using non-potable water sources

i. Irrigation systems must be water efficient such as automated sub-soil drip systems such as soil moisture and rain sensors, and

ii. use non-potable water sources (e.g. rainwater tanks)

f) Landscape design must incorporate water conservation practices including:

i. hydro zoning (grouping plants with similar water needs)

ii. providing adequate soil depth to support water retention, plant health and reduced irrigation demand.
