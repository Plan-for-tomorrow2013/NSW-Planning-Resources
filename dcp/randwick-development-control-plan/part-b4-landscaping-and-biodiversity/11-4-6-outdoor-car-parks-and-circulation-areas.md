### 4.6. Outdoor car parks & circulation areas

Explanation Landscaping is an integral component of outdoor (at-grade) parking design. Well-designed landscape treatment offers a variety of benefits, including shade for parked vehicles, screening the car parks to reduce visual dominance, improve microclimatic conditions and contribute to urban tree canopy targets.   Landscaping within open car parks also plays a key role in water sensitive urban design (WSUD), reducing stormwater runoff and mitigating urban heat island effects. Objectives

1\. Ensure outdoor ground level car parking areas are landscaped to provide effective shade and improve user comfort

2\. Minimise the visual impact of car parking within the streetscape and public domain

3\. Ensure that land uses requiring at-grade car parking, such as industrial and business park uses, incorporate adequate landscaping and vegetation to improve canopy cover reduce large areas of paved surfaces and minimise the urban heat island effects

4\. Ensure landscaping is functional and does not compromise visibility or accessibility.

Controls

a) Outdoor ground level car parks containing 5 or more car spaces must incorporate landscape planting

b) Where landscaping is required, it must be designed in accordance with the following criteria:

i. Planting should be provided along the perimeter to screen car parking from streets and integrate with existing streetscape

ii. Incorporate internal planting to break up large expanses of paving and provide shade to parked vehicles

iii. Planting should maintain clear sightlines for both drivers and pedestrians, in accordance with relevant safety and CPTED principles

iv. Internal planting should achieve a canopy tree planting rate of at least 1 tree per 4 car spaces or demonstrate an equivalent outcome capable of achieving a minimum50% canopy coverage of the car park at maturity

v. Planter beds must provide sufficient size and configuration to support long term tree growth, including adequate soil volume, depth, drainage and aeration, be protected from vehicle encroachment through kerbing or appropriate barriers and be designed to accommodate infiltration

vi. Trees installed within car parks must have a minimum container size of 100 Litres at the time of planting

c) The following plant species should be used for car parks:

i. Trees with tall trunks and ample shade coverage

ii. Plants that do not drop fruits, branches, sap or bark

iii. Plants of vigorous growth and with minimum long-term maintenance requirements

d) Landscaping must be coordinated with site infrastructure ensuring appropriate clearances from planting and lighting, above ground electricity lines, footpaths, kerbs and underground services, etc.

e) Contrasting materials and finishes, including permeable paving, must be used to break up large sections of paving and to delineate pedestrian areas/crossings, entries, car parks, special use areas or at transition zones between different uses

f) Permeable paving surfaces must be prioritised for driveways, car parking bays and paved areas rather than non-permeable surfaces

g) Development involving 5 or more at grade car spaces must incorporate Water Sensitive Urban Design (WSUD) measures, including on-site stormwater infiltration through landscaped areas or bio-retention systems; and reduction of stormwater runoff generated by impervious surfaces.
