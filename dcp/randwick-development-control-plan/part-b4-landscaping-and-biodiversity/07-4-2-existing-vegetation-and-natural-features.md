### 4.2. Existing vegetation and natural features

Explanation Significant natural features and vegetation contribute to the ecological function, landscape character and visual identity of a site and its surrounding area. These elements -including rocky outcrops, cliff lines, water bodies, trees, shrubs and groundcover vegetation should be retained and incorporated into the landscape design of the development. Objectives

1\. Conserve, protect and integrate significant natural features and vegetation into the design of development

2\. Ensure site planning responds sensitively to the natural landform, supports ecological values including habitat, soil stability and water systems

3\. Reinforces the visual and environmental character of the locality through landscaped-led design.

Controls

a) Development must be designed to avoid and minimise impacts on existing vegetation including trees, shrubs and groundcover vegetation

b) Retain and protect existing natural features as landscape elements within the site including water bodies, sandstone and rock outcrops and cliff lines and incorporate as part of the landscape design to inform the siting, orientation and form of development

c) Development must be designed to respond to natural landform and avoid excessive excavation, filling or regrading that would alter the site’s natural character

d) Topsoil must be retained and stockpiled onsite where practicable for reuse in landscaped areas to support vegetation establishment.
