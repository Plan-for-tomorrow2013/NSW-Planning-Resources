### 4.1. Trees on development sites

Explanation Development must be designed to retain and protect the urban forest values of the site by avoiding or minimising impacts on existing trees and providing appropriate replacement planting where tree removal is proposed. This requirement applies to all trees that may be affected by development, including those located on the site, within the public domain (Council owned trees) and on adjoining land. The retention and integration of existing vegetation is essential to maintaining canopy cover, supporting biodiversity and preserving the landscape character of the locality. Objectives

1\. Retain existing trees on development sites as an integral component of site planning

2\. Increase canopy cover in accordance with

Randwick’s tree canopy target

3\. Protect existing trees on development sites, adjoining properties or street trees in accordance with Australian Standard

– AS 4970

– 2009 - Protection of Trees on

Development Sites

4\. Ensure development does not adversely impact trees on adjoining land or within the public domain.

Controls

a) Development must be designed to enable the retention of existing trees, including trees on adjoining land

b) Buildings, structures and excavation must be located outside the Tree Protection Zone (TPZ) of trees to be retained (whether on the site or on adjoining land), in accordance with AS4970-Protection of trees

i. A Tree Protection Zone must be established and maintained for all existing trees to be retained prior to the commencement of any demolition or construction works

ii. The following activities are not permitted within the TPZ:

- Stockpiling of materials

- No parking of any vehicles

- Excavation, trenching or soil disturbance

- Changes to soil levels

c) Trees on public land must be protected during demolition, excavation and construction work, through the installation of protective fencing or hoarding in accordance with Section 4 of AS4970-2009

d) Council will require the payment of a security deposit to ensure protection of trees on public land where:

i. Development is proposed within the Tree Protection Zone; or

ii. Council determines that the development has the potential to impact the root zone, trunk or canopy of any tree.

e) Developments in zones other than R1 – General Residential or R2 – Low Density Residential must consider opportunities to

i. Underground overhead electrical services

ii. Utilise aerial bundled cabling to minimise ongoing impacts on the tree canopy

f) New awnings, balconies or structures projecting over public land must be designed to accommodate existing street trees and allow for the establishment and future growth of street trees

Where tree removal is approved, replacement planting must be provided to:

i. Achieve canopy replacement appropriate to the scale and species of trees removed

ii. Contribute to the long-term urban canopy of the locality.
