### 5.2. Green walls

Explanation A green wall is a vertical vegetated system that may be, either free-standing or integrated into a building façade. Green walls can enhance the visual quality of development, reduce perceived building bulk and contribute to improved environmental performance. Similar to green roofs, green walls can potentially offer a range of benefits, including lowering energy consumption through increased thermal performance, reduced noise transmission, improved air quality and enhanced urban biodiversity. Green walls are considered supplementary landscaping measure and are not a substitute for required on-site landscaped area for any development. Objectives

1\. To encourage well designed, functional and maintainable green walls in appropriate locations

2\. To promote ecologically sustainable design approaches for sites with constraints in highly urbanised contexts to achieve an appropriate quantity of landscape area

3\. To enhance urban greening, building performance and environmental quality in dense urban areas.

Controls Any development incorporating a green wall must:

a) Be designed and located to:

i. respond to the orientation, solar access, wind exposure and microclimatic conditions

ii. provide safe and practical access for installation and ongoing maintenance.

b) Include a planting scheme that:

i. utilises species suited to vertical growing conditions and the specific microclimate

ii. incorporate a mix of hardy, drought and heat tolerant species

iii. prioritises native and ornamental species in accordance with Appendix B4-2 unless otherwise justified

c) Provide detailed design documentation demonstrating:

i. the structural support system

ii. the growing medium and planting system

iii. integration with the building structure and services

d) Be designed to ensure that:

i. the structural integrity and waterproofing of the building is not compromised

ii. the system can be repaired, replaced or removed without damage to the building

e) Incorporate a water efficient irrigation system that:

i. is appropriate to the planting system and growing medium

ii. includes automated controls (e.g. timers, soil moisture sensors)

iii. utilises non-potable water sources where available

f) include a maintenance plan submitted with the development application that addresses:

i. irrigation system operation and monitoring

ii. plant establishment, maintenance and replacement

iii. inspection and upkeep of structural and support system.
