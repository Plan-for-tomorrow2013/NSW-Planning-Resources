## 5. Green roofs and green walls

### 5.1. Green roofs

Explanation Green roofs comprise roof surfaces that are partially or fully vegetated and designed as integrated landscape systems.  They can improve the building performance, reduce visual bulk when viewed from surrounding development and contribute to urban greening outcomes. Green roofs provide multiple environmental benefits, including improved thermal performance, reduced stormwater run-off, increased biodiversity and mitigation of urban heat island effects. Green roofs offer a good option for renovating and improving the amenity of existing buildings where opportunities for at grade landscaping are limited. A green roof system typically includes a waterproofing membrane and root barrier system, drainage system, filter fabric, a lightweight growing medium and vegetation. Where appropriately designed, green roofs may contribute to landscape provision; however, their suitability and extent will be assessed by Council on a merit basis, having regard to functionality, accessibility, and long-term performance. Early consultation with Council is encouraged for proposals incorporating green roofs. Objective

1\. To encourage high quality, well designed and functional green roofs in suitable buildings and locations

2\. To enhance urban greening, biodiversity and environmental performance in highly urbanised areas to achieve an appropriate quantity of landscape area

3\. To provide additional useable or visual landscape space for urban greening, food production and useable private open space for residents.

Controls

a) Green roofs must be designed as an integrated building and landscape system, having regard to:

i. site conditions, including solar access, wind exposure and microclimate

ii. the structural capacity, age and condition of the building

iii. the intended function of the green roof (e.g. intensive, extensive, accessible or non-accessible

b) Development applications incorporating a green roof must include a detailed site documentation demonstrating:

i. the site suitability, including consideration of the climate conditions (e.g. solar orientation and wind loads)

ii. the depth and composition of the growing medium

iii. planting design and species selection

iv. the structural capacity of the building, age and condition of the roof

c) Select native and drought/heat tolerant plant species suited to roof conditions including high wind exposure, solar radiation and limited soil depths in accordance with Council’s native species list as provided in Appendix B4-2. Other species may be considered and approved by Council’s landscape officers

d) Be designed with high standard components, including waterproofing membrane, growing medium, vegetation layer, root barrier, insulation and drainage system

e) Green roofs must be designed to maximise retention and reuse of stormwater

f) Identify the most suitable irrigation system based on growing medium characteristics and plant needs

g) Green roofs should be designed to enable co-location with other rooftop uses where compatible, including solar photovoltaic systems

h) A maintenance plan must be submitted with the development application and must address at a minimum:

i. Inspection and maintenance of the waterproofing roof membrane

ii. Drainage system performance and inspections

iii. Ongoing care of plant health and replacement and

iv. Maintenance of the irrigation system.
