### F7.7. Seniors Housing

#### Explanation

This part of the DCP contains objectives and controls which apply to seniors housing as defined in LEP 2015. There is need for diverse housing choice in suitable locations in the Blue Mountains for people with a disability and for older people. The concept of seniors housing is intended to be a shorthand phrase encompassing both housing for seniors and for people with a disability. Part F7.7 deals with both kinds of housing. Seniors housing is permissible in medium density zones R1 and R3, but unlike other forms of medium density, it is also permissible in the R2 zone in recognition of the need to provide greater opportunity for ageing in place. Not all sites in the

R2 zone will be suitable for this type of land use. Siting matters for consideration include access to services and public transport, as well as bush fire prone land. To facilitate ageing in place, adaptable seniors housing dwellings must incorporate construction and design elements to meet peoples changing mobility requirements over their lifetime (for example, level pathways, wider doorways and corridors and reinforced bathroom walls to enable future installation of grab rails).

Part F7: Other Development

The provisions and performance criteria for site responsive design are embodied in this DCP within various parts. Part F7.7 cannot be read in isolation. Reference to other relevant portions of the DCP is necessary. Site responsive solutions are encouraged. Applicants will need to provide the necessary plans and documentation to demonstrate that all relevant objectives have been considered and addressed.

#### Objectives

O1. To provide for the housing needs of older people to age in place in their community and ensure adequate housing choice for people with a disability, in suitable locations throughout the Blue Mountains.

O2. Provision of housing options that will meet the access and mobility needs of any occupant, that are superior in design through achieving high performance in sustainability, solar access and accessibility.

O3. Ensure housing is responsive to both the relevant zone objectives and unique character of the township in which they are situated.

O4. To provide well designed seniors housing in the R1, R2 and R3 zones, that is located within close proximity of facilities and services provided in the E1 and E2 zones.

#### Controls

General

C1. A restriction as to user is to be registered against the title of the property on which the development is to be carried out, in accordance with section 88E of the Conveyancing Act 1919, limiting the sale and use of the development, including any dwelling within that development to; a) people over the age of 55 and people with a disability, or b) people who live with older people or people with a disability, or c) staff employed to assist in the administration of and provision of services to seniors housing developments.

Site related requirements

C2. The minimum lot size for a seniors housing development is 1,100 square metres.

C3. The site frontage must be at least 20 metres wide measured at the building line.

Part F7: Other Development

C4. Reasonable pedestrian access via a footpath or other similar and safe means is to be available from the primary pedestrian entrance of the development to land zoned E1 Local Centre or land zoned and

E2 Commercial Centre.

C5. For the purpose of F7.7.C4, reasonable pedestrian access is considered to be provided if:

(a) There is a path of travel via a sealed footpath or other similar and safe means (that is suitable for access by means of an electric wheelchair, motorised cart or the like) from the development to a local or neighbourhood centre, that avoids vehicle and pedestrian conflict;

(b) The gradient of access paths does not exceed an overall average of 1:14 over the shortest path of travel from the development to the local or neighbourhood centre, provided that;

i. slopes up to 1:12 do not exceed 15 metres at a time; and

ii. slopes up to 1:10 do not exceed 5 metres at a time; and

iii. slopes up to 1:8 do not exceed 1.5 metres at a time, and

(c) There are sufficient rest stops along the route, which should include seats or level landings.

C6. Where requirements of F7.7.C5 cannot be met, residents are to have reasonable access to regular public transport that is available both to and from the proposed development during daylight hours at least once each day 5 days per week and that provides access to at least one of the zones listed in F7.7.C4, access to which is within 400 metres of the proposed development.

C7. The pedestrian access shall not involve the crossing of local roads unless safe crossing conditions are provided with reference to sight distances, level crossings, pedestrian refuges and the like.

C8. Pedestrian access shall not involve crossing of a classified road unless there is a bridge or subway providing pedestrian access, or a signalised crossing with auditory devices provided.

Note: A classified road is in accordance with the NSW Government Transport Classified Roads Schedule

C9. Seniors housing (self contained) developments should incorporate a range of dwelling sizes, particularly those which contribute to the increased provision of single and two bedroom dwelling stock.

Height of buildings

Part F7: Other Development

C10. If the development is proposed in the R2 zone:

(a) a building that is adjacent to a boundary of the site must be not more than 2 storeys in height; and

(b) a building located in the rear 25% area of the site must not exceed 1 storey in height.

Private and communal open space

C12. Private open space provided to comply with F7.7.C11 is to include at least one area that:

(a) has reasonable access to a living area of the subject dwelling; and

(b) is on a compatible level with a living area of the subject dwelling; and

(c) contains an area suitable for drying clothes; and

(d) has a slope of less than 10%; and

(e) complies with the minimum area and minimum width, based on the number of bedrooms in the dwelling, as specified in Part

F7.7 - Table 2

| Number of bedrooms | Minimum area | Minimum width |

| One (including bedsits) | 15m2 | 3m |

Part F7: Other Development

C13. Despite requirements for F7.7.C11, concession is made for reduced private open space when the deficit is consolidated in a communal open space area that is well designed, fit for purpose and appropriately landscaped to achieve excellent amenity outcomes and opportunities for social interaction.

Landscaping

C14. Development that provides communal open space is to articulate the intended use or activities of the communal space and be designed accordingly. The assessment of this communal open space will be a merit based approach based on the intended use or activity identified.

C15. Communal open space should provide features as necessary to suit its purpose, for example, appropriate as seating, tool storage, barbeque and shade facilities, potting benches or bird baths and contain plantings which characterise and enhance its functional performance, be it growing vegetables, socialising, bird watching or meditation. Large expanses of open lawn without associated plantings are to be avoided.

C16. Deep soil planting areas are preferably located in communal open space areas to complement the intended use and to retain or promote mid and rear block canopy planting. Where space and services permit, provide additional deep soil areas within the front setback to achieve high quality streetfront planting of an appropriate scale.

Parking

C17. Notwithstanding parking controls for seniors housing contained elsewhere in this DCP, where suitable, and subject to a merit based assessment, consolidated parking is encouraged, if the overall design results in superior outcomes for landscaping, communal open space and amenity. This may include car parking spaces being allocated so as to be available to all dwellings on a shared basis.

Adaptability and Accessibility

C18. In the case of self contained seniors housing dwellings, 100% of dwellings are to be adaptable, designed and constructed in accordance with Adaptable House Class B in Australian Standard 4299 - Adaptable Housing, incorporating as a minimum all first priority desirable features.

Part F7: Other Development

C19. For self contained seniors housing developments, variations will be considered regarding exclusion from the provision of private roofed car parking spaces (clauses 3.7.1 and clause 3.7.3 AS4299), where it can be demonstrated that doing so will result in strong landscape and communal open space outcomes.

C20. In the case of development comprising 5 or more self contained dwellings, at least 20% of all dwellings (to the nearest whole number) shall be accessible to people with a disability. Developments containing less than 5 dwellings shall include a minimum of 1 dwelling that is accessible to people with a disability.

C21. Development standards concerning accessibility and useability for residential care facilities are not specified in this Part. For relevant standards, see the Commonwealth aged care accreditation standards and the Building Code of Australia.
