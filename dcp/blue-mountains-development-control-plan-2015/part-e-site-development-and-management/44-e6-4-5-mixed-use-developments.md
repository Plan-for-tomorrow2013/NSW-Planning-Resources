### E6.4.5.	 Mixed Use Developments

Where both residential and commercial uses occur on a single lot, integrated on-site waste management provisions are required to permit a safe and efficient waste collections service the various uses proposed.

#### Controls

C1. The term ‘mixed-use’ is defined in the Blue Mountains LEP 2015

C2. The controls outlined in Part E6.4.3 apply to the residential component of the mixed-use development

C3. The controls outlined in Part E6.4.4 apply to the non-residential component of the mixed-use development.

C4. The integrated on-site waste collection areas for residential and commercial waste streams to be stored within separate, enclosed and locked storage/collection rooms.

### Part E7

### CONTAMINATION


### Introduction

Land contamination is most often the result of past or present land uses. It can arise from activities that took place on or adjacent to a site and be the result of improper chemical handling or disposal practices or accidental spillages or leakages of chemicals during manufacturing or storage. Activities not directly related to the site may also cause contamination. For example, diffuse sources such as polluted groundwater migrating under a site or dust settling out from industrial emissions.

When carrying out planning functions under the Environmental Planning and Assessment Act 1979 (EP&A Act), a consent authority must consider the possibility that land may be contaminated and as such may pose a potential risk to health or the environment. Decisions must then be made as to whether the land should be remediated, or use of the land restricted, in order to reduce the risk.
