### E2.4. Travel Plan

#### Explanation

A Travel Plan is a management strategy for delivering behavioural change and sustainable travel patterns across a development. It is used to influence travel behaviour associated with the ongoing use and occupation of the development

site. Different land uses may require different forms of travel plans.

#### Objectives

O1. To encourage greater use of public transport, cycling, walking and car sharing by employees, visitors and residents.

O2. Reduce car trips, particularly single-occupancy car trips.

#### Controls

C1. A Travel Plan must be submitted with all development applications where the estimated peak trip generation is greater or equal to:

(a) 30 vehicles per hour for non-residential development, or

(b) 20 vehicles per hour for residential development, or

Part E2: Traffic. Parking and access

(c) Is likely to generate significant traffic impacts according to Council.

Trip generation must be determined in accordance with the TfNSW Guide to Transport Impact Assessment.

C2. The Travel Plan is to be prepared in accordance with TfNSW Guide to Transport Impact Assessment by a suitability qualified traffic or transport consultant.

Note: The undertakings made in a Travel Plan submitted with a development

application may be included as conditions to any development consent.

### Introduction

Accessibility is an integral element of any development, particularly in the context of the aging population. The provisions within this section aim to ensure that people with a disability, the elderly and those with limited mobility have dignified and equitable access to buildings, facilities and services. This part also addresses the need for buildings to be adaptable, so that as people age or their situation changes, they are able to easily modify living spaces in a relatively straightforward way to suit their needs.

Housing choice is also another component of accessible housing. These provisions recognise that more modest, and therefore affordable dwellings are likely to be in demand as family sizes get smaller and the population ages.
