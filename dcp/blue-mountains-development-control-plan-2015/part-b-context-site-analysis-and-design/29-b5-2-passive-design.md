### B5.2. Passive Design

#### Introduction

Passive design works with the local climate to reduce or eliminate the need for additional mechanical heating or cooling, depending on your location. Certain forms of mechanical cooling and heating, such as air conditioners, are very energy intensive. With passive design, building features such as orientation, ventilation, thermal mass and insulation work together to take advantage of natural sources of heating and cooling, such as sun and breezes. It is best to use passive design principles when designing or building a new home, but many features of passive design can be added through renovations or simple home improvements. For more information on passive design refer to www.yourhome.gov.au/passive-design

#### Controls

C1. Development is to be designed to respond to the site analysis, prepared in accordance with the requirements of Part B1 Site and Context Analysis of this DCP.

C2. Development must enable natural ventilation:

(a) Windows should be openable to enable cross ventilation, excluding windows that are primarily for light ingress such as high silled windows.

(b) Ceiling or wall mounted fans should be in all habitable rooms (main living areas and bedrooms). To avoid injury, a ceiling height of 2.7 metres is recommended in living rooms.

Note: Ceiling heights exceeding 2.7m are not recommended, as higher ceilings hinder the efficient heating of rooms in winter.

C3. Development is to be oriented to achieve optimum solar access to thermal mass in winter, and shade thermal mass in summer. To achieve this:

(a) North and west facing windows are to be shaded from direct summer sun with external horizontal shading devices such as awnings, upper floor balconies, eaves and overhangs, and be designed to allow for the penetration of winter sun into the building.

(b) Vertical shading devices such as vertical louvres or fins that consider the oblique angles of the sun should be utilised on east and west facing windows, while allowing for the penetration of winter sun into the building.

(c) The use of trees and shrubs as an additional method of shading a surface or window is encouraged. Species selection and planting location needs to be carefully considered to ensure summer shading but allow for winter sun to enter the building.

C4. Living areas and private open space should have a northerly aspect wherever possible.

C5. Where possible, buildings should be positioned with the long axis extending east-west to maximise the length of north-facing walls and windows to living areas. A depth of 10-14 metres is ideal to allow natural light and cross ventilation.

C6. Buildings should be designed to have an area of roof, with appropriate orientation and pitch, that is suitable for the installation of solar panels. Solar panels are to be located clear of shading from nearby buildings and canopy trees, and should take into account the potential future building forms on adjacent properties.

Note: Any battery systems installed in association with solar panel systems must comply with AS5139:2019 (or most current version). For dwellings on bush-fire prone land, batteries with fire-resistant chemistry are to be used.

C7. Development must not unduly impact upon the ability of surrounding properties to achieve passive design strategies and solar access.

C8. Insulation is to be used in external walls and roofs to reduce heat escaping from a building in winter and to maintain a lower internal temperature in summer.

C9. Internal walls and partitions are to be positioned and designed to allow for any prevailing passage of air through the building, while allowing for the enclosure of rooms to create smaller spaces for retaining warmth and efficient heating during winter.

C10. Development should utilise operable natural ventilation systems to evacuate heat from roof or underfloor cavities in summer, and to retain warmth in winter.

C11. Development is to incorporate landscaping that provides canopy and vegetation for cooling to provide resilience during hot and dry periods. Landscaping on bushfire prone land is to be designed in accordance with Part C4 Bushfire of this DCP.
