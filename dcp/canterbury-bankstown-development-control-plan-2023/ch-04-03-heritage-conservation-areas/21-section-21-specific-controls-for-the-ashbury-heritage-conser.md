## SECTION 21–SPECIFIC CONTROLS FOR THE ASHBURY HERITAGE CONSERVATION AREA

#### Explanation

This section applies to the Ashbury Heritage Conservation Area. The heritage conservation area comprises the whole suburb of Ashbury and part of Croydon Park. This section provides guidance for the design of development in Ashbury so that it will be consistent with the heritage character of the area.

In Ashbury, private open space is in backyards that are predominantly grassed and often feature substantial trees at the rear. This pattern of mature and substantial tree planting to the rear of houses results in the mid-block planting that is characteristic of suburbs throughout Sydney. Landscaping of the front garden assists new houses to fit in with the surrounding neighbourhood. Front gardens are characteristically grassed, with significant tree and shrub planting.

While the controls focus on dwelling houses, the principles and controls (where appropriate) also apply to all other types of development ensure the retention of the heritage character of Ashbury. Note: If applicable to an application, the development controls of this section will prevail if there is an inconsistency with any other development controls in Chapter 4.3 of this DCP.

#### Objectives

O1 To ensure that new dwellings and additions to existing dwellings are compatible in scale with nearby characteristic dwellings as well as the immediate properties.

O2 To ensure that the scale of buildings relates to the topography and requires minimal cut and fill.

O3 To minimise the visual impact of driveway crossings.

O4 To maintain the existing mature, well established trees and characteristic plantings.

O5 To maintain the pattern of mid-block plantings.

#### Development controls

#### Storey limit

21.1 A maximum building height of two storeys applies to all buildings for residential zoned properties.

21.2 The height and bulk of first floor additions is to be minimised.

21.3 On sites where the land slopes downhill away from the street, use the slope of the land and place floors at a lower level to minimise building bulk.

21.4 The maximum height of fill is 300mm above existing ground level, at any point.

21.5 A foundation area of up to 1m in height is acceptable.

#### Setbacks

21.6 In the street elevation of new dwellings, a minimum of 50% of the building is to be built to the predominant building line, and the remainder of the dwelling may be behind the predominant building line. If a street has no predominant building line, build to a building line established by nearby buildings.

21.7 On streets with a staggered building alignment, the streetscape pattern is to be reinforced by maintaining the typical angle and distance from the front boundary.

21.8 The front facade is to be oriented towards the street boundary.

21.9 Any additions are to be located on or behind the predominant building line.

#### Building expression and streetscape

21.10 New buildings should reflect the consistent horizontal lines of elements of houses along the street, such as:

(a) ground level;

(b) base course – the architectural expression of the base of the house, often in different materials or finishes such as rendered brick or roughcast stone;

(c) verandah and balustrade heights;

(d) window sill and head heights;

(e) door heights;

(f) eave lines; and

(g) ridgelines.

21.11 Facades are to be horizontal in proportions and asymmetrical, and use vertical proportions for features such as windows.

21.12 Provide a break in long side walls and roofs.

21.13 Alterations and additions to the existing building are to maintain the appearance of a single storey house from the street.

21.14 The design of facades is to pay particular attention to the:

(a) mass, the arrangement and articulation of the various elements and parts of the building;

(b) roof form and pitch; and

(c) the use of architectural elements such as bay windows, porches, verandahs and balconies.

#### Driveways, garages and carports

21.15 A maximum width for driveways is 2.7m at the allotment boundary.

#### Fences

21.16 Timber fences are preferred for side fences facing streets.

21.17 Front garden walls and fences on the street boundary are to be no higher than 1.2m.

21.18 On corner sites where the facade of the dwelling presents to two street frontages, fencing is to be no higher than 1.2m for the front yard area on both frontages.

#### Landscape

21.19 A minimum 35% of the site area, at natural ground level, is to be maintained for open space.

21.20 A minimum 25% of the site area is to be maintained as soft landscaping.

21.21 All front setbacks are to consist predominantly of soft landscaping. The only paved areas in the front setback are the driveway and pathways to and around the house.
