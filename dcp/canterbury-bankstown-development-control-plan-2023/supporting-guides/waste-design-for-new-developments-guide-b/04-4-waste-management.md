## 4. Waste Management

### Considerations

### 4.1	 General

### Considerations

It is essential that you have read the components of this Guide that are relevant to the size of the development in selecting and designing waste management facilities. It is important that you:

•	 Ensure all dwellings have internal waste storage;

•	 Have a thorough understanding of the waste generated by your development and the number of bins to be allocated by Council and stored within the development;

•	 Ensure that the development can be integrated with Council’s standard HRV as per Australian Standard 2890.2 waste service;

•	 Consider what access is required to the site by collection staff and/or vehicles to facilitate the safe and efficient waste servicing of the development.

Understanding the waste facilities required, such as bin allocation and the size (and dimensions) of bins ensures that the bin storage area for the development can be designed and integrated into the overall design of the development. This will maximise convenience for residents, as well as ensuring amenity impacts such as visual, noise and odour are minimised.

The nominated collection point must be able to be accessed by collection staff. It is vital that you understand and plan for the access arrangements required. This includes providing adequate vehicular access , parking and/or manoeuvring for the standard HRV waste collection vehicle where on-site collection is required.

Council or its contractors are solely to provide the waste services to all residential development types as required under the Local Government Act 1993.

### 4.3	 Standard Waste Service

It is essential the waste management system selected considers occupant amenity and safety, as well as provides bin storage areas that can be used and accessed conveniently.

In designing and selecting the appropriate waste management system for MDH it is important that you consider:

•	 How many bins will be allocated for your development and what are their sizes?

•	 Does the number of dwellings (five or less) and street frontage allow all bins to be safely placed for kerbside collection?

•	 Is the development better suited to utilise Council’s collect and return service?

•	 How long will the collection vehicle be parked at the front of the development?

•	 How frequently is the development serviced?

The following information will assist in determining the appropriate collection options and supporting requirements to be taken into consideration during the detailed design of the development.

#### 4.3.1	 Collection Services

Council offers a range of waste collection services for MDH developments including:

•	 Kerbside collection: general waste, recycling and garden organics are collected from the kerbside;

•	 Collect and Return: collection staff enter the development to collect bins from a nominated area and return them once emptied; and

•	 On-site collection: collection occurs within a development site’s boundary by an HRV at a nominated area.

The type of service available for your development varies depending on the number of dwellings, on-site conditions, amenity and safety issues, the number of bins requiring collection, the size of the bins and time taken to empty the bins.

The collect and return service is not suitable for developments that are located on classified roads and roads with high vehicle and pedestrian traffic.

If collect and return is deemed unsuitable by Council, the development must be designed to be serviced on-site, either at ground level, basement or via a loading dock and the development will need to accommodate an HRV.

An adequate bin storage area is to be provided within the development site to store all allocated bins. Depending on the nature and density of the development, individual or communal bin storage areas may be provided.
