## SECTION 1–INTRODUCTION

Prior to the submission of any application for this type of development, it is recommended that you speak with Council’s Waste Planning Officers to confirm your buildings waste and recycling requirements. This is a free service available by contacting the Waste Planning Officer on 9707 9000 or by email Council@cbcity.nsw.gov.au

Waste requirements and services have changed over the last 50 years, and many of our older buildings are no longer suitable for the current waste collection service. This guide acknowledges that existing buildings may not be able to meet the requirements of Waste Design in New Developments Guides. It offers concessions to allow existing buildings to better meet the requirements of the current waste collection system both now and into the future.

This guide is not to be used for new developments.

#### Applicable Development Type

This Guide (Guide G) applies to residential flat buildings (RFBs)and multi dwelling housing (MDH) developments including units, apartments, townhouses and villas that were approved prior to 2012, and where the current communal waste infrastructure is no longer aligned with the Council’s Standard Domestic Waste Service.

This includes the retrofitting of existing bin rooms including the construction, demolition, and/or expansion of communal waste storage areas.

Existing older buildings may not have been built to include internal infrastructure for many new waste technologies. Retrofitting that requires significant changes to the internal layout and structure of the building is not covered by this guide. If a developer wishes to make these changes all the requirements for the new developments are to be upheld.

#### Objectives of the Guide

The City of Canterbury Bankstown (Council) aims to integrate waste management into the design fabric of urban planning to support effective collection and management of waste as an essential service.

This includes identifying sustainable waste outcomes in all developments that are safe and efficient, reduce waste generation, increase recycling and resource recovery and contribute to the built form and liveability of the community.

This Guide has been prepared to assist you to achieve the following objectives and comply with Council’s planning controls:

1\. To maximise resource recovery and encourage source separation of waste, reuse and recycling by ensuring developments provide adequate and appropriate bin storage areas and collection facilities;

2\. To ensure development incorporates well designed and responsive bin storage areas and collection facilities that are convenient and accessible to occupants;

3\. To maximise residential amenity and minimise adverse environmental and health related impacts associated with waste management such as odour from bin storage areas and noise from collection facilities;

4\. To ensure bin storage areas and collection facilities are designed to integrate with and meet the minimum requirements for Council’s domestic waste services;

5\. To ensure development facilitates all waste streams being handled, stored and collected in a manner to reduce risk to health and safety of all users including maintenance (such as caretakers), collection staff and contractors (and required vehicles and equipment); and

6\. To assist in achieving Federal and State Government waste minimisation and diversion targets as set by relevant legislation, regulations and strategies.

In addition, the design and ongoing management of waste management facilities encourages greater participation with separation of waste streams and can reduce instances of illegal dumping.

#### Waste Reduction and Resource Recovery Target

Year on year the amount of waste generated by Australians is increasing. With a growing population the Councils waste management service and associated infrastructure must be improved upon to support the separation of waste encouraging recycling and resource recovery.

The NSW EPA sets targets for Councils waste diversion from landfill. Existing developments heavily impact Council achieving these targets through existing poor waste infrastructure. By implementing improvements in design, construction and maintenance of waste management facilities we can help reduce waste to landfill and increase recycling.

Retrofitting bin bays will help to ensure that garbage, recycling, organic and bulky waste produced on-site are effectively managed to improve resource recovery.

It will also enhance the amenity, ease of use, environmental performance and ultimately, the reputation of developments with well managed waste facilities.

#### Waste Management and the Development Application Process

This Guide is to be read in conjunction with Council’s Planning and Building Application Lodgement Guide and should be used when developing a Waste Management Plan.

#### Waste Management Plan

A Waste Management Plan (WMP) is required to accompany all Development Applications (DAs)and should comply with the requirements contained within this Guide and the Canterbury-Bankstown Development Control Plan 2023.

The WMP is an important planning document that will not only be utilised as part of the DA process, but during construction and for the ongoing use of the development. Conditions of consent will be used to enforce the commitments contained within the WMP, including the requirement that the ongoing management section of the WMP is included in the by-laws of strata properties. This will ensure that all relevant parties (i.e. residents, property

managers) are aware of the WMP and that it will continue to apply as a working reference for the life of the building and community living there. Council has a template WMP to support all DAs which addresses the demolition, construction, and occupancy stage of the development. It is mandatory to use Council’s template.

Should you require advice on the preparation of the WMP you may contact the Waste Planning Officer on 9707 9000 Council@cbcity.nsw.gov.au

The WMP is to provide the following:

• Details of the developments handling of construction and demolition waste;

• Details of the ongoing management of waste by residents, including the types and estimated quantities;

• Plans of the proposed development that show the location and space allocated to the waste management facilities, along with the nominated waste collection point;

• Identification of the travel path of access to the bin storage area/s by residents and collection staff;

• Details of ongoing management, storage and collection of waste, including responsibility for cleaning, transfer of bins between storage areas and collection points, implementation and maintenance of signage, and security of storage areas; and

• Where appropriate to the nature of the development, a summary document for residents to inform them of the building’s ongoing waste management arrangements. The completed WMP, including drawings submitted by the applicant, will be used in the Council assessment of the waste management systems for the new development.

#### Demolition and Construction

The WMP is to detail how this will be achieved and is to be submitted with any new DA (this may include change of use DAs). The storage, handling and disposal of any demolition and construction waste must be undertaken in accordance with the requirements of the Protection of Environment Operations Act 1997 and associated regulations Designation of appropriately licensed facilities (recycling and landfill) to receive the demolition and construction waste should be included.

The volume of waste from demolition and construction activities is to be minimised by avoidance and reduction practices, re-use on-site and the recycling of materials.

#### Waste Management Considerations

General Considerations

It is essential that you have read the components of this Guide that are relevant to your development type in selecting and designing a waste management facility. It is important that you:

• Have a thorough understanding of the waste generated by your development and the number of bins to be allocated by Council and stored within the development; and

• Consider what access is required to the site by collection staff and/or vehicles to facilitate the safe and efficient waste servicing of the development.

Understanding the waste infrastructure required, such as bin allocation and the size (and dimensions) of bins ensures that the bin storage area for the can be designed to maximise convenience for residents, and ensure visual, noise and odour amenity impacts are minimised. The nominated collection point must be accessible to collection staff. It is vital to understand and plan for the access arrangements required. This includes providing adequate vehicular access and manoeuvring for an HRV where on-site collection is proposed.

Council or its contractors are solely to provide the waste services to all residential development types as required under the Local Government Act 1993.

#### Standard Waste Service

Collection Services

Kerbside waste collection is considered unsuitable for RFB and MDH developments with six or more dwellings. This is due to amenity and safety issues, the number of bins requiring collection, the size of the bins and time taken to empty the bins.

Council will accept a proposal for kerbside collection only in the following circumstances:

• The existing development is currently serviced by the kerbside presentation of bins;

• There is insufficient space in the front of the development site to allow for a bin storage or holding area to be built within 10m of the kerb. This includes if the frontage of the property is private open space not common property; and

• There is sufficient kerbside space for the safe presentation of bins.

Council’s preferred method of collection services for RFBs and MDH are:

• Collect and Return: collection staff enter the development to collect bins from a nominated area within 10m from the kerb and return them once emptied; and

• On-site collection: collection occurs within a development site’s boundary by an HRV at a nominated area.

The type of service available for your development varies depending on the number of dwellings, on-site conditions, amenity and safety issues, the number of bins requiring collection, the bin sizes and time taken to empty the bins.

The collect and return service is not suitable for developments that are located on classified roads and roads with high vehicle and pedestrian traffic. A list of classified roads can be found at transport.nsw.gov.au

If collect and return is deemed unsuitable by Council, the development must be either designed to be serviced on-site, at ground level via a HRV acceptable loading dock or kerbside if it meets the above requirements.
