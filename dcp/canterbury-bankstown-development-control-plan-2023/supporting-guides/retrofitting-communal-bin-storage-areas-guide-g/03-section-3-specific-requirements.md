## SECTION 3–SPECIFIC REQUIREMENTS

#### Communal bin storage area

A communal bin storage area must be designed so it is compatible with the overall design of the development. This includes materials and finishes that are in keeping with both the existing building and the surrounding streetscape.

The location of the bin storage area is important to ensure it can be accessed conveniently and will not impact on residential amenity regarding noise, odour, or visual impacts.

The bin storage area must be able to accommodate the required number of bins, and the volume of waste and recycling expected to be generated between collections based upon the volumes calculated in tables 2, 3 and 4. Collection frequency of bins will remain the same as the current service. This frequency may be increased for larger developments, only after discussion with Council’s Waste Planning Officers.

#### Size and layout

• The development must provide a communal bin storage area of sufficient size to accommodate all bins allocated for the development;

• Sufficient space must be provided to ensure there is adequate room to manoeuvre, clean and maintain all waste and recycling bins for the development (minimum aisle space of 1.5m and 15cm between bins);

• Size must not be excessive, to discourage the dumping of other bulky waste in the bin storage area; and

• The area is free from obstructions and steps, so as not to restrict the movement and servicing of the bins.

#### Location

Bin bays are to be:

• Located at ground level;

• Located where its use and operation will improve the amenity of occupants in terms of appearance, noise, and odour, including locating the bin room away from habitable windows and doorways of both the site and neighbouring blocks;

• If bins are required to be moved for collection, it is done in a safe and efficient manner in accordance with Work Health and Safety legislation;

• Orientated as to limit the visual impact of the overall frontage of the property (see Figure 1);

• The area cannot be easily accessed by the public domain; and

• Located to retain the maximum amount of landscaping and permeable surfaces.

Design

Bin bays are to be:

• A designated room or enclosure, to prevent vermin accessing a roof is desirable, however site restrictions must be considered;

• Must be compatible with the overall design of the development; and

• Screened from public view.

Access

Bin bays are to be:

• Located so access for all intended users is safe and convenient and in accordance with AS 1428 (Set) - 2003: Design for access and mobility; and

• Any doorways will be at least 2m wide and open outwards.

Construction

• Bin bays are to be constructed with; Floors must be constructed of concrete at least 75mm thick;

• Exiting drainage is to be retained. For sites without existing drainage, a Sydney Water approved drainage fitting may be provided;

• The floors must be finished to a smooth, non-slip, even surface.

• The walls must be constructed in non- absorbent material capable of being cleaned up to 1.4m in height, with perforated screening above to a height of 1.8m. Acceptable materials include: o bricks, o stone, o concrete, o metal screening, o precoated steel (commonly known by brand name colourbond), or

o timber.

• A minimum 2.1m unobstructed room height is required in accordance with the Building Code of Australia for all areas with rooves;

• Any internal walls, ceiling and floors must be finished in a light colour smooth faced non- absorbent material capable of being cleaned;

• Is to be provided with an adequate access to running water;

• A close-fitting and self-closing door or gate operable from within the area;

• Must be constructed to discourage the entry of birds and vermin; and

• Be provided with adequate light and ventilation.

Temporary Collection Area (Collect and Return)

If the communal bin storage cannot be located within 10m of the kerb, a temporary collection area may be required.  The temporary collection area must be of a sufficient size to allow the temporary storage of all allocated bins for the development. They will also require a caretaker to transfer all allocated bins from the communal bin storage area to the temporary collection area the day before the designated collection day and return them once emptied.

The bin-carting route from the communal bin storage area to the temporary collection area is to be:

• Direct and less than 30 metres;

• Minimum 2m wide solid and impervious surface;

• Non-slip, free from obstacles and steps;

• A maximum grade of 1:30 (3%);

• Wholly within property boundaries;

• Without crossing a private lot; and

• Compliant with Work, Health and Safety legislation. To reduce manual handling, bin handling equipment should be used e.g. bin tug.

The collection vehicle will stop at the nominated kerbside collection point and will collect and return bins to the temporary collection area. The health and safety of all users including caretakers and collection staff is an important consideration when selecting an appropriate location for this area.

Requirements of the temporary collection area:

• Located on the ground floor;

• Within 10m from the nominated kerbside collection point;

• Any doorways to be a minimum 2m;

• Only temporarily store bins so they can be serviced;

• Be located fully within the development site;

• Be of sufficient size to accommodate all bins with additional room for manoeuvring (minimum aisle space of 1.5m and 15cm between bins); and

• Be clearly separated from car parking bays, footpaths and landscaped areas.

Bin-Carting Route (Collect and Return)

For the collect and return service, the bin carting route from the communal bin storage area to the kerbside collection point, must comply with the following requirements:

• To be direct and less than 10 metres;

• Include a kerb ramp at the nominated collection point;

• Minimum 2m wide solid and impervious;

• Does not pass through any internal walkways, doors or rooms;

• Non-slip, free from obstacles and steps;

• A maximum grade of 1:30 (3%); and

• Compliant with Work, Health and Safety for collection staff.

Bulky Waste

Council provides a collection service for bulky household waste, such as whitegoods, mattresses and household furniture.

Developments may choose to provide an area for residents to store bulky waste awaiting collection to prevent the illegal dumping of materials on the kerbside or within common areas. The size of the bulky waste area must be appropriate to the development, see Table 7 for the minimum area required.

The bulky waste storage area is to be separate to the bin storage area(s), however the design is to comply with the requirements detailed in Section 5.3.1 of this Guide. Multiple buildings may provide separate areas.

Bulky waste rooms are particularly important for developments with high rental turnover where residents may leave between collections.

Supplementary Recycling Service

Developments with more than 50 dwellings are eligible to receive additional recycling services to increase recovery of material and to prevent illegal dumping on the kerbside or in common areas. This program requires additional recycling storage area(s) to be provided for residents to store household items, such as clothing, mattresses, polystyrene, bulky cardboard and electronic waste.

To receive the Supplementary Recycling Service a minimum area of 9m2 is required and the area is to be designed to comply with the following requirements. The area/s must:

• Be constructed as to meet the same standards as per bin storage areas in 5.3.1;

• be separate to the bin storage area or room(s) and the bulky waste storage area;

• not be visible from any street frontage; and

• Where there are multiple buildings, separate areas may be provided.

#### Design for Ongoing Use

The ongoing management of waste systems is an important consideration to ensure amenity of residents is maintained, and that caretaker and collection staff safety is assured. The waste management system, including the ongoing role and responsibilities of the caretaker can also impact on the operational costs of the development, which will be passed onto residents. It is important to identify, establish and communicate responsibilities of both residents and caretakers to ensure effective waste management at the operational stage of the development. Council recommends an active caretaker be employed to ensure effective and efficient ongoing waste management.

A site caretaker or manager is required to:

• Maintain and clean all bin storage areas;

• Maintain and clean temporary holding areas (if applicable);

• Clean and wash all bins regularly;

• Manage all bin transfers and rotations;

• Checking the number of bins and reporting any damages to Council;

• Manage bulky waste and additional recycling storage areas and arrange appropriate collections (if applicable);

• Arranging the prompt removal of dumped rubbish;

• Ensuring the recycling bins are free of contamination (which includes but not limited to garbage, plastic bags, clothing and polystyrene); and

• Ensuring all residents are informed and kept up to date with the use of the waste management system.

The site caretaker or manager is to undertake the above actions regularly to ensure the waste management system operates to a satisfactory standard.

#### Inspections by Council

Prior to the commencement of any collect-and-return or onsite waste services, an authorised Council Waste Officer must inspect the communal bin storage area and bin-carting route to ensure they have been constructed in accordance with the approved plans. The officer must also confirm that the development can be adequately serviced by the

Council. Conditions of consent will be imposed requiring these inspections be undertaken. Contact Council on 9707 9000 to arrange these inspections.

#### Deed of Agreement and Indemnity

Where collection staff or vehicles are required to enter private property to perform the service, Council will require an unimpeded easement for access to undertake on-site or collect and return services. The development is also required to indemnify Council and its Contractors against claims for wear and tear of access roads or other parts of the building. A condition of consent will be imposed requiring an Indemnity Agreement to be entered into prior to the issue of the Occupation Certificate and delivery of bins.

#### Onsite collection requirements

Onsite collection is a preferred service system for newly developed residential flat buildings, particularly new high rise within town centres. If a retrofitting site has adequate space and clearance heights onsite collection of waste may be considered. To receive an onsite collection, the development must meet the requirements for onsite collection in new developments.
