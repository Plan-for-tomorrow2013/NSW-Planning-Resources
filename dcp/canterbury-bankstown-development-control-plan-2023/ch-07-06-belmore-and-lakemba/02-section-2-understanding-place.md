#### 2.2.1 Lakemba High Street

#### The Lakemba High Street character area

#### will have its vibrant multicultural identity

enhanced, offering a dynamic mix of shopping, dining and community services, alongside new housing to support a

#### thriving atmosphere day and night.

Building on the success of the Ramadan Markets, Haldon Street will be prioritised as a pedestrianised space lined with vibrant retail uses that activate the street level, complemented by shoptop housing above to create a lively civic area. The bulk and scale of new development will reflect the predominant 2 to 3 storey street wall height in Haldon Street and the parapet character, with fine grain tenancies incorporating masonry and brick. Where possible, pre 1950 shop facades will be retained and sympathetically restored. Additional storeys will be setback on a podium, featuring green terraces and balconies.

The character area will feature an improved pedestrian environment and activated shared lanes, making it more accessible and attractive for walking and cycling in and around the new Metro station. The local services and shops will provide convenient amenities.

Maintaining and celebrating the existing low-scale character of these areas is crucial to ensuring for a cohesive streetscape and protection of low scale historic character. New developments should draw inspiration from the design and materials of existing homes, reflecting the patterns of pitched roofs, face brick and masonry materials and appropriate finishes. Limiting driveway crossovers through placing them next to each other or in rear laneways will enhance walkability and amenity in the area, contributing to a greener and shadier environment allowing for more landscaping and tree canopy along the streets and in setbacks. Within this character area, there are two distinctly low-density zones along Dennis Street and Passey/ Paxton Avenue, where the existing character should be maintained. The area surrounding Dennis Street Reserve is characterised by largely intact single storey interwar houses. Any developments adjacent to this area should be designed to respect and complement the historic character of the existing dwellings and the overall streetscape.

#### 2.2.7 Industrial lands

The industrial land character areas play a

#### vital role in supporting the local economy

#### by offering employment opportunities

#### and essential services. There are two

#### character areas.

The Punchbowl Road Industrial area features medium-scale, low-rise light industrial uses with direct access to Punchbowl Road, connecting to larger employment lands in Greenacre and Enfield. This area is well-established and situated at the northern periphery of the Precinct. By contrast, the Lakemba Street Industrial area is a smaller, fine grain cluster of light industrial tenancies embedded within the residential  context. There is a strong commitment to preserving existing industrial zoned land with unchanged controls.  As the surrounding context evolves, a sensitive and functional interface between industrial lands and residential areas will be essential for ensuring for amenity and mitigating impacts.

In particular, the Lakemba Street Industrial area presents opportunities to enhance streetscape amenity through tree planting, landscaping, and the introduction of pedestrian and cycle friendly improvements linking Belmore and Lakemba Stations. Over time, tenancies could incorporate complementary uses such as cafés or other local retail offers to activate the street and contribute to a more walkable neighbourhood.

### 2.3	 Connecting with Country

#### Objectives

O1. To recognise, respect and respond to Aboriginal cultural heritage in the planning, development and daily life of the Belmore and Lakemba Precinct.

#### Principles

P1. To take a Country centred approach and thinking that is layered into new development in the Precinct consistent with the Government Architect NSW ‘Connecting with Country framework’ (CWCF).

P2. To integrate Aboriginal cultural practices, history, narratives and values in the design of proposed development.

P3. To encourage development to respond to the physical and cultural connection of the local Aboriginal community to the land.

P4. Foster collaboration and knowledge sharing between Aboriginal and non-Aboriginal individuals.

#### Controls

> [Picture on page 38, not reproduced as text; see the original PDF.]

C1. For development where clause 6.15 of the Canterbury-Bankstown Local Environmental Plan 2023 applies, the development must demonstrate:

a)	 How the design considers aspects of Country under Sections 3 and 4 of the Government Architect NSW ‘Connecting with Country framework’;

b)	 How the design gives regard to the culture and heritage of the traditional custodians of the land, the Darug People, on which the development is proposed;

c)	 Connecting design with the existing site topography, natural environment and landscape setting; and

d)	 Incorporating sustainable practices by using natural building materials.

C2. For development where Design Excellence clause of the Canterbury-Bankstown Local Environmental Plan 2023 applies, the development must determine the appropriate scope of engagement with the Aboriginal community and its representatives, whether it be high, medium or low as outlined in Section 3.1 of the Government Architect NSW ‘Connecting with Country framework’. If engaging with the Aboriginal community, development must:

a)	 Demonstrate the methods and outcomes from the engagement with the Aboriginal community; and

b)	 Adhere to protocols outlined in the NSW Government’s Practice Note ‘Engaging with Aboriginal Communities’ and ‘Aboriginal Cultural and Intellectual Property Protocol’.

C3. If significant items such as ancestral remains or artefacts are discovered during subsoil investigations, excavation, demolition or other earthworks, the applicant must engage with the Aboriginal community to determine culturally appropriate handling, repatriation or reburial.

C4. Development that proposes open space and landscaping should demonstrate consideration of ‘walking Country’ which reflects Aboriginal cultural practices, artefacts, artwork and narratives. Aspects involving the senses of seeing, hearing, touching, smelling and tasting, should be included.
