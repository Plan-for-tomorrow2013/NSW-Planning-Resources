### 3.1 Public domain strategy

The public domain consists of streets, squares, parks, shared lanes and pedestrian links. These elements, in conjunction with the built form interface to private land, play a vital role in creating unique, vibrant and sustainable places that are attractive, inviting and inclusive for all. The public domain strategy ensures housing growth is supported by the delivery of high-quality public spaces that encourage walking, cycling, community interaction and engagement. This section outlines key opportunities for enhancing the public domain in the Belmore and Lakemba Precinct, as identified in Figure 23. Public domain strategy.

#### Objectives

O1. To improve the quality, appearance and function of the public domain.

O2. Provide a diverse range of quality public spaces to support residents, workers and visitors.

O3. Ensure a high level of permeability through the Precinct.

O4. Provide high quality, functional and attractive formal and informal spaces for community interaction.

O5. To establish a vibrant and attractive Precinct where the network of streets, shared lanes and open spaces support the increase in population density, the increased pedestrian movement and footpath trading activities.

### 3.3	 Streets, shared lanes,

### pedestrian links and arcades

Streets, shared lanes, pedestrian links and arcades are places of movement: to watch, to pass through and see the movement of people. This public framework provides for social and commercial exchange. In urban areas, a well-designed network of shared lanes and pedestrian links are essential for improving permeability and connecting to main streets and open space, creating walkability and equity of access and enjoyment. The street should be safe and inviting to pedestrians and cyclists while accommodating vehicles and traffic and be enhanced by tree canopies that promote amenity and sustainability. Pedestrian links prioritise pedestrians, featuring open skies and awnings, while shared lanes accommodate pedestrians and cyclists and driveway access. Service vehicles operate in shared lanes when they are less used. Arcades serve as internal retail 'lanes' that enhance permeability and provide an engaging shopping experience. These narrow, intimate spaces encourage pedestrians to come and stay and facilitate connections through street blocks, reducing walking distances. New development is essential for contributing to a high-quality, permeable, and connected movement network that is green, inviting and safe for everyone. ### 3.4	 Tree canopy, green cover

### and deep soil

Trees are essential for their contribution to the amenity and character of the Precinct. When appropriately selected, located, planted and maintained, trees provide a multitude of benefits to the urban environment including reducing heat gain, promoting amenity, providing shade and mitigating climate change.

One of the most effective solutions to mitigate urban heat gain is expanding tree canopy cover. Tree provide shade, cool the environment through transpiration and reduce surface temperature in densely built areas, where heat can become trapped by concrete and asphalt. Temperature differences between locations with canopy and without canopy gaps can be significant. Green cover provides further greening and shade to buildings and to the site itself. This will protect buildings from radiant heat gain and provide a please aesthetic to the public domain. Deep soil is 'landscaped area'. These areas of soil are not covered by buildings or structures. Deep soil provides for the planting of canopy trees, increases water filtration and reduces stormwater runoff. It is beneficial to co-locate deep soil with communal open space to provide shade and amenity for those using these spaces.

### 3.6	 Shade and the reduction

### of urban heat

Belmore and Lakemba face a significant urban heat island effect due to climate change and low tree canopy cover. The combination of roads, infrastructure and buildings with limited greenery leads to increased heat absorption, raising summer temperatures to over 35 degrees Celsius, and even reaching 45.3 degrees in the past. Australia has one of the highest rates of skin cancer globally. Shade, provided by natural elements or structures, is essential for comfort, UV protection and encouraging social activity. When combined with water features, permeable paving and appropriate materials shade can effectively reduce heat

gain. Together, the provision of shade balanced with solar access strengthens healthy, vibrant, attractive communities.

Image - Umbrellas and trees provide shading of playground at North Rosebery Park, Rosebery, NSW (Source: Architectus)

#### Objectives

O1. To ensure for refuge from hot summer sun in

public and publicly accessible open space.

O2. To create imaginative responses for shade

that integrate with playgrounds, water

features and areas for the community to

congregate in.

O3. To ensure for high amenity public and private

spaces that are pleasurable to use.

O4. To reduce the contribution of new

development to the urban heat island effect

by retaining and providing design elements

that store water, minimising impermeable

surfaces to increase rainwater infiltration

and allow for the improved cooling effects

of evaporation.

O5. To ensure the location of tree planting and

vegetation has considered the location

of where people sit and children play to

provide shade and reduce radiant heat.

O6. To improve user comfort in the public

domain and space accessible to the public.

> [Picture on page 65, not reproduced as text; see the original PDF.]

#### Principles

P1. Create shade linkages through implementing shade structures and awnings to shop fronts and planting trees.

P2. Integrate water features such as misting, sprays and water fountains and the like where shade is provided in public and publicly accessible open space.

P3. Consider the location of trees and structures and the resultant shadow that will be created. Locate seating and areas where people can rest in these shaded areas.

P4. Maximise greenery throughout the Precinct to reduce urban heat.

P5. Shade paved surfaces and walls where possible but also consider solar access in winter months.
