### 2.1 Structure Plan

The Structure Plan provides an overview of the intended outcomes for the Punchbowl and Wiley Park Precinct. The Structure Plan has been informed by analysis of the opportunities and constraints of the Village Centres.  These opportunities include desired land uses, built form character, housing types, open space, streetscape improvements and pedestrian and cycling connections.

Key elements of the Structure Plan consist of: ⚬ Revitalised and vibrant Village Centres with active high streets.

⚬ New and enhanced parks and plazas. ⚬ New social infrastructure that supports a growing community.

⚬ Green connections, shared lanes and pedestrian links that support walking, cycling and social interaction. ⚬ Increased tree canopy and green cover to create cool, green and attractive neighbourhoods and to reduce urban heat gain.

⚬ High-quality built form outcomes that reflect character of place and protect sensitive interfaces.

⚬ The Introduction of creative industries in employment areas.

### 2.2	 Character Areas

The Punchbowl and Wiley Park Precinct will be comprised of different areas of distinct character. Through detailed analysis, seven character areas have been identified. Applicants are to identify which character area that a development site is located in and be cognisant of the key attributes of the existing context, where relevant, and the desired future context for the character area.

#### Objectives

O1. To achieve the desired future character of the Punchbowl and Wiley Park Precinct and guide development to deliver this desired character.

#### Controls

C1. Development must consider and address the outcomes expressed in the character area statements and principles.

C2. Development Applications must be accompanied by a site and context analysis plan that demonstrates an understanding of the manner a site integrates and is consistent with the desired future character area.

#### 2.2.6 Canterbury Road

#### The Canterbury Road character

area will continue to be an

#### important vehicular connection

#### as well as providing employment

#### opportunities for the local area.

#### Commercial and retail development

#### is prioritised along the corridor

#### with some mixed use development

#### concentrated at key intersections

#### including Broadway and Duncan

#### Streets, Punchbowl Road, Rosemont

#### Street and Victoria Road.

The corridor will support mixed use and commercial infill opportunities ranging from 4 to 8 storeys. A minimum 5m ground floor to ceiling height will support large format commercial uses including showroom retail, bulky goods, as well as business uses located below residential apartments. A setback from Canterbury Road at ground level will enable opportunities for streetscape improvements including widened footpaths, verges and landscaping. Similarly rear setbacks and setbacks on side streets will enable increased opportunities for deep soil and greening to soften the corridor and improve pedestrian amenity. Within mixed use development, residential uses are to be located above the ground floor, well separated and oriented away from the busy roads to provide for the best amenity outcomes. The corridor will link back to Punchbowl and Wiley Park Village character areas via enhanced connections along Broadway, Rossmore Avenue and King Georges Road.

### 2.3	 Connecting with Country

#### Objectives

O1. To recognise, respect and respond to Aboriginal cultural heritage in the planning, development and daily life of Punchbowl and Wiley Park Precinct.

#### Principles

P1. To take a Country centred approach and thinking that is layered into new development in the Precinct and is consistent with the Government Architect NSW ‘Connecting with Country framework’ (CWCF).

P2. To integrate Aboriginal cultural practices, history, narratives, and values in the design of proposed development.

P3. To encourage development to respond to the physical and cultural connection of the local Aboriginal community to the land.

P4. Foster collaboration and knowledge sharing between Aboriginal and non-Aboriginal individuals.

#### Controls

> [Picture on page 33, not reproduced as text; see the original PDF.]

C1. For development where clause 6.15 of the Canterbury-Bankstown Local Environmental Plan 2023 applies, the development must demonstrate:

a) How the design considers aspects of Country under Sections 3 and 4 of the Government Architect NSW CWCF;

b) How the design gives regard to the culture and heritage of the traditional custodians of the land, the Darug People, on which the development is proposed, including;

c) Connecting design with the existing site topography, natural environment and landscape setting; and

d) Incorporating sustainable practices by using natural building materials.

C2. For development where Design Excellence clause of the Canterbury-Bankstown Local Environmental Plan 2023 applies, the development must determine the appropriate scope of engagement with the Aboriginal community and its representatives, whether it be high, medium or low as outlined in Section 3.1 of the Government Architect NSW CWCF. If engaging with the Aboriginal community, development must:

a) Demonstrate the methods and outcomes from engagement with the Aboriginal community; and

b) Adhere to protocols outlined in the NSW Government’s Practice Note ‘Engaging with Aboriginal Communities’ and ‘Aboriginal Cultural and Intellectual Property Protocol’.

C3. If significant items such as ancestral remains or artefacts are discovered during subsoil investigations, excavation, demolition or other earthworks, the applicant must engage with the Aboriginal community to determine culturally appropriate handling, repatriation or reburial.

C4. Development that proposes open space and landscaping should demonstrate consideration of ‘walking Country’ which reflects Aboriginal cultural practices, artefacts, artwork and narratives. Aspects involving the senses of seeing, hearing, touching, smelling and tasting, should be included.

C5. Design aspects of each development

should give regard to the culture and

heritage of the traditional custodians of

d) Incorporate Aboriginal place names, as
