### 3.1 Public domain strategy

The public domain consists of streets, squares, parks, shared lanes and pedestrian links. These elements, in conjunction with the built form interface on private land, play a vital role in creating unique, vibrant and sustainable places that are attractive, inviting and inclusive for all. The public domain strategy ensures housing growth is supported by the delivery of high-quality public spaces that encourage walking, cycling, community interaction, and engagement. This section outlines key opportunities for enhancing the public domain in the Punchbowl and Wiley Park Precinct, as identified in Figure 21. Public Domain Strategy.

#### Objectives

O1. To improve the quality, appearance and function of the public domain.

O2. Provide a diverse range of quality public spaces to support residents, workers and visitors.

O3. Ensure a high level of permeability and accessibility throughout Precinct.

O4. Provide high quality, functional and attractive formal and informal spaces for community interaction.

O5. To establish a vibrant and attractive Precinct where the network of streets, shared lanes and open spaces support the increase in population density, the increased pedestrian movement and footpath trading activities.

### 3.3	 Streets, shared lanes,

### pedestrian links and arcades

Streets, shared lanes and pedestrian links are places of movement : to watch, to pass through and see the movement of people. This public framework provides for social and commercial exchange. In urban areas, a well-designed network of shared lanes and pedestrian links are essential for improving permeability and connecting to main streets and open space, creating walkability and equity of access and enjoyment. The street should be safe and inviting to pedestrians and cyclists while accommodating vehicles and traffic and be enhanced by tree canopies that promote amenity and sustainability. Pedestrian links prioritise pedestrians and cyclists, featuring open skies and awnings, while shared lanes also accommodate service vehicles and driveway access. Arcades serve as internal retail 'lanes' that enhance permeability and provide an engaging shopping experience. These narrow, intimate spaces encourage pedestrians to come and stay and facilitate connections through street blocks, reducing walking distances. New development is essential for contributing to a high-quality, permeable, and connected movement network that is green, inviting and safe for everyone. This is achieved by thoughtfully designing how a development interfaces with streets, lanes and pedestrian links. Setbacks, public domain improvements and new connections

h)	 Where a development has the potential to create a shared lane or pedestrian link by extending an existing or proposed lane within its site, the lane should be completed or extended within the development site. ### 3.4	 Tree canopy, green cover

### and deep soil

Trees are essential for their contribution to the amenity and character of the Precinct. When appropriately selected, located, planted and maintained, trees provide a multitude of benefits to the urban environment including reducing heat gain, providing shade and mitigating climate change.

One of the most effective solutions to mitigate urban heat gain is expanding tree canopy cover. Trees provide shade, cool the environment through transpiration and reduce surface temperature in densely built areas, where heat can become trapped by concrete and asphalt. Temperature differences between locations with canopy and without canopy gaps can be significant. Greencover provides further greening and shade to buildings and to the site itself. This will protect buildings from radiant heat gain and provide a pleasing aesthetic to the public domain. Deep soil is 'landscaped area'. These areas of soil are not covered by buildings or structures. Deep soil provides for the planting of canopy trees, increases water filtration and reduces stormwater runoff. It is beneficial to co-locate deep soil with communal open space to provide shade and amenity for those using these spaces.

### 3.6	 Shade and the reduction

### of urban heat

Punchbowl and Wiley Park face a significant urban heat island effect due to climate change and low tree canopy cover. The combination of roads, infrastructure and buildings with limited greenery leads to increased heat absorption, raising summer temperatures to over 30.5 degrees Celsius, and even reaching 45.9 degrees in the past. Australia has one of the highest rates of skin cancer globally. Shade, provided by natural elements or structures, is essential for comfort, UV protection and to encouraging social activity. When combined with water features, permeable paving and appropriate materials, shade can effectively reduce heat

gain. Together, shade and seating while balancing solar access foster healthy, vibrant, attractive communities.

#### Objectives

O1. To ensure for refuge from hot summer sun in

public and publicly accessible open space.

O2. To create imaginative responses for shade

that integrate with playgrounds, water

features and areas for the community to

congregate in.

O3. To ensure for high amenity public and private

spaces that are pleasurable to use.

O4. To reduce the contribution of new

development to the urban heat island effect

by retaining and providing design elements

that store water, minimising impermeable

surfaces to increase rainwater infiltration

and allow for the improved cooling effects

of evaporation.

O5. To ensure the location of tree planting and

vegetation has considered the location

of where people sit and children play to

provide shade and reduce radiant heat.

O6. To improve user comfort in public domain

and space accessible to the public.

#### Principles

P1. Create shade linkages through implementing

shade structures, awnings to shop fronts and

planting trees.

P2. Integrate water features such as misting,

sprays and water fountains and the like

where shade is provided in public and

publicly accessible open space.

P3. Consider the location of trees and structures

and the resultant shadow that will be

created. Locate seating and areas where

people can rest in these shaded areas.

P4. Maximise greenery throughout the Precinct

to reduce urban heat.

P5. Shade paved surfaces and walls where

possible but also consider solar access in

winter months.

#### Controls

C1. Retain existing trees to maintain shade. C2. Use deciduous trees in public open spaces to provide shade in summer and sun in winter.

C3. Improve cooling by locating trees to provide shade to exposed building walls, hard surfaces and pedestrian walkways.

C4. Use trees and shade structures in combination with water features to reduce temperatures in public open space and publicly accessible spaces.

C5. Store water in the landscape using fountains and water play features and the like.

C6. If a dark roof is used, green cover must cover the roof form. Consider reflectivity in the choice of roof material and the colour.

C7. Minimise the reflection of solar heat from facades by selecting appropriate materials.

C8. Ensure that play equipment is shaded to increase useability.
