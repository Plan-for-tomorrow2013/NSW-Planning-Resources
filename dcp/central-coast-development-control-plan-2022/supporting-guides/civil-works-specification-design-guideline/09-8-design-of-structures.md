## Section 8 -  Design of Structures

### 8.1 General

The design of structures such as bridges, large drainage structures and retaining walls shall be carried out in accordance with the current relevant Austroads publications, Australian Standards and Australian Rainfall and Runoff: A Guide to Flood Estimation.

Certification will be required from a Civil or Structural Engineer stating that the design has been prepared in accordance with the required guidelines, standards and Work Health and Safety legislation. Structural designs shall be accompanied by a Safety in Design Report that specifies the hazards relating to the design of the structure.

Design certification shall also be provided at construction stage to address the adequacy of the proposed formwork and construction methodology. Refer to Council’s Civil Works Specification - Construction Specification for requirements.

Inspection certification from a certified practising Civil or Structural Engineer will be required confirming that construction has been carried out in accordance with the approved design. Refer to Council’s Civil Works Specification - Construction Specification for requirements.

The design and manufacture of all precast structures shall be certified by a certified practising Civil or Structural Engineer as being in accordance with relevant standards. Refer to Council’s Civil Works Specification - Construction Specification for requirements. Prior to approval for use of precast products all relevant technical information for installation shall be submitted together with the certification to Council’s Representative.

### 8.2 Retaining Wall Design Requirements

Aesthetic and durability considerations shall be an important factor in the design of retaining walls, especially in road cutting situations. Natural stone products are the preferred material for use throughout the Central Coast region, due to the local source of these products and the desirable appearance of retaining walls constructed using these materials.

Retaining wall structures include (but are not limited to) the following:

• Retaining walls.

• Mass gravity (durable natural stone, brick and mortar).

• Gabion baskets.

• Stacked masonry blocks.

• Post and panel/waler (composite).

Retaining wall construction materials shall be either of the following (treated pine is not permitted unless designed to achieve the required design life and is approved by Council’s Representative):

• First grade quarry sawn sandstone logs (preferred).

• Masonry units.

• Precast reinforced coloured concrete panels/walers with galvanised steel universal beams.

• Some other components associated with an approved engineered retaining system.

• May need to consider any applicable heritage value for existing retaining walls.

A retaining wall design by a Qualified Civil or Structural Engineer is not required if the retaining wall height is less than 1m and is not:

• Supporting a batter or natural ground steeper than four horizontal to one vertical (4:1); and/or

• Subject to live loads.

In addition to the requirements listed in Section 5.6, the retaining wall design drawings shall also include (but are not limited to) the following:

• Component type.

• Component size.

• Buried components.

• Subsurface drainage and flush points.

• Location of existing and proposed public utility services in vicinity of retaining wall.

• MGA co-ordinates at each end of each site.

• Plan showing: o Horizontal alignment at toe. o Horizontal alignment at top. o Horizontal alignment of buried components; rock bolts, tie backs, etc.

• Longitudinal section.

• Typical cross section showing: o Footings. o Chainage. o Zone of influence for loading at back of retaining structure. o Zone of influence for excavation at toe of structure.

With reference to the above requirements, retaining walls designed by a Qualified Civil or Structural Engineer shall require the preparation of a Safety in Design Report, in accordance with the Work Health and Safety Regulation 2011, which should include the following information as a minimum:

• Purpose for which the item is designed.

• Hazards and risks to health and safety associated with the particular design for the whole life cycle: o Construction. o Operation. o Maintenance. o Demolition.

• Controls used to mitigate risks.

• Results of calculations, analysis, testing and examination.

• Hazardous properties of any substance.

• Conditions necessary to ensure safe use for the purpose it was designed or when carrying out associated activities.

• Residual risks.

The minimum design life for retaining walls associated with road cutting and embankment formations shall be 100 years.
